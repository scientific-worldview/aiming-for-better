### Starting Over

Once we’ve cleared the slate of cultural assumptions, personal [beliefs]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#beliefs), and institutional influences, a crucial question arises:

_Where do we begin?_{: .mark}

How can we start building a **reliable framework** for *understanding the world*—one that doesn’t just feel right, but actually works?

![First-Principles]({{ "/assets/images/guide/first-principles/principles.webp" | relative_url }})

This website proposes starting with [first principles]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#first-principles)—foundational ideas that are [rational]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#rational), practical, and self-evident to anyone adopting a truly open, critical, and [evidence-based]({{ "/guide/first-principles/evidence/" | relative_url }}) mindset. They serve as our intellectual compass, guiding how we appraise ideas, claims, and beliefs going forward.

Each of these principles is introduced briefly below.

---

### 🔍 Testability: The First Filter

This first principle is simple but powerful: *if an idea cannot be tested, it cannot be trusted*.
Claims that are untestable or unfalsifiable—no matter how widely believed or how objectively stated—are **excluded from rational inquiry**.

> *They lie outside the reach of evidence and cannot be meaningfully confirmed or disproved*.

---

### ✅ There ARE Correct Answers to Objective Questions

Despite philosophical puzzles like the [Problem of Induction]({{ "/articles/philosophy/induction/" | relative_url }}) we take this practical stance:

When **reliable evidence** overwhelmingly supports a claim about an objective phenomenon, a reasonable person can accept it as *a working fact*.

Not all knowledge needs to be absolute—but some answers are demonstrably better, and closer to the truth, than others.

---

### 🌐 Objective vs. Subjective vs. Intersubjective

This principle stresses the importance of knowing *what kind of question you're dealing with*:

- Objective questions can be [tested]({{ "/guide/first-principles/testability/" | relative_url }}) and **verified**.

- Subjective matters are personal, emotional, or aesthetic.

- [Intersubjective]({{ "/guide/first-principles/intersubjective/" | relative_url }}) beliefs are shared across a group or [culture]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#culture) but may not reflect objective truth.

[Correct answers]({{ "/guide/first-principles/correct-answers/" | relative_url }}) can only exist for objective questions. For everything else, the goal shifts from truth-seeking to understanding context.

---

### 📚 Evidence

In the pursuit of truth, _only evidence counts_{: .bold}.

A claim isn’t valid simply because it’s [traditional]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#tradition), *widely believed*, or [endorsed by authority]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#authority).

Evidence must be relevant, observable, and **independent** of the person making the claim. Without it, no belief—*no matter how sincere*—can be said to reflect reality.

---

### 🔬 Reliability & The Scientific Method

Not all evidence is equal. Humans are prone to error, [bias]({{ "/guide/thinking-for-yourself/cognitive-biases/" | relative_url }}), and [manipulation]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#manipulation). That’s why evidence must be:

- _Repeatable_{: .bullet}

- _Transparent_{: .bullet}

- _Open to scrutiny_{: .bullet}

Science offers a systematic way to filter out unreliable evidence and reduce human error: the **evidence-based**, [scientific method]({{ "/guide/first-principles/scientific-method/" | relative_url }}). It’s not perfect, but so far, it’s *the most successful tool* we have for discovering reliable answers about the world.

---

### 🚧 Why These Principles Matter

This set of principles isn’t about abstract philosophy or ivory-tower thinking. It’s about **creating a mindset** that can be applied—consistently and practically—to *evaluate the world around us*. Whether you're assessing a policy, a personal belief, or a scientific claim, these _first principles_{: .bold} help anchor your reasoning in clarity, rigour, and intellectual honesty.
