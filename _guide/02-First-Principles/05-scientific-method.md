### Overview

_We need a reliable BS-filter._{: .mark}

Throughout history, human beings have explained the world using myths, traditions, [authority]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#authority), and "common sense." The problem? *These are incredibly unreliable*. If you want to get **closer to the truth**—whether in medicine, policymaking, or your everyday life—you need a system designed to strip away human [bias]({{ "/guide/thinking-for-yourself/cognitive-biases/" | relative_url }}).

That system is the **Evidence-Based Scientific Method**. 

It is not just a tool for people in white lab coats; it is the single most powerful _way of thinking_{: .bold} ever devised for separating fact from fiction.

---

### How the Method Works

The [scientific method]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#scientific-method) doesn't rely on authority or [belief]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#beliefs). It relies on a rigorous, step-by-step process to generate and test [evidence]({{ "/guide/first-principles/evidence/" | relative_url }}):

1. **Observation:** Notice something and *ask a question*.
2. **Hypothesis:** Form a proposed explanation (*an educated guess*).
3. **Prediction:** Make a specific, testable *prediction* based on that [hypothesis]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#hypothesis).
4. **Experiment:** Gather real-world data to *test the prediction*.
5. **Analyze & Conclude:** If the evidence supports the [hypothesis]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#hypothesis), you *share the results*. If it doesn't, you throw out or modify the hypothesis and start over. 

You don't _start with a conclusion_{: .warn} and look for evidence to support it. You *follow the evidence wherever it leads*.

### The Method in Action

To understand why this method is so _revolutionary_{: .bold}, look at what happens when it collides with "established authority":

#### The Ulcer Myth
- _The Observation:_{: .bullet} The medical establishment was absolutely certain that stomach ulcers were caused by stress, alcohol and/or spicy food. 
- _The Hypothesis:_{: .bullet} In the 1980s, Barry Marshall and Robin Warren proposed that a bacterium (*H. pylori*) caused ulcers, despite the "obvious" [consensus]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#consensus) that bacteria couldn't survive stomach acid.
- _The Experiment:_{: .bullet} To test this, Marshall literally drank a broth of *H. pylori*, gave himself severe gastritis, and then successfully cured himself with antibiotics.
- _The Conclusion:_{: .bullet} The evidence overturned decades of medical [dogma]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#dogma). Ulcers are now cured with antibiotics, and they won the Nobel Prize.

#### The Handwashing Outcast
- _The Observation:_{: .bullet} In the 1840s, Ignaz Semmelweis noticed women giving birth in hospitals died of "childbed fever" far more often than those giving birth at home.
- _The Hypothesis:_{: .bullet} He proposed doctors were spreading deadly "particles" from autopsy rooms to the maternity wards.
- _The Experiment:_{: .bullet} He ordered doctors to wash their hands with chlorine. Death rates instantly plummeted from 18% to below 2%.
- _The Conclusion:_{: .bullet} Despite the undeniable [evidence]({{ "/guide/first-principles/evidence/" | relative_url }}), the medical establishment was deeply insulted by the idea that *they* were causing the deaths. They _ignored_{: .warn} his findings, and Semmelweis died an outcast in an asylum. It took decades for Germ [Theory]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#theory) to finally prove him right.

### Why This Method Makes Evidence Reliable

As the Semmelweis tragedy shows, finding the truth isn't enough—the *quality and reliability* of your evidence matters. 

![reliability]({{ "/assets/images/guide/first-principles/reliability.webp" | relative_url }})

The scientific method is specifically designed to produce **reliable evidence** through a strict set of rules:

- _Testability_{: .bullet} – Can the claim be [tested]({{ "/guide/first-principles/testability/" | relative_url }})? Can it be *proven wrong* if false?
- _Reproducibility / Repeatable Method_{: .bullet} – Do *repeated tests* by different people give the exact same result?
- _Transparency_{: .bullet} – Is the method *open to public scrutiny*?
- _Peer review_{: .bullet} – Has it been heavily *critiqued* by independent experts?
- _Absence of Bias_{: .bullet} – Is it completely free from [cherry-picking]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#cherry-picking) or [_emotional manipulation_{: .warn}]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#manipulation)?

Reliable evidence doesn’t mean “perfect.” It means it was collected through fair, systematic methods, it clearly separates **what we know** from *what we think we know*, and it remains completely open to correction if new [facts]({{ "/guide/first-principles/correct-answers/" | relative_url }}) emerge.

### When the Method is Ignored

Unfortunately, **reliable evidence** is often *ignored*, *misrepresented*, or completely drowned out by voices with [_an agenda_{: .warn}]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#agendas). 

We’ve seen this play out again and again:
- Tobacco companies **paid** to *downplay the risks of smoking*.
- Political leaders **ignored** *scientific advice* during pandemics.
- Climate change warnings were **delayed** by fossil fuel [lobbying]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#lobbying).
- Cannabis was reclassified in the UK *directly against scientific advice*.
- Life-saving vaccines are constantly **undermined** by *fraud* and [moral panics]({{ "/case-studies/politics/dysfunction/moral-panics/" | relative_url }}).

> **Why does this happen?** Because policies are frequently shaped *not by facts*, but by [fear]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#fear), public image, self-interest, or _ideology_{: .warn}. 

Institutions will actively *suppress* [uncomfortable truths]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#uncomfortable-truths) if those truths cost them votes, money, or influence. Furthermore, many political decision-makers are trained purely in [_the humanities_{: .bold}]({{ "/articles/science/two-cultures/" | relative_url }}) and have very *little understanding of science*—a dangerous disconnect famously known as [→ The Two Cultures]({{ "/articles/science/two-cultures/" | relative_url }}).

The consequences? Preventable deaths, *misinformed* [laws]({{ "/case-studies/law/theory/" | relative_url }}), environmental damage, and a public left completely confused about who to trust. 

---

## Final Thought

If we want a **better world** — with smarter decisions, highly effective policies, and fewer preventable disasters — we must commit to this fundamental way of thinking.

_Go with the evidence, not the noise_{: .subtle}

Challenge authority, demand transparency, and always ensure that the evidence you base your worldview on was built using a reliable, scientific method.
