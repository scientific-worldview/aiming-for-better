### Overview

Cruelty is a word that conjures visceral images—abuse, violence, [exploitation]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#exploitation). Its opposite, [altruism]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#altruism), evokes compassion, [cooperation]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#cooperation), and [self-sacrifice]({{ "/guide/human-history/self-sacrifice/" | relative_url }}). These ideas run deep in human experience. But in modern discourse, they’re often treated as *moral absolutes*, loaded with outrage or praise but rarely explored with scientific clarity.

![Cruelty]({{ "/assets/images/guide/human-history/cruelty.webp" | | relative_url }})

This page asks:

What do altruism and cruelty really mean in [human history]({{ "/guide/human-history/" | relative_url }})? How did they evolve? And how can we move beyond vague accusations and _build systems that reduce harm?_{: .mark}

---

### What Nature Tells Us

Across our _evolutionary history_{: .bold}, both altruism and cruelty had [survival implications]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#survival-implications).

- Altruism helped [groups]({{ "/case-studies/society/social-structures/the-clan/" | relative_url }}) thrive. Mutual aid, [empathy]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#empathy), and fairness **increased chances of group [survival]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#survival).  
- Cruelty—like domination or punishment—sometimes served **short-term enforcement or control**, especially in [parent]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#parental-ownership)–child dynamics or between competing individuals.

But [evolution]({{ "/guide/human-history/evolution/" | relative_url }}) doesn’t prescribe *"what should be"*. It just explains *what was selected for*. Some traits—like altruism—offered _long-term group benefits_{: .bold}. Others—like [coercion]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#coercion) or aggression—may have been *tolerated in the short term*, especially when they didn’t endanger the group.

_What matters is what the group allowed, not what one individual tried to do._{: .subtle}

In _tightly knit_{: .cool} hunter-gatherer groups, cruelty had *limited room to flourish*. There were _too many eyes watching_{: .bold}. Problems were handled collectively, and individuals—*especially [children]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#role-of-children)—weren’t _isolated_{: .warn} [behind closed doors]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#behind-closed-doors).

---

### The Agricultural Transition: From Shared Eyes to "Behind Closed Doors"

The rise of agriculture and [civilisation]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#civilisations) brought many changes:

- [Families]({{ "/case-studies/society/normal-living/family/" | relative_url }}) gradually, eventually became *private, nuclear, enclosed unit*, not _open extended groups_{: .bold}  
- Overseeing of children shifted from the group to legal _ownership_{: .warn} by individual parents (who might be loving or otherwise) 
- Institutions (or the lack thereof) left vulnerable people invisible for years  
- [Mistreatment]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#mistreatment) was **hidden**, **normalised**, or **redefined as discipline**

What once would have been _visible_{: .bold}—and _stopped_{: .warn}—now became *personal, private, and beyond public scrutiny*.

_Cruelty didn’t increase because [human nature]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#human-nature) changed._{: .subtle} It became *harder to see*, *harder to challenge*, and **easier to justify**.

### Abuse: A Vague Word for Real Harm

Modern language often focuses on _"abuse"_{: .warn}—a word *emotionally charged*, yet very **poorly defined** scientifically.

What counts as abuse?

- A parent hitting a child? (Now illegal in several countries)  
- Telling children _untestable [religious]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#religion) stories_{: .bold} as truth?  
- Withholding affection?  
- Isolating elderly relatives in Care Homes?  
- Threatening partners emotionally without laying a hand?

We agree real harm happens. But the word “abuse” now acts as a [placeholder]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#placeholder) for all the things we find *objectionable*—_without offering a clear path to understanding or prevention_{: .mark}.

> “Abuse” is [intersubjective]({{ "/guide/first-principles/intersubjective/" | relative_url }})—it exists in *how people label and respond to acts*, not just **in the acts themselves**.

**See Also:** [Neglect and Abuse]({{ "/case-studies/society/normal-living/neglect-and-abuse/" | relative_url }})

### Legal Atrocities

History is full of *legally sanctioned cruelty*:

- Children in the UK **forcibly relocated** to Australia as “orphans” and subjected to *neglect and violence*  
- _Unmarried mothers_{: .bullet} in institutions had their children removed, often permanently  
- Corporal punishment in homes and schools was not just allowed—*it was expected*
- Elderly or disabled individuals were *warehoused in impersonal institutions*, often ignored, overmedicated, or treated without dignity

None of these were technically *“abuse”* at the time. They were **"[normal]({{ "/guide/human-history/natural-vs-normal/" | relative_url }})"**.

Which forces us to ask:

_How much cruelty today is being overlooked simply because it's legal, "normal", or behind closed doors?_{: .subtle}

### The Cruelty of Normal: Spotlight on the Nuclear Family

We are told the nuclear family is the safest place for children. But is it?

- It is where *most harm to children actually occurs*—not on the street, not by "strangers", but at home  
- It is *where women, children, and elderly are most vulnerable*, often unable to leave  
- _Economic Dependence_{: .bullet} _ties_{: .warn} individuals to homes that may not be safe  
- Ideas like *“respect your parents”* or [obey]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#obedience) your elders”* make it hard to question [authority]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#authority)  
- Loyalty to one [narrative]({{ "/case-studies/narratives/" | relative_url }})—*one version of "the truth*"—can **force silence**, even in cases of clear mistreatment

![Cruelty]({{ "/assets/images/guide/human-history/cruel.webp" | | relative_url }})

Even when no physical harm is present, nuclear families can produce:

- [Fear]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#fear), coercion, [manipulation]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#manipulation), guilt, control  
- _Pressure to conform_{: .bullet} to a family’s [beliefs]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#beliefs)—religious, political, or social  
- Lack of _emotional support_{: .bold} or outlets  
- _Isolation_{: .bullet} from *a broader support group*

_Hunter-gatherer children were part of a group._{: .subtle} They could seek _safety_{: .bold}, [mentorship]({{ "/guide/human-history/mentorship/" | relative_url }}), or _care_{: .bold} *from many adults or peers*—**not just their parents**.

Today, a child’s entire reality often depends on one or two adults, *behind a closed door*. And if one of those adults is harmful, _there is nowhere to go_{: .mark}.

### Scapegoats and Distractions

When harm does surface, modern societies tend to:

- Focus on **individual pathology** (“he was a monster”, “she was mentally ill”)  
- Label with finality: “paedophile”, “abuser”, “evil”  
- Ignore the [enabling system]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#enabling-system) that let the harm occur for years

But most sexual mistreatment is not carried out by mythical predators—it’s **committed by relatives**. And most relatives are not “paedophiles” by _scientific definition_{: .bold}—they are most often **heterosexual adults** who *harm those within reach*, behind closed doors, often because no one is watching.

We must confront the fact:

_It’s the system—not just the individual—that needs scrutiny._{: .subtle}

### Altruism: Nature’s Design for Kindness

Despite all this, humans are also _deeply cooperative creatures_{: .bold}. We evolved to:

- Help others without direct reward  
- Share resources with the vulnerable  
- Punish free-riders who harmed the group  
- Feel pain when others suffer  
- Celebrate generosity, fairness, and kindness

Children _naturally display altruism_{: .bold} by _helping peers, comforting others, and resisting unfairness_{: .mark}. Altruism is not taught—[it’s innate]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#innate).

![Altruism]({{ "/assets/images/guide/human-history/altruism.webp" | | relative_url }})

But like cruelty, altruism is _system-sensitive_{: .bold}. It thrives when:

- People are not overworked or isolated 
- Vulnerable individuals are not dependent on one or two gatekeepers  
- Group members feel safe to intervene, support, or protect

---

### Designing for Kindness, Not Just Punishing Cruelty

If we want less cruelty, we need *more than* [outrage]({{ "/case-studies/politics/political-dynamics/moral-outrage/" | relative_url }}) and [punishment]({{ "/case-studies/locked-in/crime-control/" | relative_url }}). We need:

- Clear _definitions of harm_{: .bullet}, grounded in [evidence]({{ "/guide/first-principles/evidence/" | relative_url }}), *not emotion*
- **Open** systems where vulnerable people *aren’t isolated*  
- [Shared caregiving]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#alloparenting) models, not [privatised parenting]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#intensive-parenting) **behind closed doors**  
- [Education]({{ "/case-studies/society/social-structures/education/" | relative_url }}) systems that teach empathy and communication  
- Legal systems that _prevent harm before it happens_{: .bold}, not just reacts after  
- [Mental health]({{ "/case-studies/health/mental-health/" | relative_url }}) support that *asks what happened to people*, not just "what’s wrong with them"

_Altruism cannot be outsourced to [slogans]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#slogans)._{: .subtle} It must be **designed** into our systems—made easier, more visible, and more rewarding.

---

### From Narrative to Evidence

We don’t deny cruelty exists. We don’t deny that some individuals do great harm.

But outrage without clarity just fuels more division, [blame]({{ "/case-studies/politics/dysfunction/blame/" | relative_url }}), and confusion.

Let’s move from *vague moral labels* to _clearer, evidence-based understanding_{: .bold}:

- What _reduces harm_{: .bold}?  
- What _enables kindness_{: .bold}?  
- What kind of *social structures* _protect the vulnerable_{: .bold} *before* harm occurs?

That means being *willing to question* what we call **"normal"**, even when it’s [sacred]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#sacred)—like the family, the home, or the way we raise children.

---

## Looking Forward

We may never eliminate cruelty. But we can reduce it—dramatically—if we:

- Design systems around _human nature_{: .bold}, not just _social convention_{: .bold}  
- Re-embrace _the extended group_{: .bold}, shared caregiving, and **public accountability**  
- Make kindness and care *visible, natural, and expected*—not heroic exceptions  
- Focus not on _demonising_{: .warn} individuals (*cruelty*), but _redesigning the environments_{: .bold} (*altruism*) in which people live and grow

_Let’s build societies that make cruelty harder—and altruism easier._{: .subtle}
