### Overview

From _lobotomies_{: .bold} to [mindfulness apps]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#mindfulness), the modern world has seen a dramatic rise in attempts to *manage*, *correct*, or *"heal"* human minds. Therapy, once confined to extreme cases, now shapes everything from [education]({{ "/case-studies/society/social-structures/education/" | relative_url }}) to [justice]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#justice-and-injustice) to everyday conversation.

But are these interventions always **evidence-based**? Or are they sometimes tools of [conformity]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#conformity), *industry*, and *ideology*?

![Therapy]({{ "/assets/images/guide/human-history/therapies.webp" | | relative_url }})

A [clean slate]({{ "/guide/clean-slate/" | relative_url }}) approach requires that we step back—*acknowledge* our _evolutionary heritage_{: .bold}, *examine* our modern systems, and ask what really [_serves human well-being_{: .bold}]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#wellbeing).

### Human Minds in Unnatural Systems

Our [brains]({{ "/case-studies/who-we-are/brain/" | relative_url }}) [evolved]({{ "/guide/human-history/evolution/" | relative_url }}) for small-group living, cooperative [survival]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#survival), and instinct-driven [behaviour]({{ "/guide/human-history/psychology/" | relative_url }}). But modern life—crowded cities, abstract jobs, rigid institutions—often generates [mismatches]({{ "/case-studies/mismatches/" | relative_url }}) *between* our [biology]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#biology) and our environment.

Many so-called [disorders]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#disorder) may simply reflect _adaptive responses to unnatural conditions_{: .mark}.

- _Aggression_{: .bullet} in a *deprived environment*
- _Anxiety_{: .bullet} in *unpredictable systems*
- _Withdrawal_{: .bullet} in *overstimulating societies*

Instead of _pathologising_{: .warn} the individual, we must _examine the system_{: .bold}.

### Therapy as a Mirror of Cultural Norms

What counts as _“healthy”_{: .bold} or [deviant]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#deviant) often reflects the [values]({{ "/case-studies/society/sociology/values/" | relative_url }}) of the dominant [culture]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#culture).

Historical and contemporary examples include:

- _Lobotomies and Electroshock Therapy_{: .bullet}: Silencing inconvenient emotions  
- _Conversion Therapy_{: .bullet}: Enforcing moral or [religious]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#religion) conformity  
- _Behaviour Modification in Schools and Prisons_{: .bullet}: Promoting [obedience]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#obedience) over understanding  
- _Re-education Camps_{: .bullet}: Turning _ideology_{: .warn} into _"treatment"_{: .bold}

Too often, therapy has been less about [human flourishing]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#human-flourishing)—and more about _reinforcing_{: .warn} what's considered "[normal]({{ "/guide/human-history/natural-vs-normal/" | relative_url }})" by those in [power]({{ "/case-studies/politics/governance/power/" | relative_url }}).

### Offender Rehabilitation: Good Intentions, Flawed Assumptions

In the justice system, many interventions claim to *“correct”* behaviour—but often rest on _questionable_{: .warn} [psychology]({{ "/guide/human-history/psychology/" | relative_url }}):

- _Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT) for Offenders:_{: .bullet} Assumes deviance stems from "faulty thoughts", with little attention to trauma, biology, or social context
- _Empathy Training:_{: .bullet} Assumes [empathy]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#empathy) is a learned universal trait, rather than an evolved, *variable* instinct  
- _Risk Assessments and Relapse Plans:_{: .bullet} Often lack robust evidence for long-term effectiveness

Despite *"good intentions"*, these assessments and interventions can be [pseudoscientific]({{ "/articles/science/pseudoscience/" | relative_url }}), *reductionist*, and *self-reinforcing*.

### Labels That Stick—and Systems That Grow Around Them

Once someone is _labelled_{: .warn}—*“offender”*, *“at-risk”*, *“mentally ill”*—the consequences can _snowball_{: .bold}:

- _Deviance Amplification_{: .bullet}: Labels can become [self-fulfilling prophecies]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#self-fulfilling-prophecies)  
- _Moral Panic_{: .bullet}: Public [fear]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#fear) *shapes policy more than evidence* (see [Moral Panics]({{ "/case-studies/politics/dysfunction/moral-panics/" | relative_url }}))
- _Status and Industry_{: .bullet}: An expanding **therapy industry** gives job titles, [authority]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#authority), and income to those enforcing _unproven systems_{: .bold}.

> The more problems we _medicalise_{: .warn}, the more **"professionals"** are paid to manage them.

### From Healing to Managing: The Rise of Everyday Therapy

As sociologist Frank Furedi argues, therapy has spilled out of the clinic and into culture. Its influence reshapes how we view:

- _Sadness or anger_{: .bullet} as *"medical conditions"*  
- _Resilience_{: .bullet} as *risky*, and _vulnerability_{: .bold} as *virtuous*  
- _Adulthood_{: .bullet} as a stage requiring supervision  
- _Feelings_{: .bullet} as *more valid than* [facts]({{ "/guide/first-principles/correct-answers/" | relative_url }})

This results in:

- _Pathologizing Normal Life_{: .bullet}: Sadness, struggle, or moral disagreement [framed]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#framing) as [symptoms]({{ "/case-studies/society/normal-living/treating-symptoms/" | relative_url }}) of disorder.
- _Cult of Vulnerability_{: .bullet}: Encourages *helplessness* and *emotional fragility*.
- _Infantilisation of Adults_{: .bullet}: Treating adults as emotionally immature and dependent.
- _Subjectivity Over Objectivity_{: .bullet}: Emotional reasoning replaces [rational]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#rational) argument.

This doesn’t mean therapy is inherently bad—but it should make us *pause and ask*: are we "treating" problems, or _creating them_{: .bold}?

---

### Medicalising the Social

The expansion of therapy has paralleled broader trends:

- _Deinstitutionalisation without support_{: .bullet}: Contributing to homelessness and incarceration  
- _The Therapeutic State_{: .bullet}: Using [compassion]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#altruism) as *a pretext for control*  
- _Market Incentives_{: .bullet}: Prioritising *treatments that sell* over _those that work_{: .bold}

When therapy becomes a commodity, the focus often shifts from [evidence]({{ "/guide/first-principles/evidence/" | relative_url }}) to *efficiency*—or even compliance.

---

### A Scientific Approach Would Ask:

- What counts as a _"disorder"_{: .warn}—and *who decides*?
- Are **"interventions"** [tested]({{ "/guide/first-principles/testability/" | relative_url }}), replicable, and *cross-culturally valid*?
- How do **unnatural conditions**—like [inequality]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#inequality) or *disconnection*—_affect behaviour_{: .bold}?
- Can support systems *prioritise* [autonomy]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#autonomy), understanding, and dignity?
- How do we prevent [ideological assumptions]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#ideology) from _masquerading as science_{: .bold}?

## Therapy Might Have Value—But It Must Be Grounded

Therapy has helped many people - simply because, when it's voluntary, talking and interacting is an age old "cure". Hence, it can offer support, possible insight, and healing. But when it becomes a **tool of conformity** or a *self-sustaining* _industry_{: .warn}, it risks doing *more harm than good*.

A _scientific and evidence-based approach_{: .mark} invites us to reimagine [mental health]({{ "/case-studies/health/mental-health/" | relative_url }})—not as a one-size-fits-all process of correction, but as a compassionate, [testable]({{ "/guide/first-principles/testability/" | relative_url }}), and human-centred practice.

The goal is not to fix people to fit the system.

> But to *examine the system itself*.
