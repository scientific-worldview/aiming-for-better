### Overview

When discussing anything about human beings—our [beliefs]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#beliefs), systems, and behaviours—*it’s vital not to ignore* _human history_{: .bold}. Yet all too often, it’s completely overlooked, even in conversations about [critical thinking]({{ "/guide/thinking-for-yourself/" | relative_url }}).

So before we tackle critical thinking, let's first think about our **long** [history]({{ "/guide/human-history/" | relative_url }}).

![Human-History]({{ "/assets/images/guide/human-history/human-history.webp" | | relative_url }})

Humans tend to live in the _“now”_{: .neon}, embedded in the [intersubjective]({{ "/guide/first-principles/intersubjective/" | relative_url }}) systems we call [culture]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#culture). These systems _shape_{: .mark} how we think, what we value, and what we believe is *“[normal]({{ "/guide/human-history/natural-vs-normal/" | relative_url }})”*. But culture can _trap us in a bubble_{: .warn}—making it hard to step back and see *the bigger picture*.

To truly understand [human nature]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#human-nature), we need to **zoom out**—far beyond *the narrow slice of time* we typically call “history”.

---

### The Real Timescale

The story of agriculture and [civilisation]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#civilisations)—which *dominates our idea of “history”*—is **just a blink** in _evolutionary time_{: .bold}. Modern humans ([Homo sapiens]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#modern-humans-homo-sapiens)) have existed for around **300,000 years**. If we include [earlier humans]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#early-humans) and close evolutionary cousins, the story stretches back **millions of years**. And yet, almost all of recorded history—writing, [laws]({{ "/case-studies/law/theory/" | relative_url }}), organized [religion]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#religion), money, politics—has happened *in just the last* **5,000 years**.

That’s less than **2%** of the time modern humans, *Homo sapiens*, have walked the Earth, and *less than* **0.1%** when considering the *entire human lineage*. Put another way: more than **99.9% of human history** was spent living in small, cooperative, mobile [groups]({{ "/case-studies/society/social-structures/the-clan/" | relative_url }})—without cities, without money, without [hierarchy]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#hierarchies).

![Human-History-Timescale]({{ "/assets/images/guide/human-history/timelines.webp" | | relative_url }})

The human [brain]({{ "/case-studies/who-we-are/brain/" | relative_url }}), body, and social instincts _evolved_{: .bold} *under those conditions*. What we now call “civilisation” is **radically new**, and we are still living with the consequences of its *very sudden arrival*.

---

### The Agricultural Transition

**The Agricultural Transition** refers to a *profound shift in human life*: from [egalitarian]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#egalitarian), group-based, hunter-gatherer societies to hierarchical, agricultural civilisations.

This change wasn’t just about farming. It brought *an entirely new way of living*—permanent settlements, food surplus, property, social roles, [power]({{ "/case-studies/politics/governance/power/" | relative_url }}) structures, gender [inequality]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#inequality), [slavery]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#slavery), formal religion, taxation, written laws, and the modern [nation-state]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#nation-state). In short: it moved us from what was [natural]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#natural) to what became defined as **"normal"**.

That [Agricultural Transition]({{ "/guide/human-history/agricultural-transition/" | relative_url }}), and Civilisations roughly 5,000 years later, came with both _benefits_{: .bold} and _heavy costs_{: .warn}.

#### What we gained

Many changes were beneficial, especially in the long term:

- _Food surplus_{: .bullet} → population growth, permanent settlements, urban centres  
- _Written language_{: .bullet} → *knowledge preservation and dissemination*  
- _Infrastructure_{: .bullet} → roads, sanitation, cities  
- _Specialisation_{: .bullet} → art, science, technology  
- _Medical advancements (eventually! )_{: .bullet} → systematic study of disease, public health

#### What we lost or distorted

- *Gender equality* and [social cooperation]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#cooperation)  
- Mobility, leisure, and dietary variety 
- *Connection to nature* and *kin-based networks*  
- Freedom from [coercion]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#coercion), war, and state violence  
- [Mental health]({{ "/case-studies/health/mental-health/" | relative_url }}) and [autonomy]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#autonomy)

Many harmful norms—_slavery_{: .warn}, the _subjugation_{: .warn} of women and older [children]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#role-of-children), criminalising [sexuality]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#sexuality), [obedience]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#obedience) to rulers or religious codes—**emerged** during or after this transition and became embedded in intersubjective [narratives]({{ "/case-studies/narratives/" | relative_url }}) that *continue to shape* [society]({{ "/case-studies/society/social-structures/" | relative_url }}) today.

Some of these consequences were likely inevitable given the nature of The Transition. But we must remember: just because something became **“normal”** doesn’t mean it was ever _natural_{: .bold}—or *right*.

### Natural vs. Normal

What’s _natural_{: .bold} for human beings, *in the evolutionary sense*, is often very different from what we’re told is **"normal"** by modern culture. A few examples we’ll explore:

- _Family structure_{: .bullet}  
  - Natural: cooperative [parenting]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#intensive-parenting), [alloparenting]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#alloparenting), large extended kin networks  
  - "Normal": [nuclear families]({{ "/case-studies/society/normal-living/family/" | relative_url }}), often isolated and overburdened

- _Mental health_{: .bullet}  
  - Natural: embedded in tribe, [status]({{ "/case-studies/society/sociology/status/" | relative_url }}) through contribution  
  - "Normal": isolated, performative identity, economic status

- _Education_{: .bullet}  
  - Natural: learning through imitation, play, integration and community  
  - "Normal": [standardised]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#standardisation), age-segregated schooling, abstract testing, obedience

- _Justice and law_{: .bullet}  
  - Natural: [social norms]({{ "/case-studies/society/sociology/norms/" | relative_url }}) enforced through [reciprocity]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#reciprocity) and [shame]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#shame)  
  - "Normal": centralised power, punishment, incarceration

- _Media and narrative_{: .bullet}  
  - Natural: oral storytelling and local gossip  
  - "Normal": [mass media]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#mass-media), [propaganda]({{ "/guide/human-history/propaganda/" | relative_url }}), social media algorithms, [misinformation]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#misinformation)

### What if we could wipe the slate clean?

What if we could temporarily set aside every inherited [worldview]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#worldview), religious or secular, and ask:

- What is _human nature_{: .bold}, and *what do we need to thrive*?  
- How should societies be structured, if we started from scratch?  
- Are our [laws]({{ "/case-studies/law/theory/" | relative_url }}) and [norms]({{ "/case-studies/society/sociology/norms/" | relative_url }}) based on [evidence]({{ "/guide/first-principles/evidence/" | relative_url }})—or outdated myths?  
- Could mental health and [education]({{ "/case-studies/society/social-structures/education/" | relative_url }}) be *rethought* through an **evolutionary lens**?  
- What [institutions]({{ "/case-studies/locked-in/institutional-control/" | relative_url }}) still reflect the ancient narratives that emerged after the Agricultural Transition?

These are the _human history_{: .bold} “[clean slate]({{ "/guide/clean-slate/" | relative_url }})” questions we’ll explore in the pages ahead.

### Moving Forward, Without Forgetting

In today’s world, especially in wealthy societies, many people *enjoy extraordinary benefits*:

Lower child mortality, longer lifespans, abundant entertainment, modern medicine, labour-saving technologies, and more.

We do not deny this—nor do we *romanticise the past*. **Very few would choose to return to pre-modern life**.

![Human-History]({{ "/assets/images/guide/human-history/human-evolution.webp" | | relative_url }})

But in some of the following pages, we're going to play [devil's advocate]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#devils-advocate) and stress-test some ideas of *"progress"*. After all, questioning with an [open mind]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#open-mind) is part of the [scientific method]({{ "/guide/first-principles/scientific-method/" | relative_url }}). The point of this project is not to go back—it's to *look forward* **with eyes wide open**.

We must **acknowledge** both the _gains_{: .bold} and the _costs_{: .warn} of the [Agricultural Transition]({{ "/guide/human-history/agricultural-transition/" | relative_url }}), and ask:

What **damaging norms** *still persist*? What **inherited systems** *no longer serve us*? Which **“normals”** are *worth challenging*—and _changing_{: .bold}?

The tools of science, evidence, and critical thinking now give us the **opportunity** to *revisit these legacies* with a clearer lens.

This project begins with that lens—so let's go back, as far as we can, starting with the _true story_{: .mark} of _evolution_{: .bold}.
