### Overview

For at least **99.9%** of our _evolutionary history_{: .bold}—spanning *millions of years* including [earlier humans]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#early-humans) and primate ancestors—we lived **without** governments, [laws]({{ "/case-studies/law/theory/" | relative_url }}), schools, organized [religion]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#religion), or money. We lived in [small, kin-based groups]({{ "/case-studies/society/social-structures/the-clan/" | relative_url }}), close to nature, deeply shaped by the pressures of [survival]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#survival) and social cohesion.

This was our [natural]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#natural) state.

![Transition]({{ "/assets/images/guide/human-history/transition.webp" | | relative_url }})

Then, in what amounts to a blink in [evolutionary]({{ "/guide/human-history/" | relative_url }}) time—roughly **10,000 years ago** (*agriculture*) and **5000 years ago** (*civilaisations*) — [everything changed]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#agricultural-transition).

---

### The Agricultural Shift

With the rise of agriculture came a radical transformation of human life:

- *Permanent* settlements  
- Food **surpluses** and population booms  
- Social [hierarchies]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#hierarchies) and [power]({{ "/case-studies/politics/governance/power/" | relative_url }}) structures**  
- *Specialised roles* and rigid **gender divisions**
- **Accumulated wealth** and **property ownership**  

Over time, these developments enabled complex societies. But they also introduced a new kind of existence—governed not by _evolved instincts_{: .bold}, but by _constructed_{: .warn} [norms]({{ "/case-studies/society/sociology/norms/" | relative_url }}). The result was a new version of “[normal]({{ "/guide/human-history/natural-vs-normal/" | relative_url }})” that often _conflicted_{: .warn} with what had been _natural_{: .bold}.

We transitioned from what helped us **survive in the wild** to what helped us [function in civilisation]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#civilisations).

---

### Culture: Powerful, Productive—and Problematic

This transition brought _undeniable benefits_{: .mark}:

- Agriculture sustained *larger populations*  
- Cities enabled [cooperation]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#cooperation) and innovation at scale  
- Writing and technology *preserved and transmitted knowledge*  
- Labour and economic systems led to *unprecedented growth*  

But it also brought _new problems_{: .mark}:

- Psychological _distress_{: .warn} from **status hierarchies** and [urban crowding]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#urbanisation)  
- [Suppression]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#suppression) of _natural behaviours_{: .bold} like play, [sexuality]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#sexuality), and exploration  
- _Injustices_{: .warn} like [slavery]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#slavery), ownership of women and [children]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#role-of-children), [patriarchy]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#patriarch-and-patrilineal), and **class division**—often made [sacred]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#sacred) by [myth]({{ "/case-studies/narratives/" | relative_url }}) or [religion]({{ "/guide/human-history/religion/" | relative_url }})  
- _Alienation_{: .warn} from nature, from kinship bonds, and even from ourselves  

These weren’t part of our evolutionary "design". They were *by-products* of **new systems**. Yet over time, they came to be *accepted as* **“normal”**—even “necessary.”

### What If the Agricultural Transition Had Been Evidence-Based?

Imagine if humans had approached this transition scientifically:

- What if we had [tested]({{ "/guide/first-principles/testability/" | relative_url }}) new systems before [locking them in]({{ "/case-studies/locked-in/" | relative_url }}) with [laws]({{ "/case-studies/law/theory/" | relative_url }})?  
- What if we had *observed* [human behaviour]({{ "/guide/human-history/psychology/" | relative_url }}) from our _natural past_{: .bold}, learned from trial and error, and adjusted accordingly?  
- What if we had *respected our biological heritage*, instead of _repressing_{: .warn} it?

Of course, this couldn’t have happened at the time. A [scientific worldview]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#scientific-worldview) had not yet emerged. But now that we **do** have one, we must ask:

What parts of civilisation still serve us—and which need **redesigning**?

What _costs_{: .warn} are we still paying for the Agricultural Transition?

### We Still Carry the Past in Our Bodies and Minds

[Modern humans]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#modern-humans-homo-sapiens) may live in high-rises and use smartphones, but our [brains]({{ "/case-studies/who-we-are/brain/" | relative_url }}) are still **wired** for *life on the savannah*. We evolved to live in small groups, with strong social bonds and face-to-face interactions. We were adapted for flexibility, cooperation, and immediate feedback—not bureaucracy, abstract [rules]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#rules), or chronic stress.

Yet today, we are:

- Expected to *sit still for hours* in schools and offices  
- _Separated_{: .warn} from *nature and kin* by design  
- _Judged_{: .warn} by metrics, titles, and possessions
- Surrounded by strangers, yet often _deeply lonely_{: .bold} 

[Evolution]({{ "/guide/human-history/evolution/" | relative_url }}) didn’t stop when cities arose. But many of our systems began _ignoring_{: .warn} [human nature]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#human-nature)—treating people as if they were machines, or blank slates, or inherently flawed.

Many modern ills aren’t “human nature”—*they’re [symptoms]({{ "/case-studies/society/normal-living/treating-symptoms/" | relative_url }}) of a* [mismatch]({{ "/case-studies/mismatches/" | relative_url }}).

### Questioning the New “Normal”

![Transition]({{ "/assets/images/guide/human-history/transition-old.webp" | | relative_url }})

This project does **not** argue for returning to the past (despite what our fun, romanticised and deliberately provacative images might suggest). Few would want to give up modern healthcare or technology. We rightly _celebrate_{: .bold} the *enormous progress* recently made in the last century or two—especially in reducing child mortality, increasing lifespan and science/technology.

But we must also acknowledge the _costs_{: .warn}:

- *Mental illness* is widespread and rising  
- *Loneliness and alienation* are epidemic  
- Many laws, therapies, and institutions **lack scientific foundation** (as we explore thoroughly in the [Case Studies]({{ "/case-studies/" | relative_url }}) section of this website).
- Cultural norms still restrict expression, reinforce [inequality]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#inequality), and ignore our biological needs  

These aren’t small glitches. They’re consequences of *systems designed without regard for* **human nature**.

### Where We Go From Here

This Human History section is about *context*—a foundation for [thinking critically]({{ "/guide/thinking-for-yourself/" | relative_url }}) about the systems we live in.

We must understand:

- What kind of [animal we are]({{ "/case-studies/who-we-are/" | relative_url }})
- How the [Agricultural Transition]({{ "/guide/human-history/agricultural-transition/" | relative_url }}) shaped what we now call **“normal”**
- Why many problems persist—not despite progress, but sometimes *because* of it

Civilisation is [_an experiment_{: .bold}]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#experiment). Much of it is still based on [intersubjective]({{ "/guide/first-principles/intersubjective/" | relative_url }}) **belief**, not _objective evidence_{: .bold}. And while we can't undo the Agricultural Transition, we can choose to move forward more intelligently.

_The goal is not nostalgia for the past._{: .subtle}

It’s clarity about the present—and **a better design for the future**.
