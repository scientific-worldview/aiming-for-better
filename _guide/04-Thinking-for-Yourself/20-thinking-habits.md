### Overview

[Critical thinking]({{ "/guide/thinking-for-yourself/" | relative_url }}) isn’t just a skill you learn once—it’s a set of _mental habits_{: .bold} you *practice every day*. The more you use them, the more naturally they _shape how you understand the world_{: .mark}.

![Thinking-Essentials]({{ "/assets/images/guide/thinking/habits.webp" | | relative_url }})

---

### 1. Start from a Clean Slate

Don’t _assume_{: .warn} something is "true" just because you’ve *heard it often* or it *“feels right”*. Strip away *assumptions*, _inherited_{: .warn} [beliefs]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#beliefs), and *emotional attachments*.

Begin with the question:

_“If I knew nothing about this topic, what would I need to know to make a sound judgement?”_{: .subtle}

---

### 2. Demand Testability

Ask whether a claim can actually be [tested]({{ "/guide/first-principles/testability/" | relative_url }})—and how. If there’s no possible way to prove it wrong, it’s **a belief**, _not a fact_{: .bold}.

_Testable claims can be investigated, measured, and compared against reality._{: .subtle}

---

### 3. Seek Reliable Evidence

Look for information from sources that have a track record of accuracy, transparency, and methodological rigour. Be aware of the difference between [evidence]({{ "/guide/first-principles/evidence/" | relative_url }}) and **interpretation**.

_Whenever possible, examine the original source instead of relying on second-hand summaries._{: .subtle}

---

### 4. Consider Multiple Perspectives

Your own viewpoint will *always be limited*. Seek out credible alternative explanations—especially those that _challenge your initial opinion_{: .bold}.

This isn’t about *“agreeing with everyone”* but ensuring you’re not stuck in an [echo chamber]({{ "/case-studies/politics/dysfunction/echo-chambers/" | relative_url }}).

---

### 5. Separate Facts from Values

[Facts]({{ "/guide/first-principles/correct-answers/" | relative_url }}) describe _reality_{: .bold}; [values]({{ "/case-studies/society/sociology/values/" | relative_url }}) describe *what we care about*. Confusing the two leads to poor reasoning.  

Example: “Climate change is happening” is a *factual claim*. “We should act to reduce it” is a *value-based claim*.

_Both can be discussed—but they require different kinds of reasoning._{: .subtle}

---

### 6. Identify Cognitive Biases

We all have _mental shortcuts_{: .bold} that can distort [thinking—confirmation bias]({{ "/guide/thinking-for-yourself/cognitive-biases/" | relative_url }}), anchoring, [overconfidence]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#overconfidence), and more.  
Critical thinking means _spotting these tendencies_{: .bold} and adjusting for them, **not** *pretending we don’t have them*.

---

### 7. Stay Proportionate

Give weight to claims based on the quality and quantity of supporting evidence. Extraordinary claims really do require extraordinary evidence.

_Avoid the trap of treating every opinion as equally valid._{: .subtle}

---

### 8. Be Willing to Revise Your View

[Changing your mind]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#changing-your-mind) when faced with better evidence is _a sign of strength_{: .mark}, *not weakness*.
 
_The goal is not to “win arguments” but to align your beliefs more closely with reality._{: .subtle}
