### Overview

If you're forming your view of the world through headlines and hashtags, you're probably terrified. War, crisis, [corruption]({{ "/case-studies/politics/bureaucratic-machine/corruption/" | relative_url }}), extinction—it’s endless. But is this *sense of doom* _evidence-based_{: .bold}, or is it another form of _mental conditioning_{: .bold}?

![Bad-News]({{ "/assets/images/guide/obstacles/news.webp" | | relative_url }})

If we begin from a [clean slate]({{ "/guide/clean-slate/" | relative_url }}), we must ask: **What do the numbers actually say?**.

_Not what we feel, not what we’re told, but what the data shows._{: .subtle}

### Objective Evidence vs Intersubjective Fear

Media institutions, politicians, and campaigners often operate within an [intersubjective]({{ "/guide/first-principles/intersubjective/" | relative_url }}) _bubble_{: .bold} where [fear]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#fear) is the *currency of persuasion*.

It draws attention, maintains loyalty, and fuels action. But the [scientific worldview]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#scientific-worldview)—rooted in [testability]({{ "/guide/first-principles/testability/" | relative_url }}) and []({{ "/guide/first-principles/scientific-method/" | relative_url }})reliability—demands a different approach.

To evaluate the claim that **“the world is falling apart”**, we must go beyond vibes and look at measurable, observable, falsifiable [evidence]({{ "/guide/first-principles/evidence/" | relative_url }}).

### A Reality Check: Hans Rosling’s *Factfulness*

The late **Hans Rosling** spent his career correcting misconceptions. In study after study, he found that people—rich or poor, educated or not—*consistently underestimated human progress*. And not by a little: they scored _worse than chance_{: .bold}, meaning random guesses would have been more accurate.

Why? Because our [brains]({{ "/case-studies/who-we-are/brain/" | relative_url }}) _evolved to notice threats_{: .bold}. And today's media environment _hijacks_{: .warn} that **instinct**.

### The Evidence: A Quiet Revolution in Human Wellbeing

- _Extreme Poverty Has Plummeted_{: .bullet}  
  
  In 1800, 85% of humans lived in _extreme [poverty]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#poverty). Now? Fewer than 10%. Market access, global trade, and infrastructure—not miracles—lifted billions out of misery.

- _Child Mortality Has Fallen Off a Cliff_{: .bullet}  
  
  100 years ago, 1 in 3 children died before the age of 5. Now it’s below 4% globally—and under 0.4% in developed nations. This isn't luck; it’s vaccines, hygiene, and [nutrition]({{ "/case-studies/health/diet/" | relative_url }}).

- _Lifespans Have Doubled_{: .bullet}  
  
  In 1900, average life expectancy was about 35. Now it's over 72. We've invented treatments our ancestors couldn't dream of.

- _Literacy is Normal_{: .bullet}  
  
  In 1900, the world was mostly illiterate. Today, more than 86% can read and write. And girls’ access to education—once actively denied—is transforming cultures from the ground up.

- _Global Access to Tech_{: .bullet}  
  
  Mobile phones in remote villages. Internet access in conflict zones. Access to clean water, electricity, and vaccines are now normal for billions.

### So Why the Dread?

Because improvement is invisible and silent, and fear is loud.

Even as we eliminate poverty, reduce suffering, and extend life, we are *trained to focus on the exceptions*. We treat the 4% of unsolved problems as if they define the whole. This _distortion_{: .warn} isn’t accidental—it’s _profitable_{: .warn}.

Yesterday's _miracles_{: .bold} (e.g. a surviving child, living beyond your 40s, a literate society) is now *expected*. So our [tolerance]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#tolerance) for remaining imperfections has shrunk, even as the world becomes **objectively safer**.

### The Core Issue: Narrative vs Reality

This is a textbook case of *mistaking* [intersubjective]({{ "/guide/first-principles/intersubjective/" | relative_url }}) **anxiety** for "objective decline".

Yes, *problems remain*—climate change, conflict, [inequality]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#inequality) and [injustice]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#justice-and-injustice). But _solving them requires clarity_{: .mark}, **not** [panic]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#panic).

The _scientific worldview_{: .bold} encourages us to acknowledge both *how far we’ve come* and **what remains to be done**.

> Fear doesn’t help us think clearly. *It helps others control what we think*.
