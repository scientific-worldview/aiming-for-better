### Overview

Some people cannot — **or will not** — start from a [clean slate]({{ "/guide/clean-slate/" | relative_url }}).

They are *not interested* in [testability]({{ "/guide/first-principles/testability/" | relative_url }}), falsifiability, or [first principles]({{ "/guide/first-principles/" | relative_url }}). For them, the discussion is over before it begins: their [belief]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#beliefs) is _"true"_{: .warn}, and any contrary [evidence]({{ "/guide/first-principles/evidence/" | relative_url }}) is either cherry-picked away or dismissed as fabricated.

These are the [True Believers]({{ "/articles/philosophy/true-believers/" | relative_url }}).

![True-Believers]({{ "/assets/images/guide/obstacles/true.webp" | | relative_url }})

They can be anyone — the neighbour next door, politician, a social media influencer, or a senior figure in one of the [secular priesthoods]({{ "/guide/obstacles/secular-priesthood/" | relative_url }}) we’ve just explored. Within the [echo chambers]({{ "/case-studies/politics/dysfunction/echo-chambers/" | relative_url }}) of like-minded peers, their _“truths”_{: .warn} are not just maintained but *celebrated*. Conviction itself becomes a kind of **moral trophy**, *as if stubbornness were a virtue*.

---

### Harari’s Lens: Objective, Subjective, Intersubjective

Yuval Noah Harari’s *Sapiens* offers a useful way to understand where true belief sits.

- _Objective phenomena_{: .bullet} exist independently of human opinion — gravity, atoms, the laws of physics. They can be *tested, observed, and verified*.
- _Subjective phenomena_{: .bullet} exist only within *individual experience* — the taste of coffee, a preference for blue.
- _Intersubjective phenomena_{: .bullet} exist only because _many people believe them_{: .bold}. Money, nations, corporations, religions, moral codes, and [laws]({{ "/case-studies/law/theory/" | relative_url }}) are all examples. They are sustained by shared [narratives]({{ "/case-studies/narratives/" | relative_url }}), not by nature itself.

_Intersubjective realities are not inherently bad._{: .subtle}

They [_enable cooperation_{: .bold}]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#cooperation), stability, cultural richness, and _shared purpose_{: .bold}. But they also produce [inequality]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#inequality), [oppression]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#oppression), conflict, and — crucially here — [dogmatism]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#dogma).

### When Intersubjective Becomes “Objective” in the Mind

[True Believers]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#true-believers) are those who:

- _Identify with the narrative._{: .bullet} 
  
  The belief system isn’t just something they hold — *it’s who they are*.

- _Confuse intersubjective with objective._{: .bullet}
-   
  They treat _human-made constructs_{: .bold} *as if* they were _laws of nature_{: .bold}.

- _Resist doubt._{: .bullet}
  
  Questions are dangerous. Skeptics are heretics.

- _Seek moral certainty._{: .bullet}

  They divide the world into “good” and “bad”, and their side always wears the white hat.

- _Close the echo chamber._{: .bullet}
  
  They surround themselves with people, institutions, and media that _reinforce the narrative_{: .bold}.

This mindset thrives in the same soil as the _secular priesthoods_{: .bold} — institutions with the [power]({{ "/case-studies/politics/governance/power/" | relative_url }}) to declare orthodoxy and punish dissent.

---

### Why This Is an Obstacle to Clear Thinking

True Believers don’t just reject certain [facts]({{ "/guide/first-principles/correct-answers/" | relative_url }}) — they reject the very idea that a fact could contradict their [worldview]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#worldview).  
Once you’ve granted *belief itself* the highest moral status, you no longer need to test your ideas against reality. You’ve built a closed loop.

The danger isn’t limited to the fringe. Many of history’s worst injustices — [slavery]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#slavery), colonialism, political purges — were defended by large groups of True Believers who saw their cause as _righteous beyond question_{: .bold}. The [_same psychology_{: .bold}]({{ "/guide/human-history/psychology/" | relative_url }}) drives online pile-ons, [ideological]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#ideology) censorship, and the stubborn defence of failed policies today.

### Are We Talking About Religion?

**True Believers** are not limited to the [religious]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#religion). In fact, many are entirely secular.

They can be found in academia, policy-making, and professional practice — people who *sound* scientific, dress their claims in technical jargon, and yet base their convictions on nothing more than _intersubjective narratives_{: .bold}.

### Beyond Religion

Religion has its own long history of untestable beliefs, but here our focus is different.

This obstacle is about those who *conflate the intersubjective with the objective* — who treat shared cultural narratives, moral frameworks, or theoretical models as if they were as solid and universal as the laws of physics.

These may include:

- _Theorists_{: .bullet} devoted to postmodernism, Freud, Marx, or other “isms” that claim *sweeping explanatory power* _without_{: .warn} [empirical]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#empirical) rigour.  
- _Practitioners_{: .bullet} in social work, [therapy]({{ "/guide/human-history/therapy-culture/" | relative_url }}), probation, or behavioural psychology who apply interventions as if they were grounded science, when in reality they are often narrative-based frameworks with limited or [untested]({{ "/guide/first-principles/testability/" | relative_url }}) evidence.  
- _Policy-makers_{: .bullet} and advisers who defend programmes as if their success is self-evident, without any falsifiable testing.

When their work *is* science, that’s great, and long may it continue. But too often, what is presented as _objective knowledge_{: .bold} is really just an [intersubjective]({{ "/guide/first-principles/intersubjective/" | relative_url }}) [consensus]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#consensus) within a profession or discipline.

### The Two Cultures Problem

C. P. Snow described the divide between the sciences and the humanities as [[The Two Cultures]({{ "/articles/science/two-cultures/" | relative_url }})].

While bridging that divide is valuable, a recurring problem arises when certain areas of the humanities *adopt the tone and [authority]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#authority) of science without its methods*. The result is an overconfident, jargon-laden belief system, immune to falsification, presented as if it were empirical fact.

### Why This Matters

_True Believers — religious or secular — resist genuine critical examination._{: .subtle}

Their convictions are often reinforced within closed communities or professional bubbles, where dissent is [framed]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#framing) as ignorance, moral failing, or ideological opposition.

The danger is not disagreement itself, but the *illusion of objectivity*.

If a claim cannot be tested, falsified, or subjected to evidence-based review, then it belongs in the realm of *shared belief*, **not** *established fact*. And if it is merely a shared belief, *it must be recognised as such* — especially by those advocating policy or professional practice.

**Key takeaway:** Beware of the secular “priesthood” as much as the religious kind. The robes may be replaced with PowerPoint slides and citations, but the faith-like [certainty]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#certainty) remains.

## Escaping the Trap

We can’t eliminate intersubjective realities — *nor should we*. They’re part of what makes large-scale human cooperation possible. But we can guard against the True Believer mindset by:

- _Returning to evidence_{: .bullet} even [when it’s uncomfortable]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#uncomfortable-truths).  
- Distinguishing “what exists” from “what we’ve agreed to believe exists.”  
- Valuing questions over allegiance. 
- _Welcoming dissent_{: .bullet} as a stress-test, *not a threat*.

To [think freely]({{ "/guide/thinking-for-yourself/" | relative_url }}) is to resist turning conviction into a cage.

And when someone says, *“I just know it’s true”*, that’s the moment to ask: _Show me the evidence_{: .bold}.
