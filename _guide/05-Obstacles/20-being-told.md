### Overview

A _scientific and evidence-based worldview_{: .bold} accepts that [objective]({{ "/guide/first-principles/intersubjective/" | relative_url }}) phenomena can be known with a high degree of certainty — *but only* when supported by **overwhelming, reliable evidence**. Everything else must be treated with _scepticism_{: .bold}, especially when you are merely _told_{: .warn} what to believe.

From early childhood, people are instructed in *what to think* — **not** _how to think_{: .bold}. This happens in schools, workplaces, [religious]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#religion) settings, media, and government. While some teachings are *well-intentioned*, others are grounded in [tradition]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#tradition), power, ideology, or convenience — not [evidence]({{ "/guide/first-principles/evidence/" | relative_url }}).

![Being-Told]({{ "/assets/images/guide/obstacles/told.webp" | | relative_url }})

_The problem is bigger than just false facts._{: .subtle}

We also _inherit_{: .warn} intersubjective [narratives]({{ "/case-studies/narratives/" | relative_url }}) — widely shared stories or “truths” that *bind [groups]({{ "/case-studies/society/social-structures/the-clan/" | relative_url }}) together*, but which may have _little basis in objective reality_{: .mark}. These narratives shape [laws]({{ "/case-studies/law/theory/" | relative_url }}), customs, and even scientific [consensus]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#consensus). [Thinking for yourself]({{ "/guide/thinking-for-yourself/" | relative_url }}) means spotting when you are being handed a narrative rather than verifiable truth.

### Childhood: “This is the God You Should Believe In”

Children tend to inherit religion geographically:

- In a Christian family: “Jesus is the Son of God.”
- In a Muslim family: “Muhammad is the final prophet.”
- In a Hindu family: “There are many gods.”

![Being-Told]({{ "/assets/images/guide/obstacles/being-told.webp" | | relative_url }})

These aren’t _reasoned conclusions_{: .bold} — they are *cultural defaults*. Rarely is a child encouraged to _evaluate_{: .bold} religious claims with logic, evidence, or comparative analysis.

_Almost never are they told that disbelief is an option._{: .subtle}

### School: “This is How History Happened”

History [education]({{ "/case-studies/society/social-structures/education/" | relative_url }}) often reflects nationalist or [ideological]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#ideology) [bias]({{ "/guide/thinking-for-yourself/cognitive-biases/" | relative_url }}).

- _UK_{: .bullet}: The British Empire may be described as a [civilising mission], *omitting* its _violence_{: .warn} and [exploitation]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#exploitation).
- _US_{: .bullet}: The _genocide_{: .warn} of indigenous peoples and the legacy of [slavery]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#slavery) are often sanitised.

Even the scientific canon has been distorted:

- _Galileo_{: .bullet} was forced to recant heliocentrism by the Church in 1633.
- _Giordano Bruno_{: .bullet} was burned alive for cosmological ideas later shown to be accurate.

These examples show that [authority]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#authority) often suppresses truth when it _contradicts_{: .warn} existing [power]({{ "/case-studies/politics/governance/power/" | relative_url }}).

### Society: “This is the Right Way to Live”

We are often told there is one sensible life path:

1. Go to school  
2. Get a stable job  
3. Get married  
4. Buy a home  
5. Have children  

Alternative routes — child-free living, minimalist lifestyles, creative vocations — are treated as suspect. But these *societal scripts* are _culturally constructed_{: .bold}, not objectively correct.

### Science & Health: “Eat Your Carbs, Avoid Fat”

For decades, government health advice told us to:

- Fear fat  
- Prioritise carbohydrates  
- Trust food pyramids and cereal boxes

This advice — backed by industry-friendly “experts” — ignored mounting [evidence]({{ "/guide/first-principles/evidence/" | relative_url }}) on the harms of refined carbs and sugar. The _obesity epidemic_{: .bold} is partly rooted in *decades of flawed guidance*.

### Politics: “These Are the Good Guys, Those Are the Bad Guys”

Governments and media simplify complex geopolitical realities:

- “Our wars = freedom”
- “Their wars = tyranny”

Real **motivations** often include *oil, resources, or political dominance*.

Examples:

- _The Vietnam War_{: .bullet} was presented as a fight against communism, but involved *war crimes* and *mass civilian deaths*.
- _The Iraq War (2003)_{: .bullet} was *justified* by _false claims_{: .bold} of WMDs, later shown to be baseless.

---

### Medicine: “Doctors Know Best — Don’t Question It”

Medical authority was once absolute, leading to:

- _Bloodletting_{: .bullet} to cure disease
- _Thalidomide_{: .bullet} prescriptions despite insufficient testing
- _Opioid overprescription_{: .bullet}, driven by pharmaceutical marketing

Even today, treatment guidelines can be shaped by [lobbying]({{ "/case-studies/politics/bureaucratic-machine/lobbying/" | relative_url }}), outdated assumptions, or poor evidence. Trust must be earned — not _assumed_{: .warn}.

### Workplace: “Work Hard and You’ll Be Rewarded”

The [myth]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#myth):  

_“Loyalty and hard work will be recognised”._{: .subtle}

The reality:

- Mass layoffs  
- Wage stagnation  
- [Burnout culture]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#burnout)  
- Exploitative gig work

Corporations are often incentivised to maximise _shareholder value_{: .bold}, not employee [well-being]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#wellbeing).

---

### Media: “Trust Us, We Report the Truth”

Most people get their [worldview]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#worldview) from media — yet media organisations are influenced by:

- Political _agendas_{: .warn}  
- Corporate advertisers  
- The demand for [clicks and outrage]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#clicks-and-outrage)  

Misrepresentation is *routine*:

- Cherry-picked [statistics]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#statistics)  
- Loaded language  
- Omission of key context  

Real critical thinking means _reading laterally_{: .bold}, comparing sources, and seeking primary data.

### Justice System: “Trust Us, Our Programmes Work”

The UK’s [Offending Behaviour Programmes]({{ "/case-studies/pseudoscience/offender-treatment-industry/" | relative_url }}) (OBPs) *illustrate misplaced trust*:

- _Sex Offender Treatment Programme (SOTP)_{: .bullet} was discontinued in 2017 after studies showed *higher reoffending* [behaviour]({{ "/guide/human-history/psychology/" | relative_url }}) rates among participants than non-participants.
- _RESOLVE_{: .bullet}, **BBR**, and **TSP** were rolled out without solid evidence of effectiveness — involving thousands of participants and millions of pounds.

These were "interventions" driven by _ideology_{: .warn}, **industry**, bureaucracy, and public appeasement — *not [empirical]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#empirical) success*.

### The Bigger Problem: Certainty Without Evidence

Many of history’s _worst ideas_{: .warn} were delivered with [absolute certainty]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#certainty):

- That the Sun orbited the Earth  
- That bleeding cured illness  
- That ulcers were caused by stress (not *H. pylori*)  
- That homosexuality was a mental illness  
- That race determined intelligence  

All were presented by **teachers**, **clergy**, **scientists**, or **leaders** — with the *full weight of institutional power behind them*.

But being told is not the same as _being shown_{: .bold}.

## What Thinking for Yourself Really Means

It doesn’t mean rejecting expertise — it means _demanding evidence_{: .bold}.  
It doesn’t mean assuming you’re right — it means being _willing to be wrong_{: .bold}.  
It doesn’t mean thinking alone — it means _thinking independently_{: .bold}.

A student who memorises facts *without questioning them* is not _thinking scientifically_{: .bold} — they are only reciting ‘knowledge’ that may one day be proven false.

_Ask questions. Demand evidence. Challenge authority. Think for yourself._{: .subtle}
