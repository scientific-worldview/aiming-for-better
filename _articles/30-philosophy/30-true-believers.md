The distinction between objective, subjective, and [intersubjective]({{ "/guide/first-principles/intersubjective/" | relative_url }}) and the terms “imagined order” and [true believers]({{ "/guide/obstacles/true-believers/" | relative_url }}) is given in the book “Sapiens: A Brief History of Humankind” by Yuval Noah Harari. It is easy reading for a lay person and I recommend it as a very good and interesting read.

Before encountering the term “intersubjective” I tended to use various terms such as [social construction]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#social-construction), “song sheets”, [“herd animals”]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#the-herd) and such, that were not very academic.

### Harari's Categories

- _Objective phenomena:_{: .bullet} This refers to things that exist independently of human perception and [belief]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#beliefs). It's the realm of the natural sciences – gravity, atoms, the [laws]({{ "/case-studies/law/theory/" | relative_url }}) of physics, the evolutionary process. These are facts that can be verified - there ARE [correct answers]({{ "/guide/first-principles/correct-answers/" | relative_url }})! - through observation and/or experimentation, regardless of individual opinion or cultural background.
- _Subjective phenomena:_{: .bullet} This is the realm of individual experiences, feelings, and beliefs. It's the unique way each person perceives and interprets the world. Subjective experiences are valid for the individual, but they cannot be objectively verified or shared universally. (For example: "I like the colour blue".)
- _Intersubjective phenomena:_{: .bullet} This is the realm of shared beliefs, [values]({{ "/case-studies/society/sociology/values/" | relative_url }}), and concepts that exist only because a large group of people believe in them. They are neither purely objective nor purely subjective but are constructed and maintained through shared [narratives]({{ "/case-studies/narratives/" | relative_url }}) and [mutual agreement]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#consensus). Examples include:
  - _Money:_{: .bullet} The value of a pound coin or a bitcoin exists only because people collectively agree that it has value.
  - _Nations:_{: .bullet} The concept of "Iraq”, "Japan", or "Wales" exists only in the collective imagination of people who share a common national identity.
  - _Corporations:_{: .bullet} Companies like Google or Manchester United are legal fictions – they exist only because the legal system recognises them as entities with [rights]({{ "/case-studies/politics/governance/rights/" | relative_url }}) and obligations.
  - _Religion:_{: .bullet} The belief in a god, specific [religious]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#religion) practices, and the [authority]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#authority) of religious institutions are all intersubjective.
  - _Morality:_{: .bullet} What is considered "right" and "wrong" often varies between cultures and time periods, demonstrating its intersubjective nature.
  - _Laws:_{: .bullet} Laws are a codification of shared [social norms]({{ "/case-studies/society/sociology/norms/" | relative_url }}) and agreements.

### The Positives of Intersubjective Phenomena

- _Large-Scale Cooperation:_{: .bullet} Intersubjective realities enable humans to cooperate in massive numbers, something no other species can do. Shared beliefs, values, and laws create social cohesion and allow for the formation of complex societies.
- _Social Order and Stability:_{: .bullet} Intersubjective systems, while sometimes flawed, provide a framework for social order. They create expectations, guide [behaviour]({{ "/guide/human-history/psychology/" | relative_url }}), and reduce chaos. Even when unjust, they often provide some form of stability.
- _Cultural Richness and Diversity:_{: .bullet} Intersubjective systems give rise to art, music, literature, and diverse cultures that enrich the human experience.
- _Meaning and Purpose:_{: .bullet} For many, their intersubjective realities – religion, national identity, community involvement – provide a sense of [meaning]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#meaning), belonging, and meaning to life.
- _Shared Goals:_{: .bullet} Intersubjective realities can unite people to work towards shared goals, from building [civilisations]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#civilisations) to fighting for social justice.

### The Negatives of Intersubjective Phenomena

- _Inequality and Oppression:_{: .bullet} Many historical and contemporary injustices stem from biased or unfair intersubjective systems. [Slavery]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#slavery), colonialism, sexism, racism, classism and the criminalisation of [natural]({{ "/guide/human-history/natural-vs-normal/" | relative_url }}) behaviours (such as homosexuality or ingestion of hallucinogenic mushrooms) are all supported by shared beliefs and social constructs.
- _Conflict and Violence:_{: .bullet} Disagreements over intersubjective values and beliefs can lead to conflict, violence, and even war. The persecution of [minorities]({{ "/case-studies/us-vs-them/majorities-vs-minorities" | relative_url }}) or political opponents is a direct result of conflicting intersubjective systems.
- _Exploitation and Manipulation:_{: .bullet} Powerful elites can manipulate intersubjective systems to maintain their control and [exploit]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#exploitation) the masses. [Propaganda]({{ "/guide/human-history/propaganda/" | relative_url }}), [misinformation]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#misinformation), and appeals to [nationalism]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#nationalism) or religious fervor are used to keep people compliant.
- _Resistance to Change:_{: .bullet} Because intersubjective realities are deeply ingrained, they can be resistant to change, even when they are harmful or no longer serve their purpose.
- _Dogmatism, Intolerance and True Believers:_{: .bullet} True believers in a particular intersubjective system can become [dogmatic]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#dogma) and intolerant of alternative viewpoints. This can hinder social progress and stifle creativity and innovation.

<h2><u>True Believers</u></h2>

Harari doesn't use the term "[true believers]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#true-believers)" in a strictly formal or academic sense; rather, he uses it to describe a particular mindset and a mode of relating to intersubjective phenomena. Here are the key characteristics of "true believers" as they appear in Harari's work, particularly in "Sapiens" and "Homo Deus":

#### Unquestioning Devotion to Intersubjective Narratives

- True believers are not simply people who participate in a shared belief system; they are deeply committed to and emotionally invested in it.
- They don't just believe in the narrative; they identify with it. Their sense of self is often intertwined with the intersubjective reality they adhere to.
- They are not open to questioning or critically analyzing the foundations of their belief system. They often view doubt or skepticism as a threat.

#### Perceiving the Intersubjective as Objective

- True believers tend to conflate the intersubjective with the objective. They treat their shared narratives, values, and beliefs as if they were objective truths, as solid as the laws of physics.
- They often struggle to recognise that their beliefs are ultimately human constructs and that other cultures might have different yet equally valid perspectives.
- They often see their intersubjective "truths" as superior, and this often results in intolerance toward alternative beliefs and cultures.

#### Emotional Investment and Moral Certainty

- True believers are often driven by strong emotions, such as loyalty, patriotism, religious fervor, or moral indignation.
- They tend to see the world in stark, black-and-white terms, with a clear division between "good" and "bad". Their chosen intersubjective system is typically on the "good" side of this divide.
- They have a strong sense of [moral certainty]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#certainty) and often see themselves as fighting for a just and righteous cause or [agenda]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#agendas). This moral certainty can be destructive when it results in the suffering of others, as numerous examples from history show.

#### Resistance to Dissent and Alternative Views

- True believers tend to be resistant to dissent and alternative viewpoints, and will actively seek to dismiss them as false ideology, criminal ideation, “plausible account”, etc.
- They may view those who disagree with their beliefs, norms, values or laws as enemies, traitors, infidels, criminal (ideation), [deviant]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#deviant), morally degenerate, and many other labels.
- They often create [echo chambers]({{ "/case-studies/politics/dysfunction/echo-chambers/" | relative_url }}) of like-minded individuals (often institutions such as The Church, Political Parties, [The Criminal Justice System]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#justice-and-injustice) etc), where their beliefs are reinforced and alternative perspectives are marginalized.

#### Propensity for Collective Action and Extremism

- True believers are often highly motivated to take collective action in support of their beliefs. This can lead to both positive social change (e.g., fighting for civil rights) and to negative actions (e.g., violence or [oppression]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#oppression) against religious or secular minorities).
- True believers and their [echo chambers]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#echo-chambers) often produce [sciency sounding]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#sciency-sounding) textbooks giving "authoritative" statements/explanations rather than [Evidence]({{ "/approach/02-First-Principles/04-evidence/" | relative_url }}).
- When the belief system they hold is itself negative or harmful, the "true believer" mindset can lead to extremism, fanaticism, and a willingness to commit acts of violence or oppression to uphold their belief system.

#### Why "True Believers" Are Important in Harari's Framework

- _Drivers of Social Change:_{: .bullet} Harari emphasises that intersubjective systems are powerful drivers of human behaviour. He argues that "true believers" are essential to the functioning of these systems, both for positive and negative consequences.
- _Source of Conflict and Division:_{: .bullet} However, because they are invested in "their" particular version of the intersubjective, they are often a source of conflict and division. Conflicts often arise because different groups of people are committed to different and often contradictory intersubjective realities.
- _Understanding Human History:_{: .bullet} Harari suggests that by understanding the mindset of "true believers", we can gain a deeper understanding of [human history]({{ "/guide/human-history/" | relative_url }}), including its greatest achievements and its most terrible atrocities.

### Improving Social Orders Through Scientific Integration

How can we leverage the power of the [scientific method]({{ "/guide/first-principles/scientific-method/" | relative_url }}) and [rational thinking]({{ "/guide/thinking-for-yourself/" | relative_url }}) to create better intersubjective systems? Here are some ways:

#### Evidence-Based Policymaking

- Instead of relying on [tradition]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#tradition), ideology, anecdotal evidence, authoritative explanations, or consensus of opinion, policymakers should base their decisions on rigorous scientific data and analysis.
- This applies to all areas, from healthcare and [education]({{ "/case-studies/society/social-structures/education/" | relative_url }}) to environmental protection and economic policy.
- It also means critically evaluating the [evidence]({{ "/guide/first-principles/evidence/" | relative_url }}) being presented and avoiding misinterpretations or [manipulation]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#manipulation).

#### Promoting Critical Thinking

- Instead of the [“hidden curriculum”]({{ "/posts/2026-09-05-Curiosity" | relative_url }}), education systems need to prioritise the development of critical thinking skills. This means teaching people not what to think, but “how” to think: how to evaluate information, identify [bias]({{ "/guide/thinking-for-yourself/cognitive-biases/" | relative_url }}), and engage in reasoned argumentation.
- A [society]({{ "/case-studies/society/social-structures/" | relative_url }}) of critical thinkers is less likely to fall prey to misinformation and propaganda and more likely to challenge harmful intersubjective systems.

#### Open and Transparent Dialogue

- Create spaces for open and respectful dialogue about important social issues. Encourage a diversity of perspectives and challenge assumptions and [biases]({{ "/guide/04-Thinking-for-Yourself/30-biases" | relative_url }}).
- Engage in reasoned debate based on evidence, rather than resorting to emotional appeals or personal attacks.

#### Adaptability and Change

- Recognise that intersubjective systems are not static and that they need to be adapted to changing circumstances.
- Embrace a mindset of continuous improvement and be willing to question long-held beliefs when evidence suggests a need for change.

#### Scientific Literacy

- Promote widespread scientific literacy so that people understand how scientific knowledge is generated and why it is trustworthy.
- This will help to combat anti-science attitudes and enable people to make informed decisions based on evidence.

The ability to create and maintain intersubjective phenomena has been a double-edged sword for humanity. The legal fictions known as companies and other intersubjective phenomena have enabled incredible achievements within objective reality and fostered rich cultures. But it has also been the source of great suffering and injustice, particularly when two differing intersubjective ideologies clash or, within such ideologies, at the hands of “True Believers”, such as religious or secular “priests", who very often oppress humanity with their ideologies. The hope is that by integrating scientific [principles]({{ "/guide/first-principles/" | relative_url }}) into our intersubjective systems, we can harness their positive potential while mitigating their negative impacts. If you hope that we can build more just, sustainable, and prosperous societies for all humanity, I contend this can only be realised through a [rational]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#rational), critical, and scientific approach.
