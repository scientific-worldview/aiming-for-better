## A Very Brief History of Worldviews

### China

- _Confucianism_{: .bullet} (*c. 6th–5th century BCE*) – A system of ethics, governance, and social philosophy founded by Confucius, emphasizing [hierarchy]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#hierarchies), duty, and moral virtue.
- _Taoism (Daoism)_{: .bullet} (*c. 4th–3rd century BCE*) – A philosophical and religious [tradition]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#tradition) emphasizing harmony with the Dao (the Way), spontaneity, and natural order, associated with Laozi and Zhuangzi.
- _Legalism_{: .bullet} (*c. 4th–3rd century BCE*) – A strict political philosophy advocating strong centralized rule, [laws]({{ "/case-studies/law/theory/" | relative_url }}), and punishment, influential in the Qin Dynasty.
- _Mohism_{: .bullet} (*c. 5th–3rd century BCE*) – A utilitarian and [egalitarian]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#egalitarian) philosophy founded by Mozi, advocating universal love, [meritocracy]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#meritocracy), and practical knowledge.

### India

- _Hinduism_{: .bullet} (*developing over millennia, but key texts emerging by 4th century BCE*) – A broad set of [religious]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#religion) and philosophical traditions incorporating Vedic traditions, metaphysics, and spiritual practices.
- _Buddhism_{: .bullet} (*5th–4th century BCE*) – Founded by Siddhartha Gautama (the Buddha), emphasizing the Four Noble Truths, the Eightfold Path, and liberation from suffering.
- _Jainism_{: .bullet} (*predates Buddhism, but influential c. 6th–5th century BCE*) – A non-theistic religion emphasizing nonviolence (ahimsa), strict asceticism, and karmic liberation.
- _Ajivika_{: .bullet} (*c. 5th–3rd century BCE*) – A now-extinct philosophical school that held a deterministic view of fate and denied free will.

### Persia (Iran and Beyond)

- _Zoroastrianism_{: .bullet} (*c. 6th century BCE onward*) – One of the earliest monotheistic religions, founded by Zoroaster, emphasizing the cosmic struggle between good (Ahura Mazda) and evil (Angra Mainyu).
- _Mazdakism_{: .bullet} (*c. 5th century CE but with earlier influences*) – A later offshoot of Zoroastrianism advocating radical social equality and communal living.

### Mesopotamia & Levant

- _Jewish Monotheism_{: .bullet} (*c. 6th–5th century BCE*) – The development of Judaism as a distinct monotheistic faith, influenced by Persian rule and exile experiences.

### Greco-Roman World

- _Hellenism_{: .bullet} (*c. 4th century BCE onward*) – The spread and blending of Greek [culture]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#culture), philosophy, art, and governance across the Mediterranean, the Middle East, and parts of Asia following Alexander the Great’s conquests. It emphasized reason, humanism, science, and cosmopolitanism, influencing later Western and Eastern thought
- _Platonism_{: .bullet} (*c. 4th century BCE*) – The philosophical system of Plato, emphasizing ideal forms and the role of reason.
- _Aristotelianism_{: .bullet} (*c. 4th century BCE*) – Aristotle’s [empirical]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#empirical) approach to knowledge, emphasizing logic, observation, and categorisation.
- _Cynicism_{: .bullet} (*c. 4th century BCE*) – A radical rejection of social conventions and materialism, seeking a simple and virtuous life.
- _Stoicism_{: .bullet} (*c. 3rd century BCE*) – A philosophy of resilience, [rationality]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#rational), and virtue, teaching that emotions should be controlled through reason.
- _Epicureanism_{: .bullet} (*c. 4th–3rd century BCE*) – A philosophy emphasizing moderate pleasure, knowledge, and absence of pain as the highest good.

### Other Civilisations

- _Animism and Shamanism_{: .bullet} (*worldwide, including Africa and the Americas*) – Spiritual traditions focused on nature worship, ancestor veneration, and shamanic meditation.
- _African Traditional Religions_{: .bullet} (*various, dating back thousands of years*) – Indigenous [belief]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#beliefs) systems focusing on [spirits]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#agency-detection), deities, and ancestral guidance.
- _Celtic Druidism_{: .bullet} (*pre-Roman Europe*) – The priestly and philosophical tradition of the Celts, centered on nature, oral traditions, and ritual practices.
