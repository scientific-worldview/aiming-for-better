### <u>Historical</u>

### Lightning as divine punishment  *(Ancient–18th Century)*

- Explanation: Many cultures explained lightning as the wrath of gods, requiring sacrifices or prayers.
- Evidence: Benjamin Franklin’s lightning rod (1752) and the study of electricity showed that lightning is a natural atmospheric phenomenon.

### Burning Witches to Cure Disease or Misfortune *(Middle Ages)*

- Explanation: Misunderstood events like plagues, failed crops, or illness were said to be caused by witches, requiring exorcism or execution.
- Evidence: Scientific investigation into disease, weather, and [psychology]({{ "/guide/human-history/psychology/" | relative_url }}) replaced supernatural thinking, leading to the decline of [witch hunts]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#witch-trials) (although, unfortunately, witch hunts still persist in other guises).

### <u>Medicine</u>

### Stomach Ulcers and Helicobacter pylori *(1980s)*

- Explanation: Doctors long believed and explained to patients that lifestyle, stress, spicy foods, and alcohol were the primary causes of stomach and duodenal ulcers.
- Evidence: In 1982, Barry Marshall and Robin Warren discovered that ulcers were actually caused by the bacterium Helicobacter pylori, which thrives in acidic conditions. Marshall even drank a culture of H. pylori to show the link. Their work, initially dismissed, revolutionized treatment, replacing ineffective lifestyle changes with antibiotics.

### Trepanation (Drilling Holes in the Skull) as a Cure *(Prehistoric–Early Modern Era)*

- Explanation: Drilling holes in the skull was said to release the evil [spirits]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#agency-detection) that were causing mental illness or epilepsy.
- Evidence: Research from neuroscience and medicine offers strong [evidence]({{ "/guide/first-principles/evidence/" | relative_url }}) that mental illnesses have biological and psychological causes, leading to modern treatments.

### Ignaz Semmelweis and Handwashing *(1840s)*

- Explanation: In Semmelweis’ time, doctors believed disease spread through "miasma" (bad air) rather than physical contact.
- Evidence: Semmelweis found that doctors who washed their hands with chlorinated lime significantly reduced maternal deaths in childbirth. Despite clear data, his findings were ridiculed and rejected by medical authorities, leading to unnecessary deaths until germ [theory]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#theory) (Pasteur, Lister, Koch) gained acceptance decades later.

### Bloodletting and Humoral Medicine *(Ancient–19th Century)*

- Explanation: Based on Hippocrates and Galen, early doctors explained that illness resulted from an imbalance of the "four humors" (blood, phlegm, yellow bile, black bile).
- Evidence-Based Discovery: The emergence of scientific medicine, microbiology, and physiology debunked these ideas, yet bloodletting persisted for centuries, often harming patients rather than helping them.

### Smoking and Lung Cancer *(Mid-20th Century)*

- Explanation: The tobacco industry and some medical professionals downplayed links between smoking and cancer, claiming there was no definitive evidence.
- Evidence: Epidemiological studies and lab research established an overwhelming link between smoking and lung cancer, heart disease, and other illnesses—leading to stricter regulations and anti-smoking campaigns.

### Trans Fats and Heart Disease *(20th–21st Century)*

- Explanation: For decades, margarine and trans fats were authoritatively promoted as healthier than butter.
- Evidence: Research eventually showed that trans fats significantly increase the risk of heart disease, leading to bans in many countries.

### Ultrasound and X-Rays During Pregnancy *(20th Century)*

- Explanation: In the early 20th century, X-rays were used on pregnant women without concern.
- Evidence: Evidence showed that X-rays caused harm to foetuses, leading to the widespread adoption of ultrasound as a safer alternative.

### Cholesterol and Diet *(21st Century)*

- Explanation: Dietary cholesterol (e.g., from eggs) was long blamed as a major contributor to heart disease.
- Evidence: More recent studies show that dietary cholesterol has little direct impact on blood cholesterol, and refined carbohydrates may be a bigger factor in heart disease.

### The Gut Microbiome and Health *(Ongoing Research)*

- Explanation: Until recently, gut bacteria were thought to play a minor role in health.
- Evidence-Based Discovery: Emerging research shows the gut microbiome affects digestion, immunity, [mental health]({{ "/case-studies/health/mental-health/" | relative_url }}), and even obesity, shifting how doctors approach disease prevention.

### Lead in Paint and Gasoline *(20th Century)*

- Explanation: Lead was considered a harmless additive in paint and petrol/gasoline.
- Evidence: Studies showed lead exposure causes [brain]({{ "/case-studies/who-we-are/brain/" | relative_url }}) damage, developmental issues, and toxicity, leading to bans in the late 20th century.

### <u>Science</u>

### Geocentrism vs. Heliocentrism *(16th–17th Century)*

- Explanation: The majority of scholars, [religious]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#religion) authorities, and the public believed Earth was the centre of the universe (geocentrism).
- Evidence: Copernicus, Galileo, and Kepler provided mathematical models and telescopic observations proving that Earth orbits the Sun. The [consensus]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#consensus) only shifted after centuries of resistance to the emerging evidence.

### Alchemy vs. Modern Chemistry *(Ancient–18th Century)*

- Explanation: Alchemy claimed metals could be transmuted into gold and that a philosopher’s stone granted immortality.
- Evidence: Lavoisier, Dalton, Mendeleev and others developed modern chemistry, atomic theory, and the periodic table, replacing mystical [beliefs]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#beliefs) with [empirical]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#empirical) science.

### The Ether as the Medium for Light *(Ancient–19th Century)*

- Explanation: Scientists believed light needed a medium ("luminiferous ether") to travel through space.
- Evidence: The Michelson-Morley [experiment]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#experiment) (1887) disproved the ether theory, leading to Einstein’s theory of relativity.

### Newtonian Physics vs. Relativity *(15th-20th century)*

- Explanation: Newtonian physics, which described gravity as a force between objects, was incredibly successful for centuries. So successful it was explained as fact.
- Evidence: Observation determined an anomaly in Mercury’s orbit: its perihelion (closest point to the Sun) was shifting slightly faster than Newtonian physics predicted, a discrepancy of about 0.012 degrees per century. General Relativity accounted for the discrepancy and was evidenced when Eddington’s expedition observed the bending of starlight around the Sun, confirming general relativity’s predictions.

### The Immutable Universe vs. The Expanding Universe *(20th Century)*

- Explanation: Einstein and others initially believed the universe was static and unchanging.
- Evidence: Edwin Hubble’s observations in the 1920s showed that galaxies are moving away from us, showing that the universe is expanding, leading to the Big Bang Theory.

### <u>Geology & Earth Science</u>

### The Age of the Earth *(19th–20th Century)*

- Explanation: Before radiometric dating, most people, including most scientists, underestimated Earth’s age, explaining it was only tens of millions of years old. The Church insisted it to be only thousands of years old.
- Evidence: Radiometric dating and geological evidence confirmed that Earth is 4.5 billion years old, shifting scientific consensus.

### Flat Earth Belief *(Ancient–Middle Ages)*

- Explanation: Most medieval and some ancient cultures said the Earth was flat, often based on religious or mythological explanations.
- Evidence: Eratosthenes (3rd century BCE) measured Earth’s circumference, and circumnavigation and space travel confirmed it is an oblate spheroid.

### Germ Theory vs. Miasma Theory *(19th Century)*

- Explanation: Most doctors and scientists explained that disease was caused by “bad air” (miasma) rather than microorganisms.
- Evidence: Louis Pasteur, Robert Koch, and Joseph Lister demonstrated that bacteria and viruses cause disease, leading to hygiene practices that saved millions of lives.

### Biblical Flood Geology vs. Modern Geology *(19th Century)*

- Explanation: Many early geologists explained that all landforms were shaped by a single global flood (Noachian Flood).
- Evidence: The work of Charles Lyell and others showed that mountains, valleys, and fossils were shaped by gradual processes over millions of years.

### Continental Drift & Plate Tectonics *(20th Century)*

- Explanation: Scientists rejected the idea that continents could move, dismissing Alfred Wegener’s 1912 theory of "continental drift" as impossible.
- Evidence: In the 1960s, discoveries in seafloor spreading and plate tectonics provided overwhelming evidence that continents do indeed move over time, reshaping geology.

### <u>Technology & Engineering</u>

### Oxygen vs. Phlogiston Theory *(18th Century)*

- Explanation: Chemists believed a mysterious substance called phlogiston was released during combustion.
- Evidence: Antoine Lavoisier proved oxygen, not phlogiston, is responsible for combustion and respiration, forming the foundation of modern chemistry.

### Heavier-Than-Air Flight Is Impossible *(19th Century)*

- Explanation: Many scientists and engineers, including Lord Kelvin, explained that flying machines heavier than air could never work.
- Evidence: The Wright brothers (1903) demonstrated powered flight, revolutionizing transportation.

### Supersonic Flight Is Impossible *(Early 20th Century)*

- Explanation: It was widely held that planes could not survive breaking the sound barrier due to increasing air resistance.
- Evidence: In 1947, Chuck Yeager flew the Bell X-1 past Mach 1, proving supersonic flight possible.

### <u>Psychology, Human Behaviour, Economics, Politics & Society</u>

### Witch Hunts and Trials *(Medieval–18th Century)*

- Explanation: Witchcraft and witches were seen as real threats, leading to horrible executions and persecution of innocent people.
- Evidence: Improving scientific understanding of mental illness, hysteria, and [natural]({{ "/guide/human-history/natural-vs-normal/" | relative_url }}) phenomena ended the widespread belief in witchcraft.

### Humans Have More Than Five Senses *(21st Century Research)*

- Explanation: The idea that humans have only five senses (sight, smell, taste, touch, hearing) was widely accepted.
- Evidence: Neurologists now recognise additional senses, such as proprioception (body awareness), equilibrioception (balance), and thermoreception (temperature detection).

### Women Are Biologically Unfit for Education *(Ancient–20th Century)*

- Explanation: Women were said to be mentally and physically unsuited for higher education, leadership, or intellectual work.
- Evidence: Studies in neuroscience, psychology, and [sociology]({{ "/case-studies/society/sociology/" | relative_url }}) proved no significant cognitive differences, leading to gender equality movements.

### Free Markets Will Always Regulate Themselves *(Pre-2008 Economic Crisis)*

- Explanation: Before the 2008 financial crisis, many economists said deregulated markets were inherently stable.
- Evidence: The crisis demonstrated the dangers of unregulated financial speculation, prompting stronger regulations.

### The Mozart Effect *(1990s–2000s)*

- Explanation: Popular media and some researchers claimed listening to Mozart made babies smarter.
- Evidence: Later studies found no strong evidence that classical music significantly boosts intelligence.

### Corporal Punishment as the Best Discipline *(Ancient–20th Century)*

- Explanation: Beating [children]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#role-of-children) was seen as necessary for [obedience]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#obedience) and moral development ("Spare the rod, spoil the child") according to some [authoritative]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#authority), [traditional]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#tradition) or consensus explanation.
- Evidence: Psychological studies show that corporal punishment increases aggression and mental health issues, hopefully leading to alternative discipline methods.

### Homosexuality as a Mental Illness *(19th–20th Century)*

- Explanation: Authoritative explanations classified homosexuality as a mental [disorder]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#disorder) that was often "treated" with conversion or aversion [therapy]({{ "/guide/human-history/therapy-culture/" | relative_url }}).
- Evidence: Scientific and psychological research led to its removal from the DSM (Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders) in 1973, recognizing it as a [natural]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#natural) human (and animal) variation. However, there has always been a vast amount of historical evidence. And a quick look at our closest [evolutionary]({{ "/guide/human-history/" | relative_url }}) cousins, chimpanzees and bonobos, suggest that the [sexual]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#sexuality) behaviour of humans was similar for the vast majority of human history. (It was only removed from the ICD-10 in 1992)

### Left-Brained vs. Right-Brained Thinking *(Late 20th Century)*

- Explanation: Pop psychology explained that people were dominantly left-brained (logical) or right-brained (creative).
- Evidence: Modern neuroscience shows that both hemispheres work together in nearly all cognitive tasks.

### Innate Racial Intelligence Differences *(19th–20th Century)*

- Explanation: Many scientists and institutions once believed in biological racial [hierarchies]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#hierarchies), with some races being "naturally superior" in intelligence.
- Evidence: Studies in genetics, psychology, and sociology have shown no scientific basis for racial superiority in intelligence—intelligence differences are overwhelmingly shaped by environment, [education]({{ "/case-studies/society/social-structures/education/" | relative_url }}), and [opportunity]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#opportunity).

### Slavery as a Natural Institution *(Ancient–19th Century)*

- Explanation: [Slavery]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#slavery) was considered a natural and acceptable part of [human society]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#anthropology) in many cultures.
- Evidence: Scientific evidence from anthropology and genetics disprove racist justifications. Economic evidence shows free citizens are required as consumers for optimum growth. (Notwithstanding the moral, [intersubjective]({{ "/guide/first-principles/intersubjective/" | relative_url }}) arguments that most would agree with!)

### Mercantilism vs. Free Trade *(17th–19th Century)*

- Explanation: Nations believed that hoarding gold and restricting imports made them richer.
- Evidence: Adam Smith’s Wealth of Nations (1776) showed that free trade and competition drive economic growth more effectively.

### Earth Cannot Sustain a Large Population *(18th–20th Century)*

- Explanation: Thomas Malthus (1798) claimed population growth would outstrip food supply, leading to mass starvation. 
- Evidence: The Green Revolution (20th century) dramatically increased food production, delaying Malthusian fears.

### <u>Food & Nutrition</u>

### Scurvy and Fresh Food *(16th–18th Century)*

- Explanation: Scurvy (common among sailors) was seen as a natural consequence of long sea voyages.
- Evidence: James Lind (1747) conducted experiments showing that citrus fruits (vitamin C) prevent scurvy, leading to dietary changes.

### Raw Milk Is Always Healthier Than Pasteurized Milk *(19th Century)*

- Explanation: Many people explained that boiling or pasteurizing milk would remove its "natural health benefits".
- Evidence: Louis Pasteur’s work (1860s) showed that pasteurisation kills harmful bacteria, reducing tuberculosis and foodborne illness.

### Eggs and Cholesterol *(20th–21st Century)*

- Explanation: Eating eggs was said to significantly raise blood cholesterol and cause heart disease.
- Evidence: Modern research shows that dietary cholesterol has little direct effect on blood cholesterol levels, and eggs are a healthy food source.
