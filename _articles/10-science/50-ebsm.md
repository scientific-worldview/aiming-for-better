In short: make an observation or ask a question, devise a [hypothesis]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#hypothesis) that explains the observation or answers the question, make a prediction based on the [hypothesis]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#hypothesis), gather [evidence]({{ "/guide/first-principles/evidence/" | relative_url }}) (to [test]({{ "/guide/first-principles/testability/" | relative_url }}) the prediction), analyse the evidence, reach a conclusion: if the hypothesis is supported, give the results, otherwise modify the hypothesis or devise a new one. In summary:

1. Observations spark questions.
2. Hypotheses are formed (often based on existing knowledge).
3. Make a Prediction(s) based on the hypothesis.
4. Experiments or data collection provide evidence.
5. Theories are made or adjusted based on [empirical]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#empirical) results.
6. Scientific conclusions emerge as evidence accumulates.

Rather than try to elaborate each step, let’s give some concrete examples:

## Ulcers

- _Observation:_{: .bullet} *“My doctor says that stress, alcohol, spicy food, and/or lifestyle factors have caused my stomach ulcer. But other ailments such as ‘Athlete's Foot’ aren’t caused by these factors. Often other ailments are down to infection by fungi or bacteria.”*
- _Hypothesis:_{: .bullet} In the early 1980s, Barry Marshall and Robin Warren proposed that ulcers could be caused by a bacterium that could survive in the stomach’s acidic environment. Until then, most people started with the conclusion “no bacteria can live in hydrochloric acid, end of!”, and so instead resorted to “obvious”, [authoritative]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#authority) explanation, without actually knowing.
- _Prediction:_{: .bullet} *Antibiotics will cure gastritis if it is caused by bacteria.*
- _Experimentation & Evidence Collection:_{: .bullet} They found Helicobacter pylori in ulcer patients' stomachs. They treated ulcers with antibiotics, which successfully cured the condition—something impossible if stress was the cause. To support their hypothesis, Marshall even drank H. pylori, developed gastritis, and then cured himself with antibiotics.
- _Conclusion & Impact:_{: .bullet} Their work completely overturned medical [consensus]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#consensus). Ulcers are now treated with antibiotics, not just acid-reducing drugs. They won the 2005 Nobel prise in Medicine for this discovery.

## Semmelweis and Handwashing

- _Observation:_{: .bullet} In the 1840s, Ignaz Semmelweis noticed that women giving birth in hospitals had much higher death rates from "childbed fever" compared to those giving birth at home.
- _Hypothesis:_{: .bullet} He proposed that doctors were spreading infections because they performed autopsies before delivering babies.
- _Prediction:_{: .bullet} *Hand-washing will reduce such infection*
- _Experimentation & Evidence Collection:_{: .bullet} He made doctors wash their hands with a chlorine solution before delivering babies. Death rates dropped drastically from 18% to below 2%.
- _Conclusion & Impact:_{: .bullet} His findings were ignored because they challenged the self-important medical establishment of the time. (Tragically, Semmelweis suffered a nervous breakdown, was committed to an asylum and died, probably due to beatings by the guards - not the only example of such brutal [oppression]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#oppression) by authorities when their voice is challenged. Hence, we all *should* have a problem with authority). Later, germ [theory]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#theory) (Pasteur, Lister, Koch) confirmed he was correct, and hand hygiene is now a basic medical practice.

## Pasteur’s Germ Theory vs. Spontaneous Generation

- _Observation:_{: .bullet} Before the 19th century, people believed in spontaneous generation—the idea that living organisms (e.g., flies, bacteria) appeared out of nowhere from non-living matter.
- _Hypothesis:_{: .bullet} Louis Pasteur proposed that microorganisms came from the environment, not spontaneously.
- _Prediction:_{: .bullet} *If closed off (sealed) from the environment, no bacteria will grow.*
- _Experimentation & Evidence Collection:_{: .bullet} He used swan-necked flasks filled with broth: When left open, bacteria grew. When sealed or the neck prevented airborne contamination, no bacteria appeared. This disproved spontaneous generation and showed that microorganisms come from other microorganisms.
- _Conclusion & Impact:_{: .bullet} His work led to the germ theory of disease, revolutionizing medicine and leading to pasteurisation, vaccines, and aseptic techniques.

## The Development of Vaccines (*Smallpox, COVID-19, etc.*)

- _Observation:_{: .bullet} People who recovered from smallpox seemed immune to future infections.
- _Hypothesis:_{: .bullet} Edward Jenner (1796) hypothesized that exposure to cowpox could protect against smallpox.
- _Prediction:_{: .bullet} *Infecting a patient with cowpox will protect them against smallpox.*
- _Experimentation & Evidence Collection:_{: .bullet} He infected a boy with cowpox, then later exposed him to smallpox. The boy did not develop smallpox, confirming cross-immunity.
- _Conclusion & Impact:_{: .bullet} This was the foundation of modern vaccination. Smallpox became the first disease eradicated by vaccines (1980). The same evidence-based vaccine development process was used for polio, measles, and COVID-19 vaccines.

## Einstein’s Theory of General Relativity

- _Observation:_{: .bullet} Newtonian gravity cannot fully explain Mercury’s orbit and predicted that light should not bend around massive objects.
- _Hypothesis:_{: .bullet} Einstein conjectured 4-dimensional spacetime and proposed that mass warps this spacetime causing what we experience as the “force” of gravity.
- _Predictions:_{: .bullet}

1. *Light will bend around the Sun.*
2. *The orbit of Mercury will be precisely explained by relativity.*

- _Experimentation & Evidence Collection:_{: .bullet} In 1919, Eddington observed a solar eclipse and confirmed that starlight was bent exactly as Einstein predicted. Mercury’s orbit anomaly was perfectly explained by relativity.
- _Conclusion & Impact:_{: .bullet} Newtonian physics was replaced by general relativity for large-scale cosmic phenomena. GPS systems today correct for time dilation using Einstein’s equations.

## The Structure of DNA (*Watson, Crick, and Franklin, 1953*)

- _Observation:_{: .bullet} Scientists knew genetic material existed but didn’t know its structure.
- _Hypothesis:_{: .bullet} Watson & Crick hypothesized that DNA had a double-helix structure that allowed for replication and information storage.
- _Experimentation & Evidence Collection:_{: .bullet} Rosalind Franklin’s X-ray diffraction images (without her direct credit at the time) showed a helical pattern. Watson & Crick built models to fit the data.
- _Conclusion & Impact:_{: .bullet} DNA’s double-helix model explained how genetic information is stored and replicated. This led to modern genetics, biotechnology, and medicine.

## Discovery of the Higgs Boson (*2012, Large Hadron Collider*)

- _Observation:_{: .bullet} The Standard Model of Particle Physics predicted a Higgs boson that gives mass to particles, but it had never been observed.
- _Hypothesis:_{: .bullet} If the Higgs boson exists, it should appear under high-energy collisions in a particle accelerator.
- _Experimentation & Evidence Collection:_{: .bullet} Scientists at CERN’s Large Hadron Collider (LHC) smashed protons at nearly light speed. In 2012, they found Higgs boson decay patterns matching predictions.
- _Conclusion & Impact:_{: .bullet} This confirmed the mechanism that gives particles mass. The Standard Model remains intact, guiding future physics research.

## Plate Tectonics (*Wegener’s Continental Drift Hypothesis, 1912–1960s*)

- _Observation:_{: .bullet} Continents seemed to fit together (e.g., Africa & South America). Fossil evidence showed similar species on different continents.
- _Hypothesis:_{: .bullet} Alfred Wegener proposed that continents were once joined (Pangaea) and later drifted apart.
- _Prediction:_{: .bullet} *In human lifetime, rather than geological time, continental drift will be measured in very small distances that can be measured.*
- _Experimentation & Evidence Collection:_{: .bullet} Seafloor spreading (1960s) showed continents were moving. Earthquakes & volcanoes mapped out tectonic plate boundaries.
- _Conclusion & Impact:_{: .bullet} Plate tectonics became a fundamental theory in geology. It explained earthquakes, volcanoes, and mountain formation.
