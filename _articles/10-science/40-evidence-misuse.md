**Cherry-Picking** – Selecting only the data that supports a particular argument while ignoring contradictory [evidence]({{ "/guide/first-principles/evidence/" | relative_url }}).

- _Example:_{: .bullet} *Alternative medicine advocates citing one small study while ignoring large-scale clinical trials that disprove their claims.*

**Misuse of** [Correlation and Causation]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#correlation) – Presenting a correlation between two variables as if one directly causes the other.

- *Example:** *Claiming that increased ice cream sales cause higher drowning rates, when both are actually influenced by hot weather.*

**Anecdotal Evidence Over Data** – Relying on personal stories or isolated cases rather than large-scale, peer-reviewed studies.

- _Example:_{: .bullet} *Anti-vaccine activists using one person’s negative experience to discredit robust scientific studies on vaccine safety.*

**Misrepresentation of Scientific Uncertainty** – Portraying normal scientific debate as evidence that a [theory]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#theory) is fundamentally flawed.

- _Example:_{: .bullet} *Suggesting that debates about dietary guidelines mean that [nutrition]({{ "/case-studies/health/diet/" | relative_url }}) science is unreliable, rather than evolving with new evidence.*

**Non-Falsifiable Claims** – Making assertions that cannot be [tested]({{ "/guide/first-principles/testability/" | relative_url }}) or disproven, which makes them unscientific.

- _Example:_{: .bullet} *Homeopathy proponents arguing that treatments work in ways science “hasn’t discovered yet” or psychic mediums claiming their abilities cannot be tested because they only work under special conditions.*

**Straw Man Arguments Against Science** – Distorting scientific positions to make them easier to attack.

- _Example:_{: .bullet} *Misrepresenting [evolution]({{ "/guide/human-history/evolution/" | relative_url }}) as claiming “humans descended from monkeys” rather than explaining common ancestry or claiming that “life randomly appeared” rather than describing [natural selection]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#natural-selection).*

**Ignoring the Base Rate (Base Rate Fallacy)** – Focusing on specific cases without considering the overall likelihood of an event.

- _Example:_{: .bullet} *Fearing plane crashes due to media coverage while ignoring that air travel is statistically much safer than driving.*

**Appeal to [Authority]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#authority) Without Evidence** – Using the [status]({{ "/case-studies/society/sociology/status/" | relative_url }}) of an individual or institution as proof, rather than the strength of their evidence.

- _Example:_{: .bullet} *A celebrity endorsing an alternative medicine product without scientific backing.*

**False Balance in Media Reporting** – Giving equal weight to a fringe viewpoint and a well-established scientific consensus.

- _Example:_{: .bullet} *Debates between a climate scientist and a climate change denier, suggesting both views are equally credible.*

[Suppression]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#suppression) of Unfavourable Evidence** – Hiding, downplaying, or ignoring data that contradicts a preferred position.

- _Example:_{: .bullet} *Pharmaceutical companies failing to publish negative trial results for a new drug.*

**Exaggeration or Overgeneralisation** – Taking preliminary or small-scale studies and presenting them as definitive conclusions.

- _Example:_{: .bullet} *Headlines proclaiming “Scientists discover a cure for cancer” based on a single animal study.*

**Shifting Goalposts** – Changing the standards of proof required when faced with contradictory evidence.

- _Example:_{: .bullet} *A conspiracy theorist demanding evidence, but then dismissing it as “fake” or “manufactured” when presented with it.*

**False Equivalence** – Treating two different sources of information as if they are equally valid.

- _Example:_{: .bullet} *Comparing peer-reviewed scientific research with an unverified blog post.*

[Scaremongering and Emotional Manipulation]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#manipulation) – Using [fear]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#fear)-based rhetoric instead of [objective]({{ "/guide/first-principles/intersubjective/" | relative_url }}) evidence.

- _Example:_{: .bullet} *“This chemical is found in rat poison!” without explaining that dosage and context matter.*

**Misuse of [Statistics]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#statistics) – Using misleading graphs, small sample sizes, or selective statistics to create a false impression.

- _Example:_{: .bullet} *Presenting a percentage increase (e.g., “100% increase in cases!”) without showing the absolute numbers (e.g., from 1 to 2 cases).*

**Over-Reliance on Peer Review Without Scrutiny** – Assuming that just because a study is published, it must be high quality.

- _Example:_{: .bullet} *Low-quality or fraudulent studies passing through poorly [regulated]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#regulation) journals and later being cited as "scientific proof".*

**Making Up / Manipulating / Replacing “Evidence”** – Creating or manipulating data in order to support a desired outcome or create the desired results.

- _Example:_{: .bullet} *Offering a highly literate person a questionnaire, but not allowing them to fill it out themselves, perhaps to ensure the client’s “answers” show overall “improvement”. Shocking.*
