### Overview

In his influential 1959 Rede Lecture, **"The Two Cultures and the Scientific Revolution"**, *C.P. Snow* argued that Western intellectual life had become divided into two distinct and largely isolated "cultures":

- _The Literary Intellectuals (or "Humanists"):_{: .bullet} These were primarily scholars and intellectuals in the humanities – literature, history, philosophy, languages, and the arts. Snow characterized them as often being skeptical of science, viewing it as a dehumanizing force, and not understanding even the basic [scientific concepts]({{ "/guide/first-principles/scientific-method/" | relative_url }}). Snow's "literary intellectuals" often align with the [true believers]({{ "/guide/obstacles/true-believers/" | relative_url }}) of various [intersubjective]({{ "/guide/first-principles/intersubjective/" | relative_url }}) realities that we discussed before. Many may distrust science because they see it as a challenge to their deeply held [beliefs]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#beliefs), morals, or cultural [norms]({{ "/case-studies/society/sociology/norms/" | relative_url }}).

- _The Scientists:_{: .bullet} These were the researchers and practitioners of the natural sciences. Snow portrayed them as often being ignorant of the humanities, viewing it as a less serious and less rigorous pursuit, and also not able to communicate well with non-scientists. They tend to value objective reality and the scientific method, sometimes neglecting the subjective worth of other domains of intellectual thought. This could cause them to underestimate the importance of intersubjective systems and the ways in which human needs and [values]({{ "/case-studies/society/sociology/values/" | relative_url }}) are intertwined with them.

---

#### A Lack of Communication

Snow's central concern was that these two cultures were not only separate but were actively failing to communicate with each other. He believed this lack of communication created significant problems:

- _Impediment to Progress:_{: .bullet} He argued that a lack of understanding between [the two cultures]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#the-two-cultures) hindered scientific progress and the application of scientific knowledge to solve societal problems. He felt that humanists often failed to understand the potential benefits of science and technology.
- _Missed Opportunities:_{: .bullet} Snow thought that because humanists were frequently prominent in [society]({{ "/case-studies/society/social-structures/" | relative_url }}) (and often running governments) they lacked the understanding to embrace science. Likewise, many scientists were not concerned with social policy.
- _Widening Gap:_{: .bullet} He felt that this lack of communication was leading to a widening gap between the two cultures, which could have dire consequences for society.

#### The Need for a Third Culture

To bridge this divide, Snow advocated for a "third culture" that would bring together scientists and humanists. He believed this third culture would be crucial for:

- _Solving Global Problems:_{: .bullet} Snow thought that understanding the scientific approach was crucial to solve global challenges such as [poverty]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#poverty) and environmental issues.
- _Promoting a More Integrated Understanding:_{: .bullet} The goal was to foster a more holistic understanding of the world, integrating both scientific and humanistic perspectives.

#### Bridging the Gap Through Critical Thinking

- [Critical thinking]({{ "/guide/thinking-for-yourself/" | relative_url }}) is a vital bridge between these two cultures. Both scientists and humanists can benefit from developing their ability to analyse information, identify [bias]({{ "/guide/thinking-for-yourself/cognitive-biases/" | relative_url }}), and evaluate [evidence]({{ "/guide/first-principles/evidence/" | relative_url }}).
- Humanists can use critical thinking to better understand the scientific process and recognise the potential benefits of science. Scientists can use critical thinking to understand the social and ethical implications of their work.

#### The Role of Education

- Snow's call for a "third culture" resonates with a call for [education]({{ "/case-studies/society/social-structures/education/" | relative_url }}) systems that promote critical thinking, scientific literacy, and a holistic understanding of the world.
- Education should aim to produce individuals who can move seamlessly between scientific and humanistic modes of thought, understanding both the rigor of objective analysis and the richness of human experience.

#### Improving Intersubjective Systems

- The failure of communication between the two cultures can perpetuate harmful intersubjective systems. A better understanding of scientific [principles]({{ "/guide/first-principles/" | relative_url }}) by the population as a whole can help to challenge and improve these systems by encouraging evidence-based policies and [rational]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#rational) decision-making.

#### Avoiding Polarisation

- Snow's warnings about the dangers of cultural division seem particularly relevant in today's world with a Damp Old Runt back in the White House, where we see increased polarisation and a lack of common ground.
- Promoting cross-cultural understanding and dialogue is more important than ever to avoid a widening chasm that might lead to social disintegration.
