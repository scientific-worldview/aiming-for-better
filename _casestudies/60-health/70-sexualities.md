### Sexual In-Groups vs. Out-Groups: The Morality of Desire

[Sex]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#sexuality). It's a fundamental _biological drive_{: .bold}, deeply wired into our mammal and primate nature. For millions of years, the pursuit of pleasure and reproduction has been a [natural]({{ "/guide/human-history/natural-vs-normal/" | relative_url }}), unashamed part of *animal existence*. Yet, in recent, modern [human societies]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#anthropology), sex is often the most heavily controlled, moralized, and tribalized aspect of our lives. This case study will explore the profound and often _destructive conflict_{: .warn} that happens when our ancient, biological sexual drives clash with complex, often arbitrary, moral [rules]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#rules) that define who is sexually "acceptable" and who is not.

![Shame]({{ "/assets/images/casestudies/health/shame.webp" | relative_url }})

From historical persecutions to subtle everyday judgments, societies consistently create rigid _sexual hierarchies_{: .bold}. The assumed "heterosexual [in-group]({{ "/case-studies/us-vs-them/" | relative_url }})" often looks down on others; the "chaste" tribe demonizes the "degenerate"; the "red-blooded" might dismiss the "frigid". Since the [Agricultural Transition]({{ "/guide/human-history/agricultural-transition/" | relative_url }}), and particularly the rise of [Civilisations]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#civilisations), we have invented [sin]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#sin) and law to regulate our [natural]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#natural) sexual drives. _This creates intense social pressure to conform, pushing individuals to suppress their natural inclinations just to belong._{: .mark} However, such [suppression]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#suppression) rarely works. Instead, it breeds *hypocrisy* and *deep psychological pain*, and paradoxically, can lead to societies filled with both real and imagined "perverts" and sexual problems. This case study will use our [scientific approach]({{ "/guide/first-principles/scientific-method/" | relative_url }}) to show how rigid sexual [beliefs]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#beliefs) cause _individual suffering_{: .warn}, societal [dysfunction]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#dysfunction), and ultimately work against our overall [well-being]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#wellbeing).

### Historical Perspectives and Background Information

For most of our [evolutionary history]({{ "/guide/human-history/" | relative_url }}), sexual behaviour was guided by biological needs, social signals, and strategies for successful reproduction and group bonding. While different _mating systems_{: .bold} existed (like [monogamy]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#monogamy) or polygamy), they were largely shaped by environment and social conditions, not by *abstract moral codes*.

The conflict between natural sexuality and social rules became much stronger after the shift to agriculture and settled societies, especially with the rise of organized religions and powerful states. As shared cultural [stories]({{ "/case-studies/narratives/" | relative_url }}) became dominant, sexuality became a **prime target for control**. Moral rules emerged, often tied to ideas of "purity," "sin," "family honor," or the fear of "illegitimate" children. These became powerful, entrenched ways of thinking that shaped laws and social [norms]({{ "/case-studies/society/sociology/norms/" | relative_url }}) for centuries.

- _Ancient Civilizations:_{: .bullet} Some ancient cultures (like classical Greece and Rome) had more varied understandings of sexuality (e.g., bisexuality wasn't universally condemned in the same way as in later Abrahamic traditions). Others (like ancient Israel) imposed strict rules, particularly on women.

- _Abrahamic Religions:_{: .bullet} Christianity, Islam, and Judaism (especially in their more strict interpretations) developed detailed and often _highly restrictive_{: .warn} sexual moral codes. These cultural stories defined marriage as the only legitimate place for sex, condemned non-procreative acts, and often [demonized]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#demonisation) premarital or extramarital sex, as well as same-sex relationships. These powerful beliefs shaped laws and social norms for centuries.

- _The Victorian Era:_{: .bullet} This period in Western history is a prime example of _extreme sexual suppression_{: .warn}. A rigid ideal of "chastity" (especially for women) was enforced, while any deviation was branded "degenerate". This led to widespread hypocrisy, psychological repression, and a [flourishing]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#human-flourishing) underground of suppressed desires.

- _20th Century & Beyond:_{: .bullet} The "sexual revolution" challenged many of these norms, but new forms of sexual [tribalism]({{ "/case-studies/politics/political-dynamics/tribalism/" | relative_url }}) and repression emerged, often reacting against liberation or defining new "acceptable" versus "unacceptable" behaviours. The ongoing fight for LGBTQ+ [rights]({{ "/case-studies/politics/governance/rights/" | relative_url }}) globally is a _modern battle_{: .bold} against deeply embedded sexual "in-groups".

This history shows a consistent pattern: applying complex, often arbitrary, social rules to a fundamental biological drive. This creates artificial "in-groups" and "out-groups" that cause _immense suffering_{: .warn}, rather than promoting [objective]({{ "/guide/first-principles/intersubjective/" | relative_url }}) well-being.

### Step 1: Wipe the Slate Clean


To look at sexual "in-groups" and "out-groups" objectively, we must first clear our minds of *deeply ingrained*, emotionally charged beliefs:

- _The "Natural Moral Order" Myth:_{: .bullet} The widespread, yet unscientific, belief that a specific set of sexual behaviours (e.g., heterosexual, monogamous, procreative) represents a "natural moral order" given by a divine power or inherently superior. This belief ignores the vast biological [diversity]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#diversity) of sexual expression and the fact that sexual norms vary greatly across cultures.

- _The "Purity vs. Degeneracy" Divide:_{: .bullet} The powerful idea that divides sexual behaviours into "pure" (virtuous, acceptable) and "degenerate" (sinful, harmful, [deviant]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#deviant)). This moral framework often has no basis in objective harm, but serves to control [behaviour]({{ "/guide/human-history/psychology/" | relative_url }}) and reinforce social hierarchies.

- _The "Perfect Conformity" Illusion:_{: .bullet} The unrealistic [expectation]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#tolerance) that individuals can, or should, perfectly match their complex sexual desires to arbitrary social norms without psychological or societal consequences. This ignores the natural variety of human sexuality.

The problem, then, is this: How do societies, driven by these _unscientific beliefs_{: .bold}, create rigid sexual "in-groups" and "out-groups" that lead to widespread individual suppression, psychological distress, and societal problems, instead of fostering healthy, _open communication_{: .cool} and sexual expression that supports overall well-being?

- _The Goal of This Study:_{: .bullet}

  - To use our [scientific worldview]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#scientific-worldview) to critically evaluate how sexual "in-groups" and "out-groups" have been constructed throughout history and today.

  - To show the conflict between our [evolved]({{ "/guide/human-history/evolution/" | relative_url }}) sexual [biology]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#biology) and strict moral codes about sex.

  - To understand how cultural ideas of "purity" and "degeneracy" cause deep individual harm and societal problems.

  - To analyze the objective consequences of sexual suppression, [shaming]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#shame), and persecution on individual well-being and the actual number of sexual offenses.

  - To challenge the "Natural Moral Order" [myth]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#myth) and the "Purity vs. Degeneracy" divide with rigorous, evidence-based critique.

### Step 2: Applying First Principles


#### Testability


The claims and effects of sexual "in-group" and "out-group" dynamics can be [tested]({{ "/guide/first-principles/testability/" | relative_url }}), and they often show significant problems:

- _Biological Diversity of Sexuality:_{: .bullet} Is there clear scientific evidence (from biology, neuroscience, anthropology) that human sexuality is only heterosexual, monogamous, or for reproduction? Or does research show a natural range of desires and expressions across individuals and species? _The evidence overwhelmingly points to natural diversity._{: .mark}

- _Psychological Impact of Suppression:_{: .bullet} What are the measurable _psychological effects_{: .bold} (like anxiety, depression, shame, self-hatred, sexual problems, increased risk of unhealthy behaviours) on individuals forced to suppress their natural sexual inclinations to fit into a group's norm? Clinical [psychology]({{ "/guide/human-history/psychology/" | relative_url }}) and human sexuality research provide a lot of clear evidence of harm.

- _Connection to Sexual Offending:_{: .bullet} Is there a clear connection between societies with high levels of sexual repression, shaming, and openly labeling groups as "degenerate," and the actual number of non-consensual sexual offenses (like child sexual abuse, rape)? While complex, studies often suggest that cultures of silence, shame, and suppression can ironically create environments where abuse thrives because there's no open, healthy discussion about sex.

- _Societal Well-being:_{: .bullet} Do societies with very strict sexual moral codes objectively show higher or lower rates of overall well-being, mental health, and social cohesion compared to those with more open, consensual, and inclusive approaches to sexuality? Data often indicates that greater sexual [freedom]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#autonomy) (within _consensual boundaries_{: .bold}) is linked to better well-being.


#### Objective vs. Intersubjective


- _Objective Facts:_{: .bullet} The biological ways sexual arousal and pleasure work, the statistical commonness of different sexual orientations and practices, documented cases of violence and persecution against sexual "out-groups," the psychological and physical stress responses to sexual suppression, and laws that discriminate based on sex are all _measurable facts_{: .bold}.

- _Cultural Beliefs and Feelings:_{: .bullet} The belief that certain sexual acts are "sinful," the feeling of disgust towards sex that isn't considered "normal," the story that "chastity" is better than "degeneracy," the social pressure to marry and have children, and the feeling of moral superiority of one sexual "in-group" over another are all powerful cultural beliefs and feelings. The big problem here is that these cultural moral beliefs are consistently given more importance than the objective [facts]({{ "/guide/first-principles/correct-answers/" | relative_url }}) of human biology and the clear harm caused by enforcing them.

#### Only EVIDENCE Matters

When looking at sexual "in-groups" and "out-groups," the [principle]({{ "/guide/first-principles/" | relative_url }}) of "Only Evidence Matters" means we must ask:

- What clear data shows that specific sexual behaviours (beyond non-consensual acts or causing harm to others) are inherently "degenerate" or harmful, rather than just being different from a set cultural norm?

- What measurable positive results (like fewer sexual offenses, better mental health, stronger social bonds, happier individuals) objectively come from societies enforcing strict sexual "in-group" conformity and demonizing "out-groups," compared to societies that prioritize sexual _autonomy_{: .cool} and _inclusivity_{: .cool}?

- What clear evidence explains the conflict between our deeply wired biological drives for pleasure and diverse sexual expression, and the often arbitrary moral and legal rules placed on them?

- What clear data shows the long-term societal impact of sexual shame, [secrecy]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#secrecy), and making non-harmful sexual expression illegal on social trust, mental health, and the overall number of sexual problems?

- Do cultural stories about "purity" and "immorality" objectively match a nuanced, evidence-based understanding of human psychology and sexual behaviour? Or do they instead promote *harmful conflicts* and lead to punishing behaviours simply because they are deemed "unacceptable"?

### Step 3: Identify the Objective Consequences – The Suppression Paradox & Pathological Cycle


Enforcing rigid sexual "in-groups" and demonizing "out-groups," driven by unscientific beliefs, creates several **devastating real-world consequences** and deeply ingrained problems for individual well-being and _societal health_{: .bold}:

- _The "Suppression Paradox":_{: .bullet} Forcing individuals to suppress natural sexual desires (like same-sex attraction or specific consensual fetishes) doesn't make those desires disappear. Instead, it traps them in a cycle where they suffer deep psychological pain (anxiety, depression, shame, self-hatred), live hypocritical lives, and keep secrets. This _internal conflict_{: .bold} severely harms mental health and makes *genuine, healthy intimacy* impossible.

- _Projecting Problems Onto Others:_{: .bullet} When natural sexual diversity is labeled "degenerate," societies project their fears and disgust onto sexual "out-groups." This often leads to discrimination, violence, and persecution against these groups. This projection diverts attention from society's own discomfort with sex, creating [scapegoats]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#scapegoats).

- _Enabling Abuse:_{: .bullet} _Cultures of sexual shame and secrecy, where open discussion of sex is forbidden, unintentionally make abuse easier._{: .mark} This clearly makes it harder for victims of sexual abuse to speak out, for [society]({{ "/case-studies/society/social-structures/" | relative_url }}) to have healthy conversations about consent and boundaries, and for educators to provide proper sex education. Ironically, this contributes to environments where sexual offences can thrive due to a lack of openness and understanding.

- _Societal Hypocrisy:_{: .bullet} Strict sexual rules that clash with biological reality often lead to widespread hypocrisy. Public condemnation of "deviant" behaviours frequently happens alongside private indulgence, creating a deep sense of moral dishonesty, eroding trust, and making it easy for blackmail and [exploitation]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#exploitation) to occur. This gap between stated [values]({{ "/case-studies/society/sociology/values/" | relative_url }}) and actual behaviour breeds cynicism.

- _Extreme Alienation:_{: .bullet} For individuals completely rejected and demonized by sexual "in-groups"—facing social exclusion, legal punishment, or violence because of their sexual identity—the result can be _profound alienation_{: .warn}. Stripped of belonging and dignity, the psychological toll can be immense, leading to despair, self-destructive behaviours, or, in extreme cases, a violent lashing out against a society that has completely rejected them.

The clear consequence of these problems is a society filled with sexual anxiety, unfulfilled desires, and a tragic waste of human potential. Instead of fostering healthy, consensual intimacy, this kind of tribalism often creates a world full of both real sexual harm (often hidden by shame) and imagined "perversion," where natural diversity is treated as a sickness.

### Step 4: Reflect on Human History – The Need for Consensual Sexual Autonomy

Our study of "Sexual In-Groups vs. Out-Groups" shows a clear and urgent conflict: the clash between our evolved biological drives for diverse sexual expression and the restrictive, cultural moral rules imposed by human societies. History, from the relatively open sexual norms of some ancient cultures to the rigid moral codes of others and the widespread sexual repression of more recent times, consistently reveals this tension. The urgent need now is to consciously design social and legal systems that match our scientific understanding of human sexuality, promoting objective well-being and reducing harm.

- _Conflict between Natural Diversity and Moral Sameness:_{: .bullet} Our sexual biology shows a natural range of human sexual desire and orientation. Strict sexual "in-groups," by trying to enforce a single moral standard, create a deep conflict that labels natural variation as a problem, leading to widespread suffering.

- _Conflict between Pleasure and Shame:_{: .bullet} Our mammal and primate heritage links sex to pleasure, bonding, and social connection. Imposing cultural stories of "sin" and "shame" on consensual sexual expression creates a conflict that disconnects individuals from their own bodies and authentic desires, hindering healthy emotional and social development.

- _Conflict between Ancient Instinct and Artificial Rules:_{: .bullet} The strength of our sexual drive shows its importance in evolution. Overly strict, top-down rules, often from outside authorities, create a conflict that is inherently hard to maintain without significant force, often leading to hidden activities and hypocrisy.

- _Conflict between Secrecy and Safety:_{: .bullet} Cultures of sexual secrecy and a lack of open, honest [education]({{ "/case-studies/society/social-structures/education/" | relative_url }}), driven by the [fear]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#fear) of labeling "out-groups," create a deep conflict with the need for safety and [protection]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#protection-and-protectors). This makes it harder to discuss consent, identify abuse, and seek help, unintentionally making societies less safe.

The need for sexual _autonomy_{: .cool} shows that to improve human well-being in the 21st century, we need a _scientific understanding_{: .neon} of sexuality that goes beyond cultural [dogma]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#dogma). It requires a fundamental shift from controlling desire to promoting consent, open communication, and respect for individual choice, within clear boundaries of not causing harm. This involves actively creating environments where diverse sexual expressions are understood and accepted, guiding humanity towards a future of greater intimacy, psychological health, and genuine societal flourishing.

## Conclusion: Engineering Consent – Embracing Diversity, Rejecting Dogma

_If we had to get it right, as if building a truly thriving human society depended on it—because it does—what would our conclusion be?_{: .subtle}

"Sexual In-Groups vs. Out-Groups: The Morality of Desire" reveals that cultural ideas of a "Natural Moral Order" and "Purity vs. Degeneracy" create deep problems like the **"Suppression Paradox"**, projecting problems onto others, and a critical conflict with our species' biological diversity and need for authentic expression.

- _Sexual Diversity is Natural:_{: .bullet} _Biology, not cultural rules, defines the range of human sexuality._{: .mark}

- _Suppression Causes Harm:_{: .bullet} Shaming and persecution cause deep individual and societal damage.

- _Consent is the Only Moral Compass:_{: .bullet} Non-harmful, consensual expression is the foundation of ethical sexuality.

A _scientific approach_{: .neon} to sexuality demands a proactive, [evidence-based]({{ "/guide/first-principles/evidence/" | relative_url }}) strategy to build consent and embrace diversity, rejecting dogma:

- _Remove Discriminatory Laws & Social Rules:_{: .bullet} Abolish all laws that make consensual sexual acts between people illegal or discriminate against individuals based on sexual orientation or gender identity. Actively challenge cultural stories that _demonize_{: .warn} non-harmful sexual expressions.

- _Prioritize Comprehensive, Evidence-Based Sex Education:_{: .bullet} Implement education programs from an early age that are based on biology, consent, healthy relationships, and respect for diversity, rather than [fear]({{ "/guide/obstacles/fear-mongering/" | relative_url }}) or moralistic rules. This is crucial for preventing abuse and promoting well-being.

- _Foster Cultures of Open Discussion & Acceptance:_{: .bullet} Create societal environments where sexuality can be discussed openly, honestly, and without shame, allowing individuals to explore their identities and desires in a healthy, informed manner.

- _Focus on Consent and Not Causing Harm:_{: .bullet} Reframe the _ethical core_{: .bold} of sexuality around the principles of active consent, mutual respect, and not causing harm to others. This clear framework provides a universal, scientifically sound standard.

- _Challenge "Purity" & "Degeneracy" Beliefs:_{: .bullet} Actively break down the idea of **"Purity vs. Degeneracy"**, exposing its unscientific foundations and its role in causing shame, hypocrisy, and violence.

- _Support Mental Health & Well-being:_{: .bullet} Provide strong mental health services that support diverse sexual identities and help individuals heal from the pain of sexual suppression and persecution.

By _scientifically understanding_{: .neon} and proactively creating societal approaches to sexuality that align with our [mammal]({{ "/case-studies/who-we-are/mammalian-care-blueprint/" | relative_url }}) and [primate]({{ "/case-studies/who-we-are/primate-blueprint/" | relative_url }}) nature, we can move beyond the *devastating problems* of _suppression_{: .warn} and projecting our issues onto others. We can intentionally build a future where desire is understood, consent is most important, and every individual can express their authentic, non-harmful sexuality without fear, shame, or _persecution_{: .warn}, guiding humanity towards a future of greater intimacy, [psychological health]({{ "/case-studies/health/mental-health/" | relative_url }}), and genuine _flourishing_{: .cool}.

_When dogmatic morality suppresses biology, society trades authentic intimacy for systemic dysfunction._{: .mark}
