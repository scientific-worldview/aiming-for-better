### Overview: An Evolutionary Failure Disguised as Protection

For most of [human history]({{ "/guide/human-history/" | relative_url }}), a child who lost their [parents]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#parental-ownership) or could not safely remain with them *did not* enter a *formal, state-run system*. Instead, they were absorbed into an extended kin network or the wider community. This pattern of shared caregiving — known as [alloparenting]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#alloparenting) — is deeply rooted in human [evolution]({{ "/guide/human-history/evolution/" | relative_url }}) and closely tied to early emotional development (see [Childhood & Early Development]({{ "/case-studies/society/social-structures/early-development/" | relative_url }})).

In modern developed societies, however, [children]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#role-of-children) deemed **"at risk"** are typically placed into a _formal care system_{: .bold} involving foster placements, residential homes, and rotating professional oversight. This *very recent* approach represents a sharp departure from the social environment humans evolved within for *millions of years*. The system is usually justified as a **"benevolent intervention"** acting in the **"best interests of the child"**, a [framing]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#framing) that has become so dominant it *discourages serious scrutiny*. This creates a powerful [Lock-In]({{ "/case-studies/locked-in/" | relative_url }}) where intentions are assumed to guarantee good outcomes, even when *evidence suggests otherwise*.

![The Care System]({{ "/assets/images/casestudies/society/care.webp" | relative_url }})

From a [scientific worldview]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#scientific-worldview), the modern care system — despite often sincere intentions — conflicts directly with fundamental [mammalian]({{ "/case-studies/who-we-are/mammalian-care-blueprint/" | relative_url }}) and [primate]({{ "/case-studies/who-we-are/primate-blueprint/" | relative_url }}) **needs**: stable *attachment*, unconditional *care*, and a secure sense of _belonging_{: .cool}. Instead of integrating vulnerable children into enduring social bonds, the system frequently exposes them to repeated _disruptions_{: .warn}, impersonal decision-making, and a near-total *lack of control over their own lives*. Children are **"legally protected"**, yet **practically powerless** — *managed rather than meaningfully cared for*.

This page argues that the consistent (often horrifying) _failures_{: .warn} of the care system—whether in Romania, the UK or elsewhere—*are not accidental*. They arise because the system is _structurally misaligned_{: .warn} with human developmental [biology]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#biology). When examined honestly, outcomes for children who experience care — including elevated rates of mental illness, addiction, homelessness, and [criminal justice]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#justice-and-injustice) involvement — are not anomalies but _predictable consequences_{: .bold} of a system *that substitutes procedures for relationships*.

### Historical Perspective: How Humans Actually Cared for Children

Understanding why *the modern care system* _fails_{: .warn} requires looking at how vulnerable children were historically supported. _It's that simple_{: .mark}:

#### Ancestral Foraging Societies

In hunter-gatherer [groups]({{ "/case-studies/society/social-structures/the-clan/" | relative_url }}), children were a _shared responsibility_{: .cool}. If parents died, became ill, or were unable to cope, care was immediately taken up by grandparents, aunts, uncles, older siblings, or trusted members of the group. No child was _raised by strangers for payment_{: .mark}, and no child was _isolated from a social identity_{: .mark}. This [abuse]({{ "/guide/human-history/altruism-and-cruelty/" | relative_url }}#abuse-a-vague-word-for-real-harm) never existed.

This ensured:

- _Multiple_{: .bullet} *stable attachment figures*  
- _Continuous_{: .bullet} *social belonging*  
- _Immediate_{: .bullet} *intervention when harm occurred*  

These arrangements aligned with [evolved human psychology]({{ "/guide/human-history/psychology/" | relative_url }}) and reinforced [group cohesion]({{ "/case-studies/society/social-structures/community-and-anonymity/" | relative_url }}).

#### Agricultural and Early Civilisations

As populations grew and societies stratified, *extended family support weakened*. Orphaned or abandoned children became more visible, and early _institutional responses_{: .bold} emerged, often run by [religious]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#religion) organisations. *These prioritised survival over emotional development*.

Children increasingly came to be viewed as economic assets, labour, or dependents rather than autonomous developing individuals — *a shift that echoes today’s bureaucratic mindset*.

#### Industrialisation

[Urbanisation]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#urbanisation), [poverty]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#poverty), and disease *overwhelmed informal support networks*. Large orphanages and workhouses became common. These institutions were *efficient at containment* but _disastrous_{: .warn} for emotional development, producing *generations marked by neglect and trauma*.

#### Modern Era

Recognition of institutional harm led to a move toward foster care. While an **"improvement in theory"**, this approach remained *state-managed, temporary, and unstable*. Professionalisation introduced safeguards but also *bureaucracy*, _risk aversion_{: .warn}, and _vested interests_{: .bold} — classic **Lock-Ins** *where processes matter more than outcomes*.

> *Despite decades of reform, the core structure remains unchanged, and **outcomes remain poor***.

### Step 1: Wipe the Slate Clean

We must abandon the assumption that the existing care system is the best or only solution. A [scientific approach]({{ "/guide/first-principles/scientific-method/" | relative_url }}) begins by asking a very simple question:

_What do children actually need to develop into healthy adults?_{: .subtle}

[Evidence]({{ "/guide/first-principles/evidence/" | relative_url }}) consistently points to:

- **Emotional bonds**  
- A clear *sense of belonging*  
- *Predictable caregiving*  
- *Gradually increasing agency* (see [Emancipation]({{ "/case-studies/society/social-structures/emancipation/" | relative_url }}))

The care system routinely _fails_{: .warn} on all four.

Prevailing Narratives — that professional oversight compensates for instability, or that procedural compliance equals [protection]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#child-protection) — prevent honest evaluation. These narratives form a self-reinforcing _Lock-In_{: .neon} that prioritises system legitimacy over child outcomes.

---

### Step 2: Applying First Principles

#### Testability

Claims about the effectiveness of the care system are empirically [testable]({{ "/guide/first-principles/testability/" | relative_url }}).

- _Longitudinal studies_{: .bullet} consistently show *worse life outcomes* for care-experienced individuals compared to peers.
- _Neuroscience_{: .bullet} demonstrates that chronic instability and disrupted attachment alter *stress regulation and emotional development*.
- _Comparative studies_{: .bullet} show better outcomes where _kinship care_{: .cool} and intensive family support are prioritised.
- _Education data_{: .bullet} reveals *persistent underachievement* linked to placement instability.

#### Objective vs. Intersubjective

- _Objective facts:_{: .bullet} elevated rates of *mental illness, substance misuse, homelessness, incarceration*.
- _Intersubjective narratives:_{: .bullet} *"rescue"*, *"safeguarding"*, ["best interests"]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#agendas).

### Only EVIDENCE matters

Where these conflict, evidence must prevail (see [Reality vs. Narrative]({{ "/case-studies/narratives/" | relative_url }})).

### Step 3: Objective Consequences

When a system repeatedly removes children from stable relationships and replaces them with temporary placements and rotating professionals, predictable outcomes follow:

- _Attachment disruption:_{: .bullet} *Difficulty forming trusting relationships* later in life.
- _Identity confusion:_{: .bullet} *Weak sense of self* and belonging.
- _Educational failure:_{: .bullet} Interrupted schooling and *low expectations*.
- _Reduced agency:_{: .bullet} Decisions made *about* children rather than *with* them (see [Emancipation]({{ "/case-studies/society/social-structures/emancipation/" | relative_url }})).
- _Social isolation:_{: .bullet} Loss of long-term networks that support adulthood (see [Community & Anonymity]({{ "/case-studies/society/social-structures/community-and-anonymity/" | relative_url }})).

These outcomes are not incidental — *they are structural*.

---

### Step 4: Human History and the Cost of Ignoring It

The care system reflects a broader pattern seen throughout modern society: *replacing* _evolved social solutions_{: .cool} with *administrative ones*. Like many [institutional]({{ "/case-studies/locked-in/institutional-control/" | relative_url }}) responses, it *prioritises control* and *risk management* over **human connection**.

This mirrors _failures_{: .warn} seen elsewhere — in justice, [education]({{ "/case-studies/society/social-structures/education/" | relative_url }}), and welfare — where systems persist despite evidence because dismantling them threatens **entrenched interests** (see [Why Bad Ideas Don’t Die]({{ "/case-studies/locked-in/" | relative_url }})).

## Conclusion: Engineering Belonging, Not Managing Damage

If we approached child welfare as an engineering problem — optimising for long-term human [flourishing]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#human-flourishing) — _the current care system would fail immediately._{: .mark}

The evidence supports a different approach:

- *Prevent removal* wherever safely possible
- Prioritise _kinship care_{: .cool} by default
- Ensure *permanence*, not rotation
- Restore children’s *voices and agency*
- Build _community-embedded support_{: .cool} instead of isolated institutions

Children do not need better paperwork, better assessments, or better compliance metrics.

_They need what humans have always needed: stable love, lasting bonds, and a place where they truly belong._{: .subtle}

A system that cannot provide this — and consistently produces harm — is not broken. **It is fundamentally** _misdesigned_{: .warn}.

From a scientific and evolutionary perspective, the care system is not merely flawed. It is *misaligned with* [human nature]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#human-nature) itself. And systems that conflict with human biology eventually **exact a terrible price** — paid, most often, by *those with the least [power]({{ "/case-studies/politics/governance/power/" | relative_url }}) to resist*.
