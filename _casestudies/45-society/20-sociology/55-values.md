### Overview

Turn on the news, and it won't take long before you hear a Prime Minister or President passionately defending **"Our Values"**, **"British Values"**, or **"The American Dream"**. It sounds profound, unifying, and righteous. 

But if we look at this through the lens of a [scientific worldview]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#scientific-worldview), _it is complete nonsense_{: .mark}.

![Values]({{ "/assets/images/casestudies/society/values.webp" | relative_url }})

When leaders base decisions on **"values"**, they are actively choosing to *ignore* the [evidence-based]({{ "/guide/first-principles/evidence/" | relative_url }}) scientific method. Worse, they are _exploiting_{: .warn} a [massive illusion]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#illusion). By invoking a vague, mythical set of shared ideals, authorities manipulate the public, mask their own interests, and completely avoid the difficult, measurable work of *actually solving real-world problems*.

---

### Background Info: Where Did "Values" Come From?

_The idea of "shared values" has evolved from a basic survival tool into a massive political weapon:_{: .subtle}

- _The Ancestral Tribe:_{: .bullet} In a small hunter-gatherer band, "values" weren't abstract political talking points. Things like sharing food and protecting the group were *strict, observable rules* that literally kept the tribe alive.

- _The Mega-Society:_{: .bullet} As human populations grew into *nations of millions*, you couldn't rely on _face-to-face_{: .bold} trust anymore. To keep vast groups of strangers from fighting, early states invented **"superordinate identities"**—shared myths, religions, and eventually, _"national values"_{: .neon}.

- _The Modern Political Trick:_{: .bullet} Today, modern societies are *incredibly diverse*. Because millions of people will never agree on specific policies, politicians rely on *highly abstract* value-words ("fairness," "freedom," "justice") to win elections without ever committing to _measurable action_{: .bold}.

---

### Step 1: Wipe the Slate Clean

Let’s discard the *fairy tale* that a nation of millions shares a single, unified moral compass. We need to treat **"values"** not as sacred truths, but as [made-up stories]({{ "/case-studies/narratives/" | relative_url }}) used to *control* [behaviour]({{ "/guide/human-history/psychology/" | relative_url }}). 

- _The Problem:_{: .bullet} Relying on vague _"values"_{: .bold} in politics actively destroys _evidence-based policy_{: .cool}, erases true diversity, and protects the people in power.

- _The Prevailing Myth:_{: .bullet} *"We all share the same core values."* (This ignores the massive, measurable _diversity_{: .cool} of real people).

- _The Excuse:_{: .bullet} *"Our policies must reflect our values."* (This is the ultimate excuse to ignore scientific data when it is politically _uncomfortable_{: .warn}).

---

### Step 2: Applying First Principles

#### Testability

You cannot put **"fairness"** in a test tube. Because values are *fictions*, they are fluid and unmeasurable. However, we *can* test the results of policies. We can measure if a policy objectively reduces poverty, crime, or disease. 

#### Objective vs. Intersubjective

In science, something is *objective* if it exists _regardless of what human beings think about it_{: .mark} (like gravity or a viral infection). **"Values,"** however, are entirely [intersubjective]({{ "/guide/first-principles/intersubjective/" | relative_url }}). They only exist because human beings *imagine them*. There is no biological or physical law dictating **"British Values"** or the **"American Dream."**

#### Only EVIDENCE Matters

When a leader talks about values, the principle of **"Only Evidence Matters"** demands we ask:

- Whose specific interests do these "national values" actually serve?

- When the data proves a **"values-based"** policy is _failing_{: .warn}, _why do we keep doing it_{: .mark}?

---

### Step 3: The Real-World Cost (The Lock-Ins)

_When a society prioritizes imaginary values over hard evidence, it falls into several dangerous traps:_{: .subtle}

- _The Catch-All Trap (The Rorschach Test):_{: .bullet} Words like _"fairness"_{: .bold} are completely empty. A minimum-wage worker hears "fairness" and thinks of a living wage. A corporate CEO hears the exact same word and thinks of lower taxes. Politicians use this *abstract language on purpose*. Everyone projects _their own desires_{: .mark} onto the leader, creating an [illusion]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#illusion) of unity while the system commits to _zero_{: .warn} measurable action.

- _The "Whose Values?" Trap:_{: .bullet} _"National Values"_{: .neon} almost never reflect the messy reality of the general public. They usually reflect _the values of the dominant class_{: .mark}—the wealthy, the media, and politicians. Anyone whose life doesn't fit this narrow, elite definition is quietly marginalized or labeled _"unpatriotic"_{: .bold}.

- _The Evidence-Evasion Trap:_{: .bullet} This is the most *dangerous consequence*. _"Values"_{: .bold} provide the ultimate excuse to *ignore* [facts]({{ "/guide/first-principles/correct-answers/" | relative_url }}). If an _evidence-based solution_{: .cool} to crime or drug addiction is politically unpopular, leaders will reject the science by simply claiming, *"It doesn't align with our values"*. _This guarantees that complex problems are never actually solved_{: .mark}.

---

### Step 4: Reflect on Human History (The Great Mismatch)

Our obsession with **"shared values"** reveals a profound [mismatch]({{ "/case-studies/mismatches/" | relative_url }}) between our ancient brains and the modern world.

- _The Village vs. The Mega-Nation:_{: .bullet} Our brains [evolved]({{ "/guide/human-history/evolution/" | relative_url }}) to expect *total moral agreement* within a tiny tribe of 150 people. But in a massive modern nation made up of countless different _subcultures_{: .cool}, demanding a *single set of values* is biologically and socially impossible. Trying to force it inevitably leads to the state erasing genuine diversity.

- _Feelings vs. Complex Systems:_{: .bullet} In the Stone Age, solving a problem usually meant applying a basic moral rule ("don't steal"). Today, problems like global healthcare, economics, and climate change are _massively complex systems_{: .bold}. You cannot solve a complex mathematical or logistical crisis using your **"gut feelings"** about what is morally right. _You need data_{: .mark}.

## Conclusion: Replacing Rhetoric with Reality

If we had to get it right—because human [flourishing]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#human-flourishing) actually depends on it—what would our conclusion be?

The political obsession with **"values"** is not a unifying force. _It is a trick_{: .mark}. It is a social illusion that allows leaders to hide their real interests, stifle diverse viewpoints, and completely avoid the *scientific method*.

- _Values are Fictions:_{: .bullet} They are imaginary stories, not laws of nature.

- _Rhetoric Masks Inaction:_{: .bullet} Politicians use abstract values to avoid measurable accountability.

- _Diversity Defies Consensus:_{: .bullet} A massive [society]({{ "/case-studies/society/social-structures/" | relative_url }}) cannot, and should not, share a single moral brain.

If we genuinely want to improve society, we have to stop trying to govern based on vague, intersubjective feelings. We must demand that policies be justified by empirical data, testable hypotheses, and measurable harm reduction. By replacing endless moral arguments with the evidence-based scientific method, we can finally stop arguing over whose **"values"** are superior, and start actually solving the problems that affect us all.

So the next time you hear a politician or institution banging on about **"our values"**, recognise it for what it is: *a sleight of hand*. It is a socially constructed [narrative]({{ "/case-studies/narratives/" | relative_url }}) designed to _manufacture_{: .warn} consent, _mask_{: .warn} elite interests, and _avoid_{: .warn} measurable accountability.

_In short, it is someone talking BS._{: .subtle}

### Final Thought

Are we saying society should act like cold, unfeeling robots and reject all values entirely? Not at all. Values are a deeply human part of who we are, and a scientific worldview doesn't ask us to give them up. Instead, it asks us to recognise their *true nature*: they are _made-up_{: .bold}, wildly diverse, and constantly changing. They are not _objective truths_{: .bold} handed down by the universe.

We simply have to separate *what we want* from *how we get there*. Values are perfectly fine for determining our **aggregate preferences**—deciding, as a collective, that we want less poverty, better health, or a cleaner planet. But values have absolutely *no place in objective policy-making*. You cannot let a **"moral feeling"** override hard, empirical evidence about what actually works to solve those problems. Values can pick the destination, but _only the scientific method can build the map._{: .mark}

Ultimately, realising that our values are just socially constructed stories is incredibly empowering. As we will explore later in our Morality series (looking at thinkers like Nietzsche), knowing that humans created our current values means we have the power to reimagine and change them. We don't have to be trapped by the past. We can engage in a **"revaluation of all values"**—stripping away the outdated dogmas of [secular priesthoods]({{ "/guide/obstacles/secular-priesthood/" | relative_url }}) and consciously designing new, flexible ideals that actually support _measurable human flourishing._{: .cool}
