### Overview

A plague sweeps the land. The economy collapses. The old ways of life are crumbling. Society is gripped by [fear]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#fear) and [uncertainty]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#certainty). What happens next is one of the oldest and darkest patterns in post [Agricultural Transition]({{ "/guide/human-history/agricultural-transition/" | relative_url }}) history: _a search for someone to blame_{: .mark}. (See Also: [The Blame Game]({{ "/case-studies/politics/dysfunction/blame/" | relative_url }}))

![Scapegoat]({{ "/assets/images/casestudies/mismatches/outsider.webp" | relative_url }})

This is the timeless mechanism of [scapegoating]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#scapegoats): the act of identifying a minority group, branding them as the source of all problems, and purging them to restore a sense of **"purity and order"**. From the Roman emperor Nero blaming Christians for the great fire of Rome, to the Nazis blaming Jews for Germany's economic woes, to modern politicians blaming immigrants for societal ills, *the script is horrifyingly consistent*.

But where does this ugly impulse come from? Is it an invention of evil leaders, or is it a deep-seated glitch in our own minds?

This case study will argue that the modern phenomenon of scapegoating is a _catastrophic mismatch_{: .warn}. It is the weaponisation of a primitive [survival]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#survival) instinct—our [evolved]({{ "/guide/human-history/evolution/" | relative_url }}) suspicion of outsiders—in the complex, anonymous world of civilized society. We will ask: 

_Can understanding the [evolutionary]({{ "/guide/human-history/" | relative_url }}) roots of our tribal hatred help us to finally overcome it?_{: .subtle}

### Step 1: Wipe the Slate Clean - The Natural Baseline

Let's set aside the complex politics of modern prejudice. We start by looking at the world our [brains]({{ "/case-studies/who-we-are/brain/" | relative_url }}) were designed for: a world of small, competing hunter-gatherer bands.

In this ancestral environment, a degree of [out-group]({{ "/case-studies/us-vs-them/" | relative_url }}) suspicion was a [rational]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#rational) [survival]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#survival) strategy_{: .bold}.

- _The "Us vs. Them" Instinct:_{: .bullet} Our ancestors lived in a world of finite resources. A neighboring band was not just a group of strangers; they were *direct competitors* for food, water, and territory.

- _A High-Stakes World:_{: .bullet} A stranger could be carrying a new disease, could be a scout for a raiding party, or could be looking to steal resources. A cautious, _suspicious default_{: .warn} towards outsiders was often safer than an open, trusting one.

- _Internal Cohesion:_{: .bullet} A shared suspicion of an **"other"** is also a powerful tool for strengthening the bonds and loyalty of the **"in-group"**. _Nothing unites a group faster than a common enemy._{: .mark}

This is our evolutionary inheritance: a brain hard-wired to be acutely sensitive to the distinction between **"us"** and **"them"**. It's not inherently **"evil"**; it's a _primitive survival circuit_{: .bold}.

### Step 2: Applying First Principles

#### Testability

Yes, we can test the mechanisms of scapegoating. Social [psychology]({{ "/guide/human-history/psychology/" | relative_url }}) experiments, like the famous Robbers Cave [Experiment]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#experiment), objectively demonstrate how easily tribal loyalties can be created and how quickly they can _escalate into hostility_{: .warn}. We can also measure the historical [correlation]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#correlation) between periods of societal stress (famine, economic depression, pandemics) and the rise of persecution against [minorities]({{ "/case-studies/us-vs-them/majorities-vs-minorities/" | relative_url }}).

#### Objective vs. Intersubjective

- _Objective:_{: .bullet} The fact that a specific minority group has lower economic outcomes or higher rates of imprisonment is an _objective, measurable fact_{: .bold}.

- _Intersubjective:_{: .bullet} The scapegoating [narrative]({{ "/case-studies/narratives/" | relative_url }}) that this group's situation is due to their inherent inferiority, criminality, or malevolence is a poisonous [_intersubjective myth_{: .warn}]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#myth).

- _Only EVIDENCE Matters:_{: .bullet} We must reject the emotionally satisfying [stories]({{ "/case-studies/narratives/" | relative_url }}) that blame a simple, visible group for complex, invisible problems. We must look at the real, [evidence-based]({{ "/guide/first-principles/evidence/" | relative_url }}) causes of societal problems, which are almost always _systemic and complex_{: .bold}, not personal and simple.

### Step 3: Identify the Objective Consequences - The Rupture of the Agricultural Transition

The [Agricultural Transition]({{ "/guide/human-history/agricultural-transition/" | relative_url }}) to large, anonymous agricultural [civilisations]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#civilisations) created the perfect petri dish for this ancient instinct to mutate into a devastating _social pathology_{: .warn}.

- _The Rise of the "Internal Other":_{: .bullet} For the first time, societies were large and diverse enough to contain distinct minority groups within their own borders—people with different religions, ethnicities, customs or [sexual orientations]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#sexuality). They were no longer a competing band over the hill; *they were your neighbours*.

- _The Need for Simple Explanations:_{: .bullet} As societies became more complex, so did their problems. Why did the crops fail? Why is there a plague? Why did we lose the war? These complex questions are difficult to answer. It is far easier and more psychologically satisfying to blame a visible and _powerless internal group_{: .warn}.

- _The Invention of the Modern Witch-Hunt:_{: .bullet} The European [witch-hunts]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#witch-trials) are the ultimate case study. A period of immense social stress (climate change, plague, [religious]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#religion) upheaval) created a demand for a scapegoat. The **"witch"**—typically an independent, elderly, or non-[conforming]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#conformity) woman—was the perfect target. The [objective]({{ "/guide/first-principles/intersubjective/" | relative_url }}) consequence of this mass hysteria was the torture and execution of tens of thousands of innocent people, sanctioned by both the state and the church.

### Step 4: Reflect on Human History - The Great Mismatch

Modern scapegoating is a profound _mismatch_{: .warn} between our ancient tribal psychology and the reality of a diverse, globalized world.

- _The Mismatch of Scale:_{: .bullet} Our **"us vs. them"** instinct was designed for small-group competition. In a nation of millions, it is hijacked by political and media actors to create vast, imaginary tribes ("the real Americans", "the metropolitan elite") and to _foment hatred_{: .warn} against fellow citizens.

- _The Mismatch of Information:_{: .bullet} We are bombarded by media that profits from fear and [outrage]({{ "/case-studies/politics/political-dynamics/moral-outrage/" | relative_url }}) (as explored in [Fear-Mongering]({{ "/guide/obstacles/fear-mongering/" | relative_url }})). Algorithms on social media are expertly designed to feed us content that confirms our [biases]({{ "/guide/thinking-for-yourself/cognitive-biases/" | relative_url }}) against the **"other"**, creating *digital mobs* and accelerating the process of _witch-hunts_{: .warn}.

- _The Mismatch of Problems:_{: .bullet} Our instinct is to find a simple, personal cause for our problems. But modern problems—like economic [inequality]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#inequality) or global warming—are the result of vast, impersonal systems. The scapegoating narrative offers a comforting but dangerously _false shortcut_{: .warn}, preventing us from addressing the real, _systemic causes_{: .bold}.

## Conclusion: A Verdict on Our Inner Demon

If we had to get it right, as if our ability to live peacefully in a diverse [society]({{ "/case-studies/society/social-structures/" | relative_url }}) depended on it—*because it does*—what would our conclusion be?

The evidence is clear: the instinct to scapegoat is a dangerous and _primitive survival mechanism_{: .warn} that is catastrophically maladapted for the modern world. It is a cognitive glitch that has been the justification for some of the _worst atrocities_{: .warn} in human history.

A _Scientific Worldview_{: .neon} does not deny this instinct exists. It insists that we recognise it, understand its _evolutionary origins_{: .bold}, and build cultural and institutional firewalls to *contain it*.

- _Promote Critical Thinking as a Civic Duty:_{: .bullet} The primary defence against scapegoating is a populace trained to be skeptical of simple, emotionally satisfying narratives. We must teach ourselves and children to question *who benefits from a story of blame*.

- _Protect Minority Rights with an Iron Wall:_{: .bullet} As argued in [Mob vs. Minority]({{ "/case-studies/us-vs-them/majorities-vs-minorities/" | relative_url }}), the fundamental [rights]({{ "/case-studies/politics/governance/rights/" | relative_url }}) of individuals and minorities must be constitutionally _protected_{: .bold} and placed beyond the reach of a [panicked majority]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#panic).

- _Hold Leaders and Media Accountable:_{: .bullet} We must create a [culture]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#culture) that relentlessly calls out and sanctions leaders and media outlets that use the lazy and dangerous tactic of blaming minorities for systemic problems.

- _Foster Inter-Group Contact:_{: .bullet} The evidence from social psychology is clear: one of the most powerful ways to break down *prejudice* is through positive, cooperative contact between different groups. A rational society would actively design its cities, schools, and institutions to *foster this kind of interaction*.

We may never eliminate the **"us vs. them"** instinct from our primate brains.

_But with a commitment to reason, evidence, and the [nn]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#protection-and-protectors) of individual rights, we can choose to build a society that starves this inner demon rather than feeding it._{: .subtle}
