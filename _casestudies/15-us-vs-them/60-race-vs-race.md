### Overview

In the spring of 1994, in the small Central African nation of Rwanda, the world witnessed *a descent* into _madness_{: .warn}. Over the course of just 100 days, an estimated *800,000* people were **systematically slaughtered**, most of them *hacked to death* with machetes by their own neighbours, colleagues, and even family members.

![Race vs. Race]({{ "/assets/images/casestudies/us-vs-them/race.webp" | relative_url }})

This was not a conventional war between armies. It was an intimate, face-to-face _genocide_{: .warn}. The killers were not *faceless soldiers*; they were _ordinary citizens_{: .bold}—farmers, teachers, doctors. The victims were not a distant, foreign enemy; they were the people they had lived alongside for generations.

**The Rwandan Genocide** is the ultimate and most terrifying case study in the power of an _"Us vs. Them"_{: .bold} [narrative]({{ "/case-studies/narratives/" | relative_url }}). It demonstrates, with chilling clarity, how a _powerful elite_{: .bold} can deliberately *manufacture* a tribal identity, _dehumanise_{: .warn} a minority group, and _incite_{: .warn} ordinary people to commit unimaginable acts of violence. It is a real-world laboratory for *the mechanics of* _hate_{: .warn}.

### Step 1: Wipe the Slate Clean - The Myth of Ancient Hatreds

When the genocide erupted, the international media often explained it away with a *lazy* and *misleading* [narrative]({{ "/case-studies/narratives/" | relative_url }}): _"ancient tribal hatreds"_{: .bold}. This story suggests that the two groups involved, the Hutu and the Tutsi, were timeless, natural enemies, and that this violence was an inevitable eruption of primitive animosity.

A [clean slate]({{ "/guide/clean-slate/" | relative_url }}), evidence-based approach reveals this to be a _lie_{: .warn}.

- _A Shared Culture:_{: .bullet} Before the _colonial era_{: .bold}, "Hutu" and "Tutsi" were not distinct, warring tribes. They spoke the same language, shared the same [religion]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#religion), and lived side-by-side. The terms were closer to _socio-economic classes_{: .bold} than to *rigid ethnic identities*: "Tutsi" generally referred to those who owned cattle (a sign of wealth), and "Hutu" to those who farmed the land. It was possible for a Hutu to become a Tutsi, *and vice versa*.

- _The Colonial Invention of Race:_{: .bullet} The real poison was introduced by the Belgian [colonial administrators]({{ "/case-studies/pseudoscience/colonial-administrations/" | relative_url }}) in the early 20th century. Obsessed with the _pseudoscientific_{: .warn} *racial theories* of the time, they decided that the taller, more "European-looking" Tutsi were a "superior" race, destined to rule. They issued ethnic identity cards, [locking]({{ "/case-studies/locked-in/" | relative_url }}) these previously *fluid* social categories into rigid, unchangeable racial destinies. They systematically favoured the Tutsi minority, placing them in positions of [power]({{ "/case-studies/politics/governance/power/" | relative_url }}) and *discriminating* against the Hutu majority.

The "ancient hatred" was not ancient at all. _It was a modern, colonial creation_{: .mark}.

### Step 2: Applying First Principles

#### Testability

Can we [test]({{ "/guide/first-principles/testability/" | relative_url }}) the claims of the genocidal narrative? Yes. The claim that the Tutsi were a *"foreign race"* of "invaders* can be tested against the historical, linguistic, and genetic evidence, all of which show a deeply intertwined and *shared heritage*. The claim that they were plotting to exterminate the Hutu was an unfalsifiable *conspiracy theory*.

#### Objective vs. Intersubjective

- _Objective:_{: .bullet} The fact that Hutu and Tutsi people are genetically almost indistinguishable is an [objective]({{ "/guide/first-principles/intersubjective/" | relative_url }}), scientific fact.

- _Intersubjective:_{: .bullet} The [belief]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#beliefs) that they were two distinct and enemy "races" was a powerful intersubjective [narrative]({{ "/case-studies/narratives/" | relative_url }}), first created by the Belgians and then [weaponised]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#weaponise) by Hutu extremists.

#### Only EVIDENCE Matters

We must reject the *simplistic* "ancient hatreds" story and look at the [evidence]({{ "/guide/first-principles/evidence/" | relative_url }}) of how the genocidal _ideology_{: .bold} was deliberately and systematically _constructed_{: .warn} and broadcast.

### Step 3: Identify the Consequences - The Mechanics of Hate Propaganda

In the years leading up to 1994, as a Hutu elite faced a *political crisis* and a *civil war*, they used their control of the state and media to turn their Tutsi neighbours into an existential threat. This was a *deliberate*, [top-down]({{ "/case-studies/mismatches/top-down-control/" | relative_url }}) campaign of _dehumanisation_{: .warn}.

- _The Narrative:_{: .bullet} The Tutsi were not _fellow Rwandans_{: .bold}; they were a foreign, alien race of "cockroaches" (inyenzi) who had historically oppressed the Hutu and were now plotting to re-enslave them and kill their children. The only way for the Hutu to survive was to strike first and exterminate them all.

- _The Vested Interest:_{: .bullet} The extremist Hutu Power movement was *losing its grip* on [power]({{ "/case-studies/politics/governance/power/" | relative_url }}). By creating a racial enemy, they could **unify** the Hutu majority behind them and eliminate their political opponents (both Tutsi and moderate Hutu). The genocide was, at its core, a desperate and brutal strategy *to hold onto power*.

- _The Propaganda Machine:_{: .bullet} The state-controlled radio station, RTLM, became the primary weapon. Day after day, it broadcast a relentless stream of _hate speech_{: .warn}, *conspiracy theories*, and *racist caricatures*. It read out the names and addresses of Tutsi and moderate Hutu to be targeted. When the genocide began, the radio gave the direct orders: "The graves are not yet full. Who will help us to do the good work?"

The _objective consequence_{: .bold} of this *manufactured narrative* was _the systematic slaughter of 800,000 human beings in 100 days_{: .mark}.

## Conclusion: A Verdict on Dehumanisation

If we had to get it right, as if our very [survival]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#survival) as a _multi-ethnic_{: .bold}, *multi-belief* species depended on it—because it does—what would our conclusion be?

The _Rwandan Genocide_{: .bold} is not a story about *"ancient hatreds"* or the unique evil of one group of people. It is a terrifying case study in the universal and timeless mechanics of [dehumanisation]({{ "/case-studies/mismatches/scapegoats/" | relative_url }}).

- _"Us vs. Them" is a Political Tool:_{: .bullet} The creation of a racial or ethnic "other" is not a [natural]({{ "/guide/human-history/natural-vs-normal/" | relative_url }}) phenomenon; it is a _political strategy_{: .bold}. It is used by those in power to *consolidate* their own position, *distract* from their failures, and *mobilise a base of support*.

- _Dehumanisation is the Prerequisite for Genocide:_{: .bullet} Before you can convince ordinary people to _murder_{: .warn} their neighbours, you must first convince them that their neighbours *are not human*. The _language_{: .bold} is the first weapon. Calling a group "cockroaches", "vermin", or a "disease" is not just an insult; it is a *necessary step* in switching off our [natural]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#natural) [_human empathy_{: .bold}]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#empathy).

- _The Danger of a Single Story:_{: .bullet} When a single, powerful, state-sponsored narrative is allowed to _monopolise_{: .warn} the airwaves and there are *no alternative sources* of information, _a society can be driven to collective insanity_{: .mark}.

A _scientific worldview_{: .bold}, with its _commitment to evidence_{: .bold} and its deep understanding of our _shared human biology_{: .bold}, is the ultimate antidote to racist _ideology_{: .warn}. It reveals that "race" itself is largely a *social construct*, not a biological reality. The lesson from Rwanda is a brutal and permanent one. The most dangerous people in the world are not those who are different from us, but those who tell us that those differences are all that matter.

> We must be relentlessly vigilant against the **language of dehumanisation**, for it is the sound of *a deadly virus being released into the airwaves*.
