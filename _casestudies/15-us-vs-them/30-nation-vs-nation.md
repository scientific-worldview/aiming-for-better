### Overview

In the summer of 1914, Europe was the most advanced, prosperous, and interconnected [civilisation]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#civilisations) the world had ever known. It was a golden age of science, art, and trade. Yet, within a few short weeks, this *entire edifice collapsed* into a catastrophic, four-year orgy of _industrialised slaughter_{: .warn} that killed an estimated **20 million people**.

_Why?_{: .mark}

It was not a war for resources, for [survival]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#survival), or for any tangible, [rational]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#rational) gain. It was a war fueled by a new and potent *secular religion*: [Nationalism]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#nationalism).

![Nation-State]({{ "/assets/images/casestudies/us-vs-them/nation.webp" | relative_url }})

We will see how the _imagined community_{: .bold} of the [Nation-State]({{ "/case-studies/politics/miscelleneous/nation-state/" | relative_url }}) became the new tribe, demanding an absolute loyalty that overrode reason, self-interest and _our shared humanity_{: .mark}.

This case study will examine World War I as the definitive example of a modern _"Us vs. Them"_{: .bold} conflict. It demonstrates how the ancient tribal instinct, when *scaled up* to the level of the [nation-state]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#nation-state) and armed with industrial technology, can lead *an entire continent* to commit _collective suicide_{: .warn} for the sake of an _abstract idea_{: .bold}.

### Step 1: Wipe the Slate Clean - The Invention of the "Nation"

Let's set aside the maps, the flags, and the national anthems. We must remember that _the nation-state is a recent invention_{: .mark}.

- _The Ancient Tribe:_{: .bullet} For 99.5% of our [history]({{ "/guide/human-history/" | relative_url }}), our "Us" was a small, tangible kin-group of people *we knew personally*.

- _The Modern Tribe:_{: .bullet} Nationalism is the attempt to *hack this ancient [psychology]({{ "/guide/human-history/psychology/" | relative_url }}). It convinces **millions of strangers** that they are all part of the same "family"—the French, the Germans, the British. It uses *powerful symbols* (flags), *shared myths* (history), and *rituals* (anthems) to create an _"imagined community"_{: .bold} (as termed by Benedict Anderson) that commands the same *ferocious loyalty* as the ancient tribe.

- _The Tribal Logic:_{: .bullet} This new [narrative]({{ "/case-studies/narratives/" | relative_url }}) *hijacked our tribal instincts*. The Nation became the _"[in-group]({{ "/case-studies/us-vs-them/" | relative_url }})"_{: .bold}, and every other nation became a potential _"out-group"_{: .bold}. The [flourishing]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#human-flourishing) of one nation was seen as dependent on the *defeat or humiliation* of it's _rivals_{: .warn}. It was a _zero-sum game_{: .bold} played on a *continental scale*.

In 1914, this new [tribalism]({{ "/case-studies/politics/political-dynamics/tribalism/" | relative_url }}) had reached a fever pitch. Every major European power had spent decades _indoctrinating_{: .warn} its citizens with a _narrative_{: .bold} of their own national superiority and the inherent treachery of their neighbours.

### Step 2: Applying First Principles

#### Testability

Can we [test]({{ "/guide/first-principles/testability/" | relative_url }}) the claims of nationalism? This is a crucial question. We must separate specific, measurable claims from the core, unfalsifiable narrative.

- _Measurable Claims:_{: .bullet} A nation can make testable claims like "We have the highest GDP", "Our citizens have the longest life expectancy", or "Our military is the most powerful". These are [objective]({{ "/guide/first-principles/intersubjective/" | relative_url }}) statements that can be verified with data.

- _The Unfalsifiable Narrative:_{: .bullet} The core claim of nationalism, however, is not a specific metric. It is the vague, all-encompassing, and emotionally charged [belief]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#beliefs) that *"our nation is inherently superior"* often based on [Our Values]({{ "/case-studies/society/sociology/values/" | relative_url }}). This claim of overall moral or cultural superiority is *unfalsifiable nonsense*. Why? Because there is no agreed-upon, objective standard for what "superior" means. If a nationalist claims their country is superior because it has the highest GDP, a critic can retort that another country is superior because it has the lowest infant mortality or the highest levels of happiness. The debate is not about [evidence]({{ "/guide/first-principles/evidence/" | relative_url }}); it's about _which value you choose as your yardstick_{: .mark}. The core claim is a statement of _tribal pride_{: .bold}, *not a testable [hypothesis]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#hypothesis).

#### Objective vs. Intersubjective

- _Objective:_{: .bullet} The fact that a German soldier and a French soldier are genetically almost identical, share the same basic human needs, and have more in common with each other than with their own aristocratic leaders is an objective, biological reality.

- _Intersubjective:_{: .bullet} The belief that they are mortal enemies because one was born on one side of a river and the other on the opposite side is a purely *intersubjective narrative*.

#### Only EVIDENCE Matters

We must reject the romantic poetry of "dying for one's country" and look at the evidence of the trenches.

> Was the sacrifice of *millions of young men* a rational exchange for the *negligible territorial gains* achieved?

### Step 3: Identify the Consequences - The Industrialisation of Tribalism

When the crisis of 1914 hit, the interlocking alliances and mobilisation timetables of the great powers turned a regional dispute in the Balkans into a continental catastrophe. The _"Us vs. Them" _{: .bold}logic took over completely.

- _The Narrative of Existential Threat:_{: .bullet} Every nation convinced its people that they were fighting a defensive war for survival against a barbaric aggressor. The Germans feared *"Slavic hordes"* from the East; the French feared *"Prussian militarism"*; the British feared German *dominance of the seas*.

- _The Dehumanisation of the Enemy:_{: .bullet} [Propaganda Machines]({{ "/guide/human-history/propaganda/" | relative_url }}) went into overdrive. British newspapers published entirely *fabricated stories* of German soldiers bayoneting Belgian babies. The enemy was *no longer human*; they were **"The Hun"**, a monstrous *"other"* that had to be destroyed.

- _The Reality of the Trenches:_{: .bullet} The result was a *stalemate* of unimaginable _horror_{: .warn}. Millions of men lived in mud and filth *for years*, periodically *ordered to charge into machine-gun fire* to capture a few hundred yards of devastated ground. The tribal loyalty to the nation was so strong that it overrode the most basic human instinct for self-preservation on a massive scale.

- _The End of the Illusion:_{: .bullet} The war *solved nothing*. It destroyed the economies of Europe, led to the collapse of four empires (German, Austro-Hungarian, Russian, Ottoman), and sowed the bitter seeds—*economic ruin*, *national humiliation*, and a *desire for revenge*—that would lead directly to an even greater catastrophe just two decades later.

### Conclusion: A Verdict on the Flag

If we had to get it right, as if the future *peace of the world* depended on it—because it does—what would our conclusion be?

World War I is the ultimate, tragic proof that _nationalism_{: .warn} is a dangerous and obsolete operating system for a modern, interconnected world. It takes our ancient capacity for _tribal loyalty_{: .bold} and scales it up to a level where it becomes *a relentless engine for mass death*.

A [scientific worldview]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#scientific-worldview) does not ask us to abandon our love for *our home*, [our culture]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#culture), or *our community*. But it demands that we recognise the crucial difference between _patriotism_{: .bold} (*a love for one's home*) and _nationalism_{: .warn} (a belief in the "superiority" of one's nation over others).

- _Question the "Imagined Community":_{: .bullet} We must constantly remind ourselves that the nation is a _human construct_{: .bold}, *not* a biological reality. Our shared humanity is *a deeper and more fundamental* truth than our different passports.

- _Beware the Dehumanizing Narrative:_{: .bullet} We must be _fiercely skeptical_{: .bold} of any *leader* or *media outlet* that uses _inflammatory language_{: .warn} to describe another *nation* or *group of people* (or even individuals).

> Dehumanisation is *always the prelude to violence*.

- _Recognise the Trap of "Us vs. Them":_{: .bullet} In a world of *global challenges* like _climate change_{: .bold} and _pandemics_{: .bold}, the *only* viable "Us" is _humanity itself_{: .bold}. Continuing to operate on the outdated software of competing national tribes is not just irrational; _it is an existential threat to our entire species_{: .mark}.

The lesson of 1914 is written in the graveyards of Europe: when we allow *abstract tribal narratives* to override our common human reality, we do not achieve glory. We only achieve _a senseless and industrialised form of collective suicide_{: .mark}.
