The following pages examine some clashes between [narratives]({{ "/case-studies/narratives/" | relative_url }}).

This particular Case Study will not focus on a single conflict. Instead, *it will deconstruct the meta-clash itself*: the timeless battle between [_Dogmatic Belief_{: .bold}]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#beliefs) and _Evidential Inquiry_{: .bold}. We will dissect the *"virtuous-sounding"* language that fuels these conflicts and propose a more [rational]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#rational), scientific set of **"virtues"** designed *to dismantle the engine of conflict itself*.

### Overview: Myth vs. Myth

What is the most destructive force in [human history]({{ "/guide/human-history/" | relative_url }})? It is not a weapon, a plague, or a natural disaster. It is the _engine_{: .bold} that drives the *creation and use* of weapons, that hinders our response to plagues, and that fuels conflict in the aftermath of disaster. It is the unwavering, absolute [certainty]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#certainty) that **"my group's story is the one true story"**.

![Us vs. Them]({{ "/assets/images/casestudies/us-vs-them/myths.webp" | relative_url }})

Throughout post [Agricultural Transition]({{ "/guide/human-history/agricultural-transition/" | relative_url }}) history (approx. the last 10,000 years), the greatest source of human-inflicted suffering has been the violent clash of one [intersubjective]({{ "/guide/first-principles/intersubjective/" | relative_url }}) _narrative_{: .warn} against another. From the [religious]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#religion) wars that tore Europe apart to the [ideological]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#ideology) slaughterhouses of the 20th century, the pattern is the same: one group, armed with a powerful and [unfalsifiable]({{ "/guide/first-principles/testability/" | relative_url }}) belief system, *seeks to impose its* _"truth"_{: .warn} *on another*.

---

### Step 1: Wipe the Slate Clean

Let's set aside the *grand labels*—Christianity, Islam, Communism, Fascism, Liberalism, Conservatism. Let's ignore the *flags*, the *holy books*, and the *manifestos* for a moment.

We start by asking a fundamental question: **What is the functional purpose of a large-scale belief system?**

From a [clean slate]({{ "/guide/clean-slate/" | relative_url }}) perspective, an intersubjective [narrative]({{ "/case-studies/narratives/" | relative_url }}) is _a tool for social cohesion_{: .mark}. It is a set of shared stories, myths, and [rules]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#rules) that *allows a large group of strangers* to _cooperate_{: .cool}, trust each other, and work towards _common goals_{: .cool}.

_It is the operating system for a civilisation._{: .subtle}

But like any powerful tool, *it can be used for creation or* _destruction_{: .warn}. The problem arises when the users *forget* they are holding _a tool_{: .bold} and begin to believe they are holding *the one and only* _blueprint for reality_{: .bold}.

---

### Step 2: Applying First Principles

#### Testability

By their very nature, most of these grand, all-encompassing _narratives_{: .warn} are _unfalsifiable_{: .bold}. Although fascist ideas of race can now be falsified, there's often *no* [experiment]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#experiment) you can run to *prove or disprove* other ideas: the existence of heaven, the historical inevitability of the proletariat, or the "spiritual superiority" of a chosen people. Because they cannot be [tested]({{ "/guide/first-principles/testability/" | relative_url }}), they are removed from the realm of rational inquiry. They must be accepted on _faith_{: .warn}.

#### Objective vs. Intersubjective

This is the core of the issue. These systems take **intersubjective beliefs** (*stories we agree upon*) and present them as _objective truths_{: .bold} ([facts]({{ "/guide/first-principles/correct-answers/" | relative_url }}) about the universe). The believer conflates *"what my group believes"* with *"the way the world is".* This is a profound and dangerous _category error_{: .warn}.

#### Only EVIDENCE Matters

The only way to judge a belief system is not by the beauty of its stories or the passion of its believers, but by its measurable, real-world _consequences_{: .warn}: 

_Does it lead to human flourishing or human suffering?_{: .subtle}

### Step 3: Identify the Language of Dogma - The "Virtuous-Sounding Notions"

Intersubjective _narratives_{: .warn} are protected from scrutiny by _a powerful set of words that sound like virtues_{: .mark} but are often **shields** for *intellectual laziness and intolerance*. A rational thinker must learn to *identify and question them*.

- _Faith:_{: .bullet} The belief in something without, or in direct opposition to, [the evidence]({{ "/guide/first-principles/evidence/" | relative_url }}). It is praised as a virtue, but from a [scientific perspective]({{ "/guide/first-principles/scientific-method/" | relative_url }}), *it is a* _cognitive flaw_{: .bold}.

- _Conviction & Moral Certainty:_{: .bullet} *The feeling of being right*. This _emotional state_{: .bold} is often prized above the actual evidence. History is a monument to the _catastrophic consequences_{: .warn} of men with **unwavering conviction**.

- _Principles:_{: .bullet} Rigid, pre-determined rules that are applied *without regard* to context or outcome. A principled stand can feel noble, but if it leads to *preventable suffering*, it is simply a form of [dogma]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#dogma).

- _Purity & Sanctity:_{: .bullet} The idea that certain beliefs or groups are [sacred]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#sacred) and must be protected from "contamination" by outside ideas. This is a direct *justification* for censorship, persecution, and violence against **"heretics"** or **"unbelievers"**.

- _Loyalty & Patriotism:_{: .bullet} The demand for _allegiance_{: .warn} to a group's narrative above all else, including *evidence*, *reason*, and *your own conscience*. It reframes [critical thinking]({{ "/guide/thinking-for-yourself/" | relative_url }}) as betrayal.

---

### Step 4: Reflect on Human History - A Litany of Clashes

The post [Agricultural Transition]({{ "/guide/human-history/agricultural-transition/" | relative_url }}) world is a _testament_{: .bold} to the *destructive power* of these clashes.

- _Religion vs. Religion:_{: .bullet} The Crusades, the Thirty Years' War, the conflicts between Sunni and Shia Muslims. All driven by the *absolute certainty* that each side possessed the one true path to God.

- _Ideology vs. Ideology:_{: .bullet} The 20th century saw secular religions—Fascism, Communism—wage war on an unprecedented scale, each convinced of its own historical destiny and the need to annihilate the other.

- _Nation vs. Nation:_{: .bullet} The _cult_{: .warn} of the [[nation-state]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#nation-state)] has led to *endless wars* over borders, resources, and "national honour", _sacrificing millions of lives for an imaginary line on a map_{: .mark}.

In every case, the *fuel for the fire* was the set of *"virtuous-sounding notions"* listed above. People did not kill for _evidence_{: .bold}; they killed for faith, for conviction, for the purity of their tribe.

## Conclusion: _How to Think_{: .bold}, not *What to Think*

If we had to get it right, as if the [survival]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#survival) of our species depended on it—*because it does*—what would our conclusion be?

**The evidence is clear:** intersubjective belief systems, when held with *dogmatic certainty*, are the single greatest driver of large-scale human _conflict_{: .warn}.

The problem is not the narratives themselves; _humans need shared stories to cooperate_{: .mark}. The problem is our *relationship* to those stories. A rational, [scientific worldview]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#scientific-worldview) does not offer a new "true story" to replace the old ones. Instead, it offers a new set of cognitive virtues—*a different way of holding and evaluating all stories*.

### The Rational Virtues

- _Intellectual Humility:_{: .bullet} The foundational virtue. It is the active awareness that _you might be wrong_{: .mark}. It is the opposite of _moral certainty_{: .warn}.

- _Open-mindedness:_{: .bullet} The *willingness* to engage with _competing ideas_{: .bold} and to [change your mind]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#changing-your-mind) when confronted with _better evidence_{: .bold}. It is the opposite of _unwavering conviction_{: .warn}.

- _Skepticism:_{: .bullet} The *habit* of _questioning all claims_{: .bold}, especially those that are comforting, popular, or presented by an [authority]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#authority). It is the opposite of _faith_{: .warn}.

- _Evidentialism:_{: .bullet} The [core principle]({{ "/guide/first-principles/" | relative_url }}) that the strength of your belief should be *directly proportional* to the strength of the _evidence_{: .bold} supporting it. "Faith" and "certainty" in things like germ [theory]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#theory) or [evolution]({{ "/guide/human-history/evolution/" | relative_url }}) are not the same as religious faith; they are _earned confidence_{: .bold} based on *mountains of verifiable data*.

- _Pragmatism:_{: .bullet} The *focus* on _real-world outcomes_{: .bold}. The measure of a belief system is not its internal consistency or its emotional appeal, but its _measurable impact_{: .cool} on human and sentient [well-being]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#wellbeing).

Ultimately, a _scientific approach_{: .bold} is not a set of facts to be memorised. It is a *commitment to a process*. It is a stance that chooses the _discomfort of doubt_{: .bold} over the _false_{: .warn} *comfort of certainty*, and the _hard work_{: .mark} of _inquiry_{: .bold} over the _easy_{: .mark} path of belief.

> It is the only known antidote to the **engine of conflict** that has *plagued our "civilised" world for millennia*.
