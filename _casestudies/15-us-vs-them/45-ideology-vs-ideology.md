This Case Study will explore the two biggest **"isms"** of the 20th century: _fascism_{: .bold} and _communism_{: .bold}. We will not ask which side was worse. We will apply a [rational]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#rational), evidence-based framework to analyse their methods, their claims, and their objective consequences, revealing how two supposedly opposing paths led to the same destination: the _concentration camp_{: .warn}, the _gulag_{: .warn}, and the _mass grave_{: .warn}.

![Twins]({{ "/assets/images/casestudies/us-vs-them/hell.webp" | relative_url }})

### Overview

The 20th century was defined by a titanic, world-spanning struggle, largely between two opposing _ideologies_{: .warn}: Fascism and Communism. One championed _the nation_{: .bold} and _"the race"_{: .bold}; the other championed the international _"working class"_{: .bold}. They saw themselves as mortal enemies, locked in a fight to the death for *the future of humanity*. Millions of people fought, suffered, and died in the name of this conflict.

But what if this epic battle was, from a structural and humanistic perspective, *an illusion*? What if Fascism and Communism were not opposites, but were in fact _totalitarian twins_{: .bold}, born of the same historical moment and sharing a terrifying *family resemblance*? And what can we learn from this _"Warning From History"_{: .mark}?

---

### Step 1: Wipe the Slate Clean

Let's set aside the symbols—the swastika and the hammer and sickle. Let's ignore their self-proclaimed goals—a *classless society* or a *thousand-year Reich*.

We start by asking a fundamental question: What kind of _system_{: .bold} did each _ideology_{: .warn} actually build when it gained [power]({{ "/case-studies/politics/governance/power/" | relative_url }})?

This forces us to look past their *philosophical claims* and focus on the machinery of the state they created. We must judge them not by their promises, but **by their actions**.

---

### Step 2: Applying First Principles

#### Testability

Were the core claims of these ideologies [testable]({{ "/guide/first-principles/testability/" | relative_url }})? Yes, and they *failed spectacularly*.

- _Communism's Claim:_{: .bullet} The abolition of private property will lead to a workers' paradise. Result: It led to *economic collapse*, *mass famine*, and the *rise of a new bureaucratic elite*.

- _Fascism's Claim:_{: .bullet} Racial purity and authoritarian rule will lead to national greatness. Result: It led to *genocidal war*, *economic ruin*, and *the moral and physical destruction of the nation*.

#### Objective vs. Intersubjective

Both systems were built on *powerful* [intersubjective]({{ "/guide/first-principles/intersubjective/" | relative_url }}) _myths_{: .warn}. For communists, it was the [myth]({{ "/case-studies/narratives/" | relative_url }}) of a heroic proletariat marching towards a historical destiny. For fascists, it was the [myth]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#myth) of a pure, chosen people destined to rule over inferior races. Both of these *stories* were presented not as [beliefs]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#beliefs), but as scientific, objective truths about [history]({{ "/guide/human-history/" | relative_url }}) and [biology]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#biology). **This was a catastrophic lie**.

#### Only EVIDENCE Matters

We must ignore the [propaganda]({{ "/guide/human-history/propaganda/" | relative_url }}) and the **passionate speeches**. The only evidence that matters is the _objective outcome_{: .bold}: the body count, the economic data, and the testimony of the survivors.

---

### Step 3: Identify the Objective Consequences - A Shared Blueprint for Tyranny

When we analyse the structures they built, the family resemblance is undeniable. Despite their different targets, both Fascism and Communism relied on _an identical blueprint for totalitarian control_{: .mark}.

- _A Single, Infallible Party:_{: .bullet} Both were one-party states ruled by a dictator (Hitler, Stalin, Mussolini, Mao) who held absolute power. The Party was the sole source of _"truth"_{: .warn}.

- _A "Secular Priesthood":_{: .bullet} Both created a new _elite_{: .bold}—the Nazi Party officials or the Communist apparatchiks—who acted as a [secular priesthood]({{ "/guide/obstacles/secular-priesthood/" | relative_url }}), interpreting the [sacred]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#sacred) [doctrine]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#doctrine) and enforcing its [rules]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#rules).

- _A Sacred Text:_{: .bullet} Each had its foundational text (Hitler's Mein Kampf, Marx and Lenin's writings) that was treated not as a theory, but as *"infallible scripture"*.

- _The Elimination of Dissent:_{: .bullet} Both systems used [secret police]({{ "/case-studies/locked-in/mind-control/" | relative_url }}) (the Gestapo, the NKVD), _censorship_{: .warn}, and a network of informants to crush all opposition. [Thinking for yourself]({{ "/guide/thinking-for-yourself/" | relative_url }}) *was the ultimate crime*.

- _The Creation of an Enemy:_{: .bullet} Both required an [internal enemy]({{ "/case-studies/mismatches/scapegoats/" | relative_url }}) to blame for all of society's problems and to justify the state's brutality.

  - For the Fascists, the enemy was the Jew, the intellectual, the disabled—*a racial or biological "contaminant"*.

  - For the Communists, the enemy was the bourgeoisie, the kulak (land-owning peasant), the class enemy—*an economic "parasite"*.

- _The Concentration Camp System:_{: .bullet} Both perfected the concentration camp (the Gulag in the Soviet Union) as the ultimate tool for eliminating these designated enemies and _terrorizing_{: .warn} the rest of the population into *submission*.

- _The Promise of Utopia:_{: .bullet} Both *justified* their horrific present-day actions by *promising* a glorious, utopian future that was always just over the horizon.

The objective consequence was the same: the complete _annihilation_{: .warn} of _individual [liberty]({{ "/case-studies/law/pragmatic/liberty/" | relative_url }}) and the state-sponsored _murder_{: .warn} of *tens of millions* of **human beings**.

### Step 4: Reflect on Human History - The Danger of the Collective

Both Fascism and Communism are extreme and pathological expressions of a deep human instinct: [tribalism]({{ "/case-studies/politics/political-dynamics/tribalism/" | relative_url }}). They are both a [mismatch]({{ "/case-studies/mismatches/" | relative_url }}) on a terrifying scale.

They _hijack_{: .warn} our _evolved desire_{: .bold} to [belong to a group]({{ "/case-studies/us-vs-them/" | relative_url }}) and [weaponise]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#weaponise) it. They take the [natural]({{ "/guide/human-history/natural-vs-normal/" | relative_url }}) loyalty* we feel for our small kin-group and demand we apply it to an enormous, abstract collective—**"The Race"** or **"The Class"**.

In doing so, they demand the surrender of _individual conscience_{: .bold} to the *will of the group*. "Good" is no longer about individual actions or [universal principles]({{ "/guide/first-principles/" | relative_url }}); "good" is whatever serves the Party, the Nation, or the Revolution. This is the moral logic that allows ordinary people to participate in extraordinary [cruelty]({{ "/guide/human-history/altruism-and-cruelty/" | relative_url }}).

## Conclusion: A Verdict on All Utopias

If we had to get it right, as if our [freedom]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#autonomy) and our lives depended on it—because they did, and they still do—what would our conclusion be?

The evidence from the 20th century delivers a brutal and unequivocal verdict: any _ideology_{: .warn} that demands the sacrifice of the individual to an abstract collective—whether it be class, race, or nation—_will inevitably lead to tyranny and mass murder_{: .mark}.

Fascism and Communism were not opposites. They were *competitors* for the same prize: total _control_{: .warn} over the human mind. They were both [secular religions]({{ "/guide/obstacles/secular-priesthood/" | relative_url }}), demanding *absolute faith* and *promising a future paradise* to justify a present hell.

_A rational, [scientific worldview]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#scientific-worldview) is the ultimate defence against such ideologies._{: .subtle}

It teaches us to:

- _Be Skeptical of All Grand Narratives:_{: .bullet} Any theory that claims to have a simple, all-encompassing *explanation* for human history and a perfect *solution* for all its problems is almost certainly a dangerous _lie_{: .warn}.

- _Prioritise the Individual over the Collective:_{: .bullet} A just [society]({{ "/case-studies/society/social-structures/" | relative_url }}) is one that protects the [rights]({{ "/case-studies/politics/governance/rights/" | relative_url }}) and dignity of the individual against the demands of any group, tribe, or state. The individual is _real_{: .bold}; the collective is an _abstraction_{: .bold}.

- _Judge Systems by Their Results, Not Their Intentions:_{: .bullet} The *noblest-sounding ideals* can produce the most horrific outcomes. We must *ignore* the utopian promises and focus relentlessly on the [evidence]({{ "/guide/first-principles/evidence/" | relative_url }}) of what a system *actually does* to the human beings who live under it.

The war between Fascism and Communism was not a battle between good and evil. It was a gang war between two rival families of the same totalitarian mafia.

_The lesson is not to pick a better gang, but to reject the logic of gangsterism entirely._{: .subtle}
