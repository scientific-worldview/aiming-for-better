### Overview

**"The majority rules"**. It's a phrase we're taught to see as the very definition of fairness and [democracy]({{ "/case-studies/politics/democracy/" | relative_url }}). The idea is simple: in a disagreement, the side with more votes wins. It feels intuitive, just, and is the bedrock of our [political systems]({{ "/case-studies/politics/political-ideas/" | relative_url }}).

_But is it?_{: .mark}

What happens when [the majority]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#majorities) decides that a minority group is a *threat*, an *abomination*, or an *enemy*? What happens when the [will of the people]({{ "/case-studies/politics/democracy/" | relative_url }}) is to strip a smaller group of its [rights]({{ "/case-studies/politics/governance/rights/" | relative_url }}), its *property*, or even its *life*?

![Mob]({{ "/assets/images/casestudies/us-vs-them/mob-min.webp" | relative_url }})

This case study will [wipe the slate clean]({{ "/guide/clean-slate/" | relative_url }}) of the *romantic ideal* of "majority rule". We will not ask whether democracy is good, but rather, what kind of system is needed to prevent it from becoming *a tool* of [oppression]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#oppression). We will examine the objective consequences of *unchecked* majority [power]({{ "/case-studies/politics/governance/power/" | relative_url }}) and explore the [rational]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#rational) mechanisms required to protect minorities from the *passions of the mob*.

_History is a long and brutal testament to the fact that a majority can be just as tyrannical as any king or dictator._{: .subtle}

---

### Step 1: Wipe the Slate Clean

Let's set aside the *comforting notion* that _"the people"_{: .bold} are inherently *wise* or *just*. Let's discard the _assumption_{: .warn} that a democratic [vote]({{ "/case-studies/politics/democracy/boundary-of-the-ballot/" | relative_url }}) is a reliable path to a correct or moral outcome.

We start with a fundamental, [uncomfortable]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#uncomfortable-truths) question:

_Why should the fact that 51% of people believe something give them the moral or political right to impose it on the other 49%?_{: .subtle}

This forces us to see "majority rule" not as a [sacred principle]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#sacred), but as a practical—and potentially dangerous—_mechanism_{: .bold} for making decisions.

_It is a tool for managing [power]({{ "/case-studies/politics/governance/power/" | relative_url }}), not a guarantee of [justice]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#justice-and-injustice)._{: .subtle}

---

### Step 2: Applying First Principles

#### Testability

Can we [test]({{ "/guide/first-principles/testability/" | relative_url }}) the outcomes of unchecked majority rule? Yes, absolutely. History is our laboratory. We can objectively document the [laws]({{ "/case-studies/law/theory/" | relative_url }}) passed, the *groups* _persecuted_{: .warn}, and the *rights* _violated_{: .warn} by democratically empowered majorities.

#### Objective vs. Intersubjective

- _Objective:_{: .bullet} The fact that a specific law was passed by a majority vote is an objective, historical fact.

- _Intersubjective:_{: .bullet} The [belief]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#beliefs) held by that majority—that their actions were *"right"*, *"necessary"*, or *"for the good of the nation"*—is a purely [intersubjective narrative]({{ "/articles/philosophy/true-believers/" | relative_url }}). We must judge the system by its _objective consequences_{: .bold}, *not* the majority's _self-justification_{: .warn}.

#### Only EVIDENCE Matters

We must reject arguments from [populism]({{ "/case-studies/politics/democracy/populism/" | relative_url }}) ("The people have spoken!") and [tradition]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#tradition) ("This is how democracies work"). We must look at the [evidence]({{ "/guide/first-principles/evidence/" | relative_url }}).

_What happens to minorities when their fundamental rights are subject to a popular vote?_{: .subtle}

### Step 3: Identify the Objective Consequences - A History of Legalized Oppression

The evidence from history is unequivocal: unchecked majority rule is a direct threat to minorities.

- _The Witch-Hunts (Europe & America, 15th-18th Centuries):_{: .bullet} This is the classic example of a [moral panic]({{ "/case-studies/politics/dysfunction/moral-panics/" | relative_url }}). A widespread, popular belief (an [intersubjective]({{ "/guide/first-principles/intersubjective/" | relative_url }}) [narrative]({{ "/case-studies/narratives/" | relative_url }})) about the existence of witches led to an _horrific outcome_{: .warn}. It wasn't just a lawless mob; it was the [legal system]({{ "/case-studies/law/theory/" | relative_url }}), *supported* by the _majority_{: .warn} of the population, that tried and executed thousands of people (mostly women) based on spectral "evidence" and coerced confessions. The _"will of the people"_{: .bold} was to _purge_{: .warn} a [demonized minority]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#demonisation).

- _Racial Minorities (Jim Crow America):_{: .bullet} The system of apartheid and racial segregation in the American South was not imposed by a dictator. It was enacted and sustained by a [democratic]({{ "/case-studies/politics/democracy/" | relative_url }}) white majority who consistently voted to _deny_{: .warn} basic human and civil rights to the African American minority. The majority *"willed"* _segregation_{: .warn} into existence.

- _Religious Minorities (Countless Examples):_{: .bullet} The persecution of Huguenots in Catholic France, the violent pogroms against Jews in Christian Europe, the oppression of Christians in some modern Muslim-majority nations. In each case, the majority's [religious certainty]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#certainty) was used to _justify_{: .warn} the legal and social _persecution_{: .warn} of a [religious]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#religion) minority.

- _Sexual Minorities (The Criminalisation of Homosexuality):_{: .bullet} For decades, laws criminalizing homosexual acts were overwhelmingly popular and democratically supported in Western nations. The majority's moral disgust was written into the legal code, leading to the imprisonment, blackmail, and ruin of countless individuals. Using [pseudoscience]({{ "/case-studies/pseudoscience/offender-treatment-industry/" | relative_url }}) and [law]({{ "/case-studies/law/theory/" | relative_url }}), the majority _defined_{: .warn} a minority's [sexual attraction]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#sexuality) as [deviance]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#deviant) and a "crime".

### Step 4: Reflect on Human History - The Mismatch of the Tribe

This pattern is a predictable [mismatch]({{ "/case-studies/mismatches/" | relative_url }}). Our [brains]({{ "/case-studies/who-we-are/brain/" | relative_url }}) [evolved]({{ "/guide/human-history/evolution/" | relative_url }}) in small, homogenous hunter-gatherer bands. We are hard-wired with _a powerful instinct_{: .bold} for [tribalism]({{ "/case-studies/politics/political-dynamics/tribalism/" | relative_url }}): a *deep loyalty* to our _in-group_{: .bold} and a corresponding suspicion of the _out-group_{: .bold}.

In a large, diverse [nation-state], this *instinct* is a _disaster_{: .warn}. It allows a majority to easily define a minority—based on race, religion, sexuality, or belief—as the ["out-group"]({{ "/case-studies/us-vs-them/" | relative_url }}). Political leaders and media can then [weaponise]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#weaponise) this *instinct*, using [fear]({{ "/guide/obstacles/fear-mongering/" | relative_url }}) and [moral panic]({{ "/case-studies/politics/dysfunction/moral-panics/" | relative_url }}) to *unite the majority* against the designated *"other"*.

> The **democratic process**, far from preventing this, can become *the very tool used to legitimise the oppression*.

## Conclusion: A Verdict on How to Build a Just Society

If we had to get it right, as if *our freedom* and the [freedom]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#autonomy) of *future generations* depended on it—because they do—what would our conclusion be?

The evidence is overwhelming: pure, unchecked majority rule is a *recipe for* _tyranny_{: .warn}. A [society]({{ "/case-studies/society/social-structures/" | relative_url }}) that stakes its "justice" on the *shifting passions of the mob* is **not** a _free society_{: .bold}.

A rational, just, and stable society is not a pure democracy. It is a _Constitutional Republic_{: .bold} or a [Liberal Democracy]({{ "/case-studies/politics/political-ideas/liberalism/" | relative_url }}). This is a system deliberately designed with _firewalls_{: .bold} to _protect individuals from the tyranny of the group_{: .mark}. The solution is not to abandon democracy, but to *constrain it* with a higher set of principles.

- _Codify and Protect Inalienable Rights:_{: .bullet} A rational society must create a constitution or a charter of rights that places certain fundamental individual liberties—[free speech]({{ "/case-studies/politics/miscelleneous/free-speech/" | relative_url }}), privacy, freedom of conscience, equality before the law, and bodily _autonomy_{: .cool}—*beyond the reach of a temporary majority*. These are not gifts from the majority to the minority; they are the foundational rules that make a civilized society possible for everyone. *You cannot vote away someone's right to exist*.

> The UK does _not_{: .warn} have a _written constitution_{: .bold} or written _charter of rights_{: .bold}. Rights exist **at Parliament's pleasure!?**.

- _Foster a Culture of Critical Thinking:_{: .bullet} Rights on paper are not enough. A society must _actively cultivate_{: .mark} a _culture of skepticism_{: .bold} and [intellectual humility]({{ "/guide/thinking-for-yourself/intellectual-humility/" | relative_url }}). It must teach its citizens to be wary of mob sentiment, to question their own *tribal instincts*, and to evaluate claims based on [evidence]({{ "/guide/first-principles/evidence/" | relative_url }}), not emotional appeal. This is the ultimate *long-term defence* against [witch-hunts]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#witch-trials).

- _An Independent Judiciary:_{: .bullet} There must be an _independent_{: .bold} judiciary with the ability—when absolutely necessary—to strike down laws—even popular, democratically passed laws—that _violate_{: .warn} these fundamental rights. The courts must serve as the ultimate guardians of the constitution against the passions of the moment.

> The goal is not to thwart the *"will of the people"*.

It is to create a system where the "will of the people" is channeled through a framework of reason and firewalls, ensuring that the majority must respect the *dignity* and [freedom]({{ "/case-studies/law/pragmatic/liberty/" | relative_url }}) of even the smallest and most unpopular minority.
