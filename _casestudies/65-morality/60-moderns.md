### Overview

In our exploration of ancient philosophies, we discovered powerful _"cognitive toolkits"_{: .neon} for living a better life. The Stoics gave us a **framework for resilience**, and the Epicureans offered a map to *sustainable happiness*. These were primarily philosophies for individual [well-being]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#wellbeing).

As our societies grew more complex, so did our _moral problems_{: .warn}. The great challenge of _modern ethics_{: .bold} is not just "How should I live?" but "How should we live together?" *How do we design a fair and just society* for millions of diverse people?

Twentieth-century philosophy, armed with the tools of modern logic and political science, developed a new set of **powerful thought experiments** to tackle these questions. These are not complete systems for living, but _analytical instruments_{: .bold} designed to cut through our [biases]({{ "/guide/thinking-for-yourself/cognitive-biases/" | relative_url }}) and force us to think more clearly and _think more clearly_{: .cool} and compassionately about [justice]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#justice-and-injustice).

_Here are two of the most influential and useful tools for our modern toolkit._{: .subtle}

### 1. The Veil of Ignorance: A Machine for Generating Fairness (John Rawls)

The Core Idea: Imagine you and a group of people are tasked with designing the *fundamental principles* of a new society. You get to decide the [rules]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#rules) for everything: the economic system, the [legal rights]({{ "/case-studies/politics/governance/rights/" | relative_url }}), the social safety net. But there's a catch. You are all behind a _"Veil of Ignorance"_{: .neon}.

Behind this veil, you know nothing about who you will be in the [society]({{ "/case-studies/society/social-structures/" | relative_url }}) you are creating. You do not know your race, your gender, your [sexual orientation]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#sexuality), your wealth, your intelligence, your health, your talents, or even your [religious]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#religion), secular or philosophical [beliefs]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#beliefs). You might be born a brilliant, healthy billionaire, or you might be born a severely disabled person in the poorest community.

![Morality]({{ "/assets/images/casestudies/morality/rawls.webp" | relative_url }})

_Now, from this position of radical uncertainty, what kind of society would you design?_{: .subtle}

#### The Toolkit in Action

_The Veil of Ignorance is arguably the most powerful thought experiment ever devised for thinking about justice._{: .mark} It is a machine for forcing [empathy]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#empathy) and **objectivity**.

- _It Obliterates Vested Interests:_{: .bullet} Behind the veil, you can't design a system that benefits your own group, because you don't know which group you'll be in. A man can't design a [patriarchal]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#patriarch-and-patrilineal) society, because he might be a woman. A *rich person* can't design a system with no taxes, because they might be poor.

- _It Forces You to Protect the Most Vulnerable:_{: .bullet} A [rational]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#rational) person, not knowing where they will end up, would not gamble on being a king. They would design a system that is as fair as possible for the person in the _worst possible position_{: .bold}. You would insist on a *strong social safety net*, universal healthcare, and robust protections for minority rights, because you might be the one who needs them most.

- _It Generates a Rational Basis for Rights:_{: .bullet} You would demand *freedom of speech* and religion, because you wouldn't want to be silenced or persecuted for your beliefs, whatever they might be.

The Scientific Connection: The Veil of Ignorance aligns perfectly with a scientific and [evidence-based]({{ "/guide/first-principles/evidence/" | relative_url }}) worldview. It is a tool for removing *personal bias* from the _experimental design_{: .terminal} of a society. It forces us to move beyond our [intersubjective]({{ "/guide/first-principles/intersubjective/" | relative_url }}) loyalties and think in terms of _systemic fairness_{: .bold} and _risk mitigation_{: .cool}.

### 2. The Expanding Circle: A Machine for Generating Compassion (Peter Singer)

- _The Core Idea:_{: .bullet} The philosopher Peter Singer, drawing on the work of earlier historians and psychologists, popularized the concept of the _"Expanding Circle"_{: .neon}. He argues that _the entire history of moral progress can be understood as a slow, halting, but consistent process_{: .mark} of expanding the circle of beings to whom we grant moral consideration.

- _The Starting Point (The Ancestral Circle):_{: .bullet} For most of [human history]({{ "/guide/human-history/" | relative_url }}), our "circle of moral concern" was both deep and narrow. It extended powerfully to our immediate kin-group or band of roughly 25-150 people. People outside that circle were often seen with _suspicion_{: .warn}, as rivals for resources.

- _The Rupture and Rebuilding:_{: .bullet} The [Transition]({{ "/guide/human-history/agricultural-transition/" | relative_url }}) to agriculture and [civilisation]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#civilisations) shattered this [natural]({{ "/guide/human-history/natural-vs-normal/" | relative_url }}) structure. Loyalty was no longer to a *tangible kin-group*, but was demanded by new, abstract entities. The circle of moral concern had to be slowly and painfully rebuilt over millennia, driven by philosophy, trade, and the hard-won recognition of a shared humanity.

  - From the local settlement to the city-state or kingdom.

  - From the kingdom to the [nation-state]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#nation-state) (often with _brutal exclusion_{: .warn} of those outside the national identity).

  - From one's own nation to the concept of universal human rights—the radical, modern attempt to finally extend a baseline of moral consideration to every member of our species.

- _The Current Frontier:_{: .bullet} Singer argues that the expansion is not finished. The logical and moral progression of this trend compels us to ask: should the circle now be expanded to include *non-human animals*? And what about future generations who will be affected by our actions on climate change?

#### The Toolkit in Action

The Expanding Circle is a powerful historical and ethical framework. It provides a _diagnostic tool_{: .terminal} for our own _moral blind spots_{: .warn}.

- _It Identifies Prejudice:_{: .bullet} It allows us to see that arguments used today to deny rights or consideration to certain groups (e.g., animals) are often *eerily similar* to the arguments that were once used to deny rights to other humans (e.g., [slaves]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#slavery), women).

- _It Provides a Direction for Progress:_{: .bullet} It suggests that the arc of moral history is one of _increasing empathy_{: .cool} and inclusivity. It challenges us to ask, "Who is still outside my circle of concern, and why?"

- _It Forces a Global Perspective:_{: .bullet} It is a direct antidote to the "[mismatch]({{ "/case-studies/mismatches/" | relative_url }})" of our tribal [psychology]({{ "/guide/human-history/psychology/" | relative_url }}). It provides a rational [framework for thinking]({{ "/guide/thinking-for-yourself/" | relative_url }}) about our responsibilities not just to our *kin or our nation*, but to the entire planet and its future inhabitants.

- _The Scientific Connection:_{: .bullet} This concept is deeply rooted in an evidence-based, historical understanding of *social change*. It is also consistent with the findings of evolutionary psychology, which recognises both our [innate]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#innate) [tribalism]({{ "/case-studies/politics/political-dynamics/tribalism/" | relative_url }}) and our capacity for reason-driven empathy.

## Conclusion: Tools for Building a Better "Ought"

These modern tools are not [dogmatic]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#dogma) rulebooks. They are **analytical instruments**. The Veil of Ignorance helps us design a *fairer starting point*. The Expanding Circle challenges us to extend our [compassion]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#altruism) more broadly.

They do not give us easy answers, but they force us to ask better, more rational, and more **humane questions**. In the great, ongoing project of building a better _moral framework_{: .cool}—a better "ought" from the "is" of our existence—these thought experiments are among the most valuable and powerful tools we have.

_Thought experiments remove tribal bias to engineer systemic justice._{: .mark}
