### Overview

What is a "*country*"? It is the single most *powerful* and *defining* political entity of the modern world. It commands our loyalty, collects our taxes, sends us to war, and shapes our very *identity* from the moment we are born. We are taught to see the [nation-state]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#nation-state)—with its flags, its borders, and its anthems—as a permanent, [natural]({{ "/guide/human-history/natural-vs-normal/" | relative_url }}), almost [sacred]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#sacred) feature of the human landscape. But from a historical and [scientific perspective]({{ "/guide/first-principles/scientific-method/" | relative_url }}), it is a recent, artificial, and _profoundly radical_{: .bold} [experiment]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#experiment).

![Nation State]({{ "/assets/images/casestudies/politics/nanny-state.webp" | relative_url }})

This case study will *deconstruct* this powerful "imagined community". We will analyse it not as a sacred entity, but as a specific, historically-contingent tool for organizing human society. We will weigh its objective consequences—both the **immense progress** it has enabled and the **catastrophic violence** it has unleashed—and ask a crucial question: Is the nation-state the final and best form of human political organisation, or is it an outdated piece of _software_{: .terminal} that is dangerously mismatched with the challenges of the 21st century?

---

### Step 1: Wipe the Slate Clean - Deconstructing the Nation

Let's set aside our *passports* and our *patriotism*. From a [clean slate]({{ "/guide/clean-slate/" | relative_url }}) perspective, what is a nation-state? When we look at the world without *inherited maps*, what would we see? We wouldn't see the bold, colored lines dividing France from Germany, or the United States from Mexico. We would see a *continuous landscape* of mountains, rivers, and plains, populated by a single, interconnected human species.

- _An "Imagined Community":_{: .bullet} You will never meet, know, or speak to the vast majority of your fellow citizens. The nation-state is a community that exists only in your *collective imagination*, held together by *shared stories* (history), *symbols* (flags), *rituals* (national holidays), and a [belief]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#beliefs) in a common destiny.

- _An Accident of History:_{: .bullet} The modern nation-state is a *European invention*, largely emerging after the Peace of Westphalia in 1648. Its borders are not [natural]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#natural) lines; they are the _scars_{: .warn} of ancient wars, *colonial ambitions*, and political treaties.

- _A Monopoly on Violence:_{: .bullet} At its core, the state is the entity that successfully claims a *monopoly* on the legitimate use of _physical force_{: .bold} within a given territory.

### Step 2: Applying First Principles

#### Testability

Can we [test]({{ "/guide/first-principles/testability/" | relative_url }}) the claims made by nation-states? Yes. When a nation claims to promote [liberty]({{ "/case-studies/law/pragmatic/liberty/" | relative_url }}) and [justice]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#justice-and-injustice) for all", we can measure its incarceration rates and social mobility. When it claims to be a "peaceful nation", we can count the wars it has fought. We can and must test its *founding myths* against its *objective outcomes*.

#### Objective vs. Intersubjective

Is national identity objective? No.

- _Objective:_{: .bullet} *National borders are historical accidents*. The land you call your "sacred motherland" is a *temporary arrangement* of tectonic plates that has existed for a tiny fraction of *geological time*. The fact that a border is an imaginary line on a map that often cuts through cohesive ethnic or ecological regions is an objective, geographical fact.

- _Intersubjective:_{: .bullet} The belief that this line is a *sacred boundary* worth dying for is a powerful [intersubjective]({{ "/guide/first-principles/intersubjective/" | relative_url }}) [narrative]({{ "/case-studies/narratives/" | relative_url }}). The idea of a homogenous, unified "people" is a _historical fiction_{: .warn}.

#### Only EVIDENCE Matters

We must reject the emotional appeals of [nationalism]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#nationalism) and look at the **measurable consequences** of dividing the human species into competing, _territory-based tribes_{: .neon}.

### Examining the Evidence: A Double-Edged Sword

The nation-state has been both a **powerful engine** of human progress and a **catastrophic instrument** of human suffering. A [rational]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#rational) analysis must acknowledge both sides of the ledger.

#### The Positive Consequences

- _Large-Scale Cooperation:_{: .bullet} The nation-state has proven to be an incredibly *effective tool* for organizing millions of people, enabling the creation of _complex infrastructure_{: .cool} (roads, hospitals), legal systems, and *social order* on a massive scale.

- _The Welfare State:_{: .bullet} It has facilitated the creation of _social safety nets_{: .cool}, public [education]({{ "/case-studies/society/social-structures/education/" | relative_url }}), and healthcare systems that have *dramatically improved* the quality of life for billions.

- _Economic Stability:_{: .bullet} It provides the _legal framework_{: .cool}—enforcing contracts, protecting property, issuing currency—necessary for *market economies* to function.

#### The Negative Consequences

- _War and Nationalism:_{: .bullet} The flip side of [in-group]({{ "/case-studies/us-vs-them/" | relative_url }}) loyalty* is *out-group hostility*. The nation-state is the most effective _war-making machine_{: .warn} in [human history]({{ "/guide/human-history/" | relative_url }}). It can convince millions of ordinary people to kill or die for an *abstract symbol* like a flag or a border on a map, or narratives like "national honour" or _sovereignty_{: .neon}.

- _Arbitrary Borders and Divided Peoples:_{: .bullet} National borders are historical accidents, often drawn by colonial powers with no regard for the ethnic, linguistic, or cultural groups on the ground. This has led to endless conflict, _ethnic cleansing_{: .warn}, and the creation of *permanent refugee populations* (e.g., the Kurds).

- _Prioritizing Land Over People:_{: .bullet} Nationalism elevates a piece of land to a sacred status, often prioritizing its "sovereignty" over the [well-being]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#wellbeing) of actual human beings. This leads to _hostile immigration policies_{: .warn} where we let people drown in the sea to "protect our borders", and to *absurd territorial disputes* over uninhabited rocks.

- _Suppression of Internal Diversity:_{: .bullet} To forge a unified "national identity", states have historically [suppressed]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#suppression) minority languages, cultures, and religions. The ideal of a homogenous nation is a [myth]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#myth) used to justify _cultural erasure_{: .warn} and *persecution*.

### The Great Mismatch: Evolved Brains in an Artificial World

The nation-state is the *ultimate* [mismatch]({{ "/case-studies/mismatches/" | relative_url }}) with our evolved [psychology]({{ "/guide/human-history/psychology/" | relative_url }}). For 99.5% of our history as [Homo sapiens]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#modern-humans-homo-sapiens) (and for millions of years as [earlier humans]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#early-humans) and primates), we were wired for loyalty to a *small, tangible kin-group* of around 150 people. Our sense of justice, fairness, and community is scaled for _face-to-face interaction_{: .bold}.

- _The Mismatch of Scale and Loyalty:_{: .bullet} The nation-state demands we override this *natural instinct* and extend our loyalty to millions of strangers and an abstract concept of "the country". It hijacks our evolved _tribal psychology_{: .bold} and scales it up to a monstrous and dangerous level, transforming healthy community spirit into *aggressive* _nationalism_{: .neon}. Just as the [isolated nuclear family]({{ "/case-studies/society/normal-living/family/" | relative_url }}) creates a mismatch that leads to [burnout]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#burnout) and *loneliness*, the nation-state creates a mismatch that leads to *jingoism* and war.

- _The Mismatch of Tribalism:_{: .bullet} It takes our evolved "us vs. them" instinct, which was adapted for *small-group competition*, and scales it up to a monstrous level. This turns healthy community spirit into aggressive nationalism, making global [cooperation]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#cooperation) incredibly difficult. This is the psychological software that powers both world wars and modern political polarisation. Nationalism is a classic example of ["herd morality"]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#the-herd). It provides a sense of purpose and belonging to individuals, making them feel part of something grand and powerful. In reality, it makes them more *obedient*, more *uniform*, and more willing to sacrifice their *individuality* and their lives for the goals of the state—the herd. The "love of your country" becomes a noble-sounding justification for [conformity]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#conformity) and a herd-like hatred of outsiders.

> It is a powerful tool *for those who wish to lead the herd*, not *for those who wish to* [think for themselves]({{ "/guide/thinking-for-yourself/" | relative_url }})

### Alternatives to the Nation-State

If the nation-state is a recent, *flawed invention*, what could replace it? This is where we can engage in some speculative but _rational brainstorming_{: .bold}.

- _Global Governance & Supranational Institutions:_{: .bullet} Are organisations like the United Nations or the European Union a step towards a _post-national world_{: .cool}? We can analyse their *successes* (e.g., preventing major European wars) and *failures* (e.g., bureaucracy, lack of [democratic]({{ "/case-studies/politics/democracy/" | relative_url }}) accountability).

- _City-States & Bioregionalism:_{: .bullet} What if [power]({{ "/case-studies/politics/governance/power/" | relative_url }}) was _decentralized_{: .cool}? Could we organise ourselves into smaller, more manageable units like city-states (like modern-day Singapore) or "bioregions" defined by natural geography (like a watershed) rather than arbitrary political lines? This might align better with our evolved capacity for *smaller-scale governance*.

- _Digital Nomadism & Non-Territorial Citizenship:_{: .bullet} In an increasingly digital world, is citizenship tied to a piece of land becoming *obsolete*? Could we have forms of _digital citizenship_{: .cool} where individuals choose which legal and [social systems]({{ "/case-studies/politics/political-ideas/" | relative_url }}) they want to belong to, regardless of their physical location?

- _A Return to First Principles:_{: .bullet} What problems is the nation-state actually trying to solve? Security, resource allocation, *dispute resolution*. Could we _design new systems_{: .cool} from scratch, using technology and our understanding of [human nature]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#human-nature), to solve these problems more effectively and humanely, without the baggage of nationalism?

## Conclusion: A Verdict Based on a Higher Loyalty

If we had to get it right, as if the _future of humanity_{: .mark} and a peaceful and sustainable world depended on it—because they do—what would our conclusion be?

The nation-state is a _tool, not a destination_{: .mark}. It is an *intersubjective construct* that, while useful in organizing societies (but only long after the [Agricultural Transition]({{ "/guide/human-history/agricultural-transition/" | relative_url }})), is not natural, eternal, or sacred. The [evidence]({{ "/guide/first-principles/evidence/" | relative_url }}) is clear: it has delivered both immense progress and unimaginable catastrophe. Its core logic—dividing humanity into competing, land-based tribes—is a *dangerous anachronism* in an interconnected world facing *global challenges* like climate change, pandemics, and artificial intelligence.

A [scientific worldview]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#scientific-worldview) does not call for a naive abolition of all borders tomorrow. It calls for a _rational and gradual evolution_{: .mark} beyond the limits of the nation-state.

- _Recognise its Artificiality:_{: .bullet} The first step is to demote the nation-state from a sacred entity to a practical (and often flawed) *administrative unit*.

- _Challenge the Narrative of Nationalism:_{: .bullet} We must actively resist the dangerous "us vs. them" logic of nationalism and cultivate a primary identity as *members of the human species*.

- _Strengthen Supranational Institutions:_{: .bullet} We must invest in and reform *global institutions* designed to solve global problems—from climate change to pandemics to [human rights]({{ "/case-studies/politics/governance/rights/" | relative_url }})—that individual nations are incapable of solving alone.

Our *evolutionary inheritance* is not loyalty to a flag or a patch of dirt. It is loyalty to our tribe. And in the world following the [Agricultural Transition]({{ "/guide/human-history/agricultural-transition/" | relative_url }}), that tribe has to be our species. Our capacity for [empathy]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#empathy) and cooperation does not stop at a border drawn on a map. The great political challenge of the 21st century is to finally scale that cooperation up to the level of the entire species, recognizing that our loyalty must ultimately be to people, not to the *imaginary lines* we have drawn between them.

Therefore, the only rational and ethical path forward is to recognise the nation-state for what it is: a _flawed and temporary stage in human social evolution_{: .mark}. We should strive for its peaceful and gradual replacement with systems that are better aligned with both our evolved nature and our future challenges.

We should be organized into communities working together as humanity. The goal is not a world government, but a world of interconnected, cooperative, and smaller-scale communities that reflect our human nature and prioritise human well-being over national glory. Our loyalty must be to people, not to land; to our species, not to our state.

_Our loyalty must be to people, not to land; to our species, not to our state._{: .subtle}

### PS "Dying For Your Country?"

During World War Two, I would **not** have willingly given my life for my country. The idea is anathema, for the reasons given in the conclusion above. However, **I would have** given my life (if necessary, but hopefully not!) to **fight fascism**, one of the worst intersubjective narratives that human *ideology* has invented. Likewise, I would resist any intersubjective narrative that *does more harm than good*.

_Humanity must come before the state, and people before borders.{: .mark}_
