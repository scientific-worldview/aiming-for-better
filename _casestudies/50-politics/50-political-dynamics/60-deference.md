### Authoritarian Followers: The Psychology of Deference

In [democratic]({{ "/case-studies/politics/democracy/" | relative_url }}) societies, we value [critical thinking]({{ "/guide/thinking-for-yourself/" | relative_url }}), individual [autonomy]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#autonomy), and *the ability to question* [authority]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#authority). The *prevailing* [narrative]({{ "/case-studies/narratives/" | relative_url }}) suggests that citizens *freely choose* their [political systems]({{ "/case-studies/politics/political-ideas/" | relative_url }}) and leaders, and that *authoritarian regimes* are primarily imposed through force or deception. When individuals support authoritarian figures or movements, it's often attributed to a lack of [education]({{ "/case-studies/society/social-structures/education/" | relative_url }}), economic hardship, or susceptibility to [propaganda]({{ "/guide/human-history/propaganda/" | relative_url }}).

![Deference]({{ "/assets/images/casestudies/politics/deference.webp" | relative_url }})

This case study will argue that, from a [scientific worldview]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#scientific-worldview), the phenomenon of _Authoritarian Followers_{: .neon} is rooted in *identifiable psychological predispositions* that make certain individuals more susceptible to authoritarian appeals, particularly in times of perceived threat or [uncertainty]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#certainty). This represents a powerful "Psychological [Lock-In]({{ "/case-studies/locked-in/" | relative_url }})", where traits like a *strong need for order*, conventionalism, and *aggression towards out-groups* lead to a preference for strong, decisive leaders and a **rigid, hierarchical social structure**. It highlights a fundamental "[Mismatch]({{ "/case-studies/mismatches/" | relative_url }})" between these deep-seated psychological needs and the demands of complex, pluralistic, and open [democratic]({{ "/case-studies/politics/democracy/" | relative_url }}) societies that require [tolerance]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#tolerance) for ambiguity, respect for diverse viewpoints, and constant questioning of [power]({{ "/case-studies/politics/governance/power/" | relative_url }}). We will explore the characteristics of authoritarian followers, the conditions that activate these tendencies, and the **profound dangers** they pose to democratic institutions and civil liberties.

---

### Background Information

The presence of individuals predisposed to authoritarianism has been observed across history:

- _Ancient Empires & Absolute Monarchy:_{: .bullet} Throughout much of human history, deferential [obedience]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#obedience) to absolute rulers, priests, or tribal chiefs was the [norm]({{ "/case-studies/society/sociology/norms/" | relative_url }}), often seen as *divinely sanctioned* and *essential for order*.

- _The Rise of Fascism (20th Century):_{: .bullet} The horrors of Nazi Germany and other totalitarian regimes in the mid-20th century *spurred academic inquiry* into the "authoritarian personality". Researchers like Theodor Adorno sought to understand the psychological underpinnings of individuals who readily embraced *extreme obedience* and intolerance.

- _Cold War & Social Science:_{: .bullet} Post-WWII, research into authoritarianism expanded, seeking to understand why some individuals exhibit a cluster of traits including conventionalism, authoritarian submission, and authoritarian aggression (Altemeyer's Right-Wing Authoritarianism - RWA scale).

- _Modern Resurgence:_{: .bullet} In the 21st century, with rising global anxieties (*economic instability*, terrorism, mass migration, pandemics), and the emergence of populist strongmen, the concept of the authoritarian follower has gained *renewed relevance*, as these psychological predispositions are increasingly activated.

_This history demonstrates that while specific authoritarian regimes rise and fall, the underlying psychological susceptibility to authoritarian appeals is a consistent human variable that can be activated under specific societal conditions._{: .subtle}

### Step 1: Wipe the Slate Clean

Let’s *strip away* the simplistic explanations for why people support authoritarian figures. We begin with a [clean slate]({{ "/guide/clean-slate/" | relative_url }}) to examine the *fundamental psychological needs* and predispositions that drive [deference]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#deference) and a preference for *rigid order*.

#### The Problem

Societies need **order, stability**, and mechanisms for decision-making. Individuals need to *feel secure* and have a sense of [meaning]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#meaning) and belonging.

#### The Stated Need

Democracies require citizens who are *critically engaged*, *tolerant of ambiguity*, and willing to defend individual liberties.

#### The Prevailing Narrative

- _Elitist View:_{: .bullet} Authoritarian followers are *uneducated* or *unsophisticated masses* easily manipulated.

- _Authoritarian View:_{: .bullet} They are *patriotic citizens* who desire strength and [traditional]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#tradition) [values]({{ "/case-studies/society/sociology/values/" | relative_url }}).

These narratives fail to address the deeper, often subconscious, _psychological architecture_{: .bold} that predisposes certain individuals to embrace authoritarian structures, often as a *response to perceived chaos* or threat.

### Step 2: Applying First Principles

#### Testability

The existence and characteristics of authoritarian followers are highly [testable]({{ "/guide/first-principles/testability/" | relative_url }}) through psychological and [sociological]({{ "/case-studies/society/sociology/" | relative_url }}) research:

- _Psychometric Scales:_{: .bullet} [Standardized]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#standardisation) psychological scales (e.g., Altemeyer's RWA scale, Doty's Authoritarianism scale) *objectively measure* the cluster of traits associated with authoritarianism across populations.

- _Correlational Studies:_{: .bullet} Researchers can *objectively correlate* high scores on authoritarianism scales with specific political attitudes (e.g., intolerance of dissent, support for harsh punishments, prejudice), [voting]({{ "/case-studies/politics/democracy/boundary-of-the-ballot/" | relative_url }}) behaviour, and susceptibility to certain types of rhetoric.

- _Experimental Studies:_{: .bullet} Lab experiments can *objectively test* how perceived threat or uncertainty activates authoritarian tendencies, leading to *increased support* for aggressive policies or deference to authority figures.

- _Neuroscience:_{: .bullet} [Brain]({{ "/case-studies/who-we-are/brain/" | relative_url }}) imaging can *objectively show* how individuals with authoritarian predispositions process information related to social threat, norm violations, and out-groups.

- _Cross-Cultural Research:_{: .bullet} Studies across different cultures can *objectively reveal* the universality or cultural specificity of authoritarian psychological traits and their political manifestations.

#### Objective vs. Intersubjective

- _Objective:_{: .bullet} The objective fact that individuals scoring high on RWA scales are more likely to support capital punishment, hold prejudiced views, or favour restrictions on civil liberties is verifiable. The measurable [correlation]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#correlation) between perceived societal threat and increased support for strongman leaders is objective.

- _Intersubjective:_{: .bullet} The [belief]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#beliefs) that "order and traditional values must be restored at all costs", that "strong leaders know what's best", or that "dissent undermines national unity" are powerful [intersubjective]({{ "/guide/first-principles/intersubjective/" | relative_url }}) narratives within authoritarian mindsets. These beliefs provide a *coherent framework* for understanding the world, but they must be critically examined against [objective evidence]({{ "/guide/first-principles/evidence/" | relative_url }}) of their impact on [individual rights]({{ "/case-studies/politics/governance/rights/" | relative_url }}), societal [well-being]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#wellbeing), and the long-term health of democratic institutions.

#### Only EVIDENCE Matters

_We must move beyond moral condemnation and objectively analyse the psychological predispositions that make individuals receptive to authoritarian appeals._{: .subtle}

- What are the *quantifiable psychological traits* that reliably predict support for authoritarian leaders and policies?

- Under what *objective societal conditions* (e.g., economic stress, perceived external threat, cultural change) do these traits become more politically salient?

- What are the *measurable consequences* of having a significant segment of the population with high authoritarian predispositions on democratic health, civil liberties, and social cohesion?

### Step 3: The Real-World Cost: The Psychology of Blind Obedience

When we look closely at the [psychology]({{ "/guide/human-history/psychology/" | relative_url }}) of authoritarian followers, we see a _terrifying dynamic_{: .warn}. A deep, fundamental psychological need for order, certainty, and strong leadership naturally drives people to support systems that actively crush individual [freedom]({{ "/case-studies/law/pragmatic/liberty/" | relative_url }}) and critical thought.

#### Unquestioning Obedience

People with high authoritarian tendencies have a profound urge to submit to whoever they perceive as the "legitimate" authority. They will _follow a leader’s commands without asking questions, even when those commands openly violate democratic norms_{: .mark} or basic ethics.

#### The Desire to Punish

This mindset also breeds _intense aggression_{: .warn} toward anyone who breaks the [rules]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#rules) or belongs to an "[out-group]({{ "/case-studies/us-vs-them/" | relative_url }})." These followers readily embrace—and actively demand—*harsh, unforgiving penalties* against dissidents, [minorities]({{ "/case-studies/us-vs-them/majorities-vs-minorities/" | relative_url }}), or opposing political factions.

#### Fear of Change

Authoritarian followers *cling fiercely* to traditional values and social conventions. They view any deviation from the norm not just as different, but as a **literal threat**. This makes them fiercely resistant to progressive social movements, cultural [diversity]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#diversity), and even scientific advancements that challenge their [worldview]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#worldview).

#### Craving Absolute Certainty

The real world is deeply complex, but authoritarian minds have an incredibly _low tolerance_{: .bold} for ambiguity. They crave *clear, black-and-white answers*. This discomfort makes them highly susceptible to demagogues who offer *simple, forceful (and often false) solutions* to complicated problems.

#### In-Group Purity and Prejudice

This psychology is heavily rooted in [nationalism]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#nationalism) and ethnocentrism. Outsiders are viewed as an active threat to the group’s cohesion and traditional way of life, a [fear]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#fear) that reliably fuels xenophobia, racism, and _systemic discrimination_{: .warn} .

#### Rejecting the Experts

Because they crave simple narratives and **absolute loyalty**, authoritarian followers develop a profound distrust of intellectuals, scientists, and independent journalists. Anyone who introduces [uncomfortable]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#uncomfortable-truths) complexities, reports objective [facts]({{ "/guide/first-principles/correct-answers/" | relative_url }}), or challenges the established leader is immediately viewed as an *enemy*.

#### The Overall Consequence

Ultimately, the result is a [society]({{ "/case-studies/society/social-structures/" | relative_url }}) where a significant chunk of the population actively wants fewer personal freedoms, demands strict social [conformity]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#conformity), and craves an unchallengeable leader. This _underlying psychology creates a devastating lock-in—a deep, structural vulnerability_{: .mark} that paves the exact road for authoritarian movements to dismantle democracy from the inside.

### Step 4: The Human Element: Why Democracy Feels Unnatural to Some

The mindset of an authoritarian follower isn't just a political choice; it exposes a **deep friction** between our ancient need for order and the messy, demanding reality of a *free society*. Democracy requires us to tolerate ambiguity, respect diverse viewpoints, and constantly question power—things that our ancient brains often perceive as *dangerous*.

#### The Safety of the Tribe vs. A Diverse World

In our [evolutionary]({{ "/guide/human-history/" | relative_url }}) past, strictly following tribal rules and deferring to a single strong leader is what kept [ny]({{ "/case-studies/society/social-structures/the-clan/" | relative_url }}) safe from predators and rival tribes. But in a modern, interconnected, and diverse society, demanding _rigid conformity_{: .bold} and treating all outsiders with suspicion is entirely *counterproductive*. It directly clashes with our modern need for global tolerance and [cooperation]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#cooperation).

#### Craving Simple Answers in a Complex World

Our brains naturally crave order and simple explanations. However, modern societies are *inherently complex*, dynamic, and full of gray areas. The authoritarian follower's deep-seated _psychological need_{: .bold} for things to be simple and orderly makes them actively reject nuanced, evidence-based solutions. Instead, they flock to strongman rhetoric that promises *impossible, easy fixes*.

#### Blind Obedience vs. Holding Power Accountable

_A healthy democracy relies entirely on citizens who are willing to critically examine policies_{: .mark}, challenge authority, and hold their leaders accountable. The authoritarian’s [natural]({{ "/guide/human-history/natural-vs-normal/" | relative_url }}) impulse to simply bow to whoever is in charge completely *short-circuits this process*, dismantling the very mechanisms that keep democratic power in check.

#### Rigid Beliefs vs. Evolving Knowledge

Authoritarianism relies heavily on the comforting idea of a fixed, _unchangeable truth_{: .bold}—whether traditional, [religious]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#religion), or [ideological]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#ideology). But modern science and democratic debate thrive on the exact opposite: *questioning assumptions* and constantly adapting to new evidence. This creates a *severe friction* between a rigid mind and the constant evolution of human knowledge.

#### The Predictable Result

Ultimately, the presence of authoritarian followers is a completely [natural]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#natural) leftover from our evolutionary heritage. But when these ancient [survival]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#survival) instincts are triggered in the modern political arena, they create a _profound mismatch_{: .bold}—a _dangerous biological trap_{: .warn} that makes our societies highly vulnerable to dictators and deeply threatens the foundations of an open democracy.

## Conclusion: A Verdict on Nurturing Autonomy and Challenging Fear

If we had to get it right, as if the *resilience of our democracies* and the protection of individual liberties depended on it—because it does—what would our conclusion be?

The phenomenon of **"Authoritarian Followers"** is *not merely a matter of political preference* but is rooted in identifiable psychological predispositions, characterized by a need for order, conventionalism, and aggression towards out-groups. These traits create a powerful deference _Lock-In_{: .neon}, leading to a preference for strong, decisive leaders and rigid social structures, particularly in times of perceived threat. This fundamental "Mismatch" between these psychological needs and the demands of open, pluralistic societies makes democracies vulnerable to anti-liberal movements and the erosion of civil liberties.

- _It's a Deep Psychological Trait:_{: .bullet} We must abandon the naive belief that everyone equally values critical thinking and individual _autonomy_{: .cool}. Some individuals have a strong psychological need for order and deference.

- _Threat Activates Deference:_{: .bullet} Ignoring the "Mismatch" between the need for security and the demands of open society makes us vulnerable when perceived threats activate authoritarian tendencies.

- _Autonomy is a Choice and a Skill:_{: .bullet} Nurturing democratic resilience requires actively cultivating critical thinking, tolerance for ambiguity, and individual _autonomy_{: .cool}, especially in challenging times.

A [scientific approach]({{ "/guide/first-principles/scientific-method/" | relative_url }}) to building *resilient democracies* requires understanding these predispositions and actively cultivating societal conditions and individual capacities that resist authoritarian appeals:

- _Reduce Perceived Threat & Uncertainty:_{: .bullet} Implement policies that genuinely address societal anxieties (economic insecurity, social disruption, real threats) through _effective, evidence-based governance_{: .cool}, thereby reducing the psychological conditions that activate authoritarian predispositions.

- _Cultivate Critical Thinking & Media Literacy:_{: .bullet} Provide widespread education that teaches individuals to critically evaluate information, question authority, identify cognitive [biases]({{ "/guide/thinking-for-yourself/cognitive-biases/" | relative_url }}), and embrace complexity, directly challenging the "Certainty Lock-In" and "Simplified Reality Lock-In".

- _Foster Tolerance for Ambiguity & Diversity:_{: .bullet} Promote educational and social environments that encourage exposure to diverse viewpoints, celebrate cultural pluralism, and teach the value of nuanced thinking, directly countering the "Mismatch of Simple Order".

- _Strengthen Democratic Norms & Institutions:_{: .bullet} Build _robust democratic institutions_{: .cool} and reinforce norms that protect civil liberties, minority rights, and the rule of [law]({{ "/case-studies/law/theory/" | relative_url }}), making it harder for leaders to [exploit]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#exploitation) deference or suppress dissent.

- _Encourage Independent Thought:_{: .bullet} Create cultural spaces that reward _intellectual courage_{: .cool}, critical inquiry, and independent judgment, rather than mere conformity or obedience.

- _Leadership That Values Collaboration & Evidence:_{: .bullet} Support and promote leaders who embody democratic values – who seek collaboration, rely on expert advice and evidence, respect institutional processes, and build consensus, rather than demanding unquestioning loyalty.

- _Address Economic & Social Injustice:_{: .bullet} Feelings of [injustice]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#justice-and-injustice) and marginalisation can exacerbate feelings of threat and fuel a desire for radical change or strong leadership. Addressing these through equitable policies can reduce susceptibility.

Ultimately, the challenge of "Authoritarian Followers" is to *recognise and strategically mitigate* a powerful psychological force. It requires *consciously designing societies* and cultivating citizens who are equipped to navigate complexity, embrace freedom, and defend democracy, even when faced with the primal comfort of *unquestioning deference* to a strong leader.

_True democratic resilience requires cultivating individual autonomy over blind deference to power._{: .mark}
