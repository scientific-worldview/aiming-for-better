### Political Tribalism: Our Deepest Instinct

In modern democracies, we often idealise politics as a *rational* marketplace of ideas, where citizens objectively weigh policies and elect leaders based on *merit*. The prevailing [narrative]({{ "/case-studies/narratives/" | relative_url }}) suggests that political differences are primarily about policy preferences, economic models, or competing visions for society, and that informed deliberation should lead to compromise and [consensus]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#consensus). When divisions become acrimonious, it's often attributed to _extremism_{: .warn}, [misinformation]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#misinformation), or **individual moral failings**.

![Tribalism]({{ "/assets/images/casestudies/politics/tribalism.webp" | relative_url }})

This case study will argue that, from a [scientific worldview]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#scientific-worldview), modern political [behaviour]({{ "/guide/human-history/psychology/" | relative_url }}) is profoundly shaped by "_Political Tribalism_{: .neon}", a deep-seated, [evolved instinct]({{ "/guide/human-history/evolution/" | relative_url }}) for [in-group]({{ "/case-studies/us-vs-them/" | relative_url }}) loyalty and *out-group suspicion*. This is a powerful "Social [Lock-In]({{ "/case-studies/locked-in/" | relative_url }})" that often overrides *rational self-interest* and [objective evidence]({{ "/guide/first-principles/evidence/" | relative_url }}). It represents a fundamental "[Mismatch]({{ "/case-studies/mismatches/" | relative_url }})" between our ancient, small-group social [psychology]({{ "/guide/human-history/psychology/" | relative_url }}) and the demands of complex, large-scale, **pluralistic societies**. We will explore how these tribal instincts manifest in contemporary politics – from hyper-partisanship to identity politics, from [moral outrage]({{ "/case-studies/politics/political-dynamics/moral-outrage/" | relative_url }}) to the [demonisation]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#demonisation) of opponents. Understanding political [tribalism]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#tribalism) as an evolved human tendency, rather than simply a regrettable deviation, is crucial for diagnosing the roots of modern political _dysfunction_{: .bold} and designing systems that can better channel or mitigate its destructive potential.

---

### Background Information

The roots of tribalism are as old as humanity itself:

- _Hunter-Gatherer Bands:_{: .bullet} For most of [human history]({{ "/guide/human-history/" | relative_url }}), our ancestors lived in small, kin-based, highly cohesive [groups]({{ "/case-studies/society/social-structures/the-clan/" | relative_url }}). [Survival]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#survival) depended on *fierce loyalty* to the in-group and suspicion, often _hostility_{: .warn}, towards out-groups (other tribes competing for resources).

- _Early Agricultural Societies:_{: .bullet} The [Agricultural Transition]({{ "/guide/human-history/agricultural-transition/" | relative_url }}) to larger, more settled societies brought new forms of social organisation, but *in-group/out-group dynamics* persisted, often formalized into clans, castes, or early national identities.

- _Religious & Ethnic Conflicts:_{: .bullet} Throughout recorded history, many of the most *brutal conflicts* have been driven by deep-seated tribal loyalties disguised as [religious]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#religion), ethnic, or nationalistic differences. The [Myth vs. Myth](/) series directly explores this.

- _The Rise of Nation-States:_{: .bullet} The modern [nation-state]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#nation-state), while ostensibly uniting disparate groups under a common banner, often simply scaled up the tribal impulse, defining a larger "in-group" (the nation) against other nations.

- _Modern Political Parties:_{: .bullet} In democracies, political parties have increasingly become proxies for tribal identity, embodying the *"us vs. them"* dynamic, often more about group belonging and loyalty than policy nuance.

- _Digital Age Amplification:_{: .bullet} Social media and personalized news feeds (as we might discuss in "The Fourth Estate") have created powerful [echo chambers]({{ "/case-studies/politics/dysfunction/echo-chambers/" | relative_url }}), inadvertently amplifying _tribal divisions_{: .bold} and reinforcing in-group narratives.

_This history shows that the tribal impulse is a foundational element of human social organisation, consistently re-emerging even in seemingly sophisticated modern contexts._{: .subtle}

### Step 1: Wipe the Slate Clean

Let’s strip away the idealisation of *rational civic engagement* and the assumption that political affiliation is purely a matter of *conscious choice*. We start with a [clean slate]({{ "/guide/clean-slate/" | relative_url }}) to examine the fundamental drivers of group loyalty.

#### The Problem

Humans live in groups and need mechanisms for [cooperation]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#cooperation) and defence. Modern societies are large, diverse, and require cooperation among *millions of individuals* who do not know each other personally.

#### The Stated Need

[Political systems]({{ "/case-studies/politics/political-ideas/" | relative_url }}) should facilitate *rational deliberation*, fair competition among ideas, and the **peaceful resolution** of differences for the benefit of all citizens.

#### The Prevailing Narrative

Political parties are *voluntary associations* based on shared principles; citizens choose parties based on self-interest and policy alignment.

This narrative often overlooks the *subconscious, emotional, and identity-driven forces* that bind individuals to political groups and fuel antagonism towards opposing ones.

---

### Step 2: Applying First Principles

#### Testability

The existence and effects of political tribalism are highly [testable]({{ "/guide/first-principles/testability/" | relative_url }}) through various scientific disciplines:

- _Social Psychology Experiments:_{: .bullet} Lab experiments can objectively measure *in-group favoritism*, out-group derogation, and [conformity]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#conformity) to group [norms]({{ "/case-studies/society/sociology/norms/" | relative_url }}) in political contexts (e.g., "minimal group paradigm").

- _Neuroscience:_{: .bullet} [Brain]({{ "/case-studies/who-we-are/brain/" | relative_url }}) imaging can objectively reveal the neurological correlates of in-group loyalty and out-group threat perception, showing how *emotions often precede* [rational thought]({{ "/guide/thinking-for-yourself/" | relative_url }}) *in political judgments*.

- _Behavioural Economics:_{: .bullet} Studies can objectively demonstrate how individuals will make *economically irrational decisions* to benefit their political in-group or _harm_{: .warn} an out-group.

- _Survey Data & Public Opinion:_{: .bullet} Longitudinal surveys can objectively track the rise of _affective polarisation_{: .bold}, partisan sorting, and the willingness to believe or disbelieve information based on its source's political alignment.

- _Historical & Cross-Cultural Analysis:_{: .bullet} Comparing different political systems and historical periods can objectively reveal how varying institutional designs either mitigate or *exacerbate tribal dynamics*.

#### Objective vs. Intersubjective

- _Objective:_{: .bullet} The objective fact that individuals are more likely to [vote]({{ "/case-studies/politics/democracy/boundary-of-the-ballot/" | relative_url }}) for their party's candidate *regardless of policy stance*, that people will consume news that confirms their pre-existing political views, or that a specific policy receives different approval ratings depending on whether it's attributed to "our party" or "their party", are all verifiable.

- _Intersubjective:_{: .bullet} The [belief]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#beliefs) that "our party is inherently good" and "their party is inherently evil", that "we are fighting for righteousness", or that "our opponents are malicious and stupid" are powerful [intersubjective]({{ "/guide/first-principles/intersubjective/" | relative_url }}) narratives. These beliefs are often core to political tribalism, serving to rationalise in-group loyalty and _dehumanise_{: .warn} the out-group, but they must be critically examined against objective evidence of shared humanity and complex motivations.

#### Only EVIDENCE Matters

We must move beyond self-congratulatory narratives about our own group's virtue and the assumed malice of opponents. We must look at the **measurable impact** of tribal psychology on political outcomes.

- Does increased tribal loyalty lead to more *effective governance* or greater dysfunction?

- Are policy decisions based on objective evidence, or are they *filtered and distorted* by partisan identity?

- What are the quantifiable costs of political tribalism in terms of social cohesion, _policy gridlock_{: .bold}, and the erosion of [democratic]({{ "/case-studies/politics/democracy/" | relative_url }}) norms?

### Step 3: The Real-World Cost: How Political Tribalism Breaks Down Society

When we look closely at political tribalism, we see how it acts as a _massive social trap_{: .mark}. It profoundly distorts rational government, amplifies division, and completely erodes our ability to solve problems together.

#### Hating the Other Side

Tribalism leads to what political scientists call "*affective polarization*"—a state where disliking and distrusting the opposing political party becomes far more important than actual disagreements over policy. When pure animosity toward the other side becomes the primary goal, compromise is viewed as _betrayal_{: .warn}, making cooperation almost impossible.

#### Living in Different Realities

People naturally seek out information that *confirms their existing views* and actively ignore [facts]({{ "/guide/first-principles/correct-answers/" | relative_url }}) that challenge them. This creates deep filter bubbles and echo chambers. When each political tribe essentially lives in a completely different factual reality, having a shared, evidence-based debate is off the table.

#### The Trap of Moral Outrage

Within these political tribes, members constantly compete to prove their loyalty and **moral purity**. They do this by expressing extreme outrage against the opposing side, or by attacking anyone in their own group who seems insufficiently loyal. This relentless *virtue-signaling* escalates rhetoric, punishes nuanced thinking, and drives both sides toward _extremism_{: .bold}.

#### Party Brand Over Practical Solutions

When political identity is everything, policy decisions are no longer made based on whether a solution actually works. Instead, politicians and voters support or reject ideas based purely on whether they align with their *party’s "brand"*—or if they can be used as a weapon against the opposition. This guarantees *chronic gridlock* and leaves pressing societal problems totally unsolved.

#### Dehumanizing the Opposition

Tribalism fundamentally relies on viewing the other side not just as wrong, but as "other"—*morally corrupt*, unintelligent, or genuinely dangerous. This deeply ingrained habit strips away human [empathy]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#empathy) and justifies _toxic tactics_{: .warn}, completely poisoning [democratic]({{ "/case-studies/politics/democracy/" | relative_url }}) discourse.

#### Winning at the Expense of Democracy

When political conflict is [framed]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#framing) as an **existential fight** for [survival]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#survival), groups start prioritizing "winning" over basic democratic rules. Respecting election results, ensuring a *peaceful transfer of power*, and treating opponents as legitimate are suddenly cast aside in the name of victory, threatening the very foundations of democracy itself.

#### The Overall Consequence

The ultimate result is a fractured political system incapable of rational thought. When the ancient human impulse to protect "us" against "them" takes over, it creates a _vicious, self-sustaining cycle_{: .mark} of escalating conflict that prevents any meaningful progress for the collective good.

### Step 4: Reflect on Human History – Small-Tribe Instincts in Mass Politics

Political tribalism reflects a deep tension between our *small-group evolutionary instincts* and the realities of large, diverse, modern societies. Our psychology was shaped for life in small tribes, but our political systems now operate at national and global scale. Many of today’s political failures follow directly from this gap.

#### Small-Group Loyalty vs. Large-Scale Pluralism

Human social instincts evolved for tight-knit groups where loyalty, shared identity, and *mutual obligation* were essential for survival. Modern societies require cooperation across millions of strangers with different [values]({{ "/case-studies/society/sociology/values/" | relative_url }}) and backgrounds. Yet we still apply small-tribe loyalty [rules]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#rules) to political parties and ideologies, producing suspicion, _factionalism_{: .bold}, and conflict at scale.

#### Scarcity Thinking vs. Complex Economies

In ancestral settings, many conflicts really were **zero-sum** — if one group gained, another lost. Modern economies are more complex and often allow mutual gains. Political tribalism, however, still frames many issues as *winner-takes-all* struggles, pushing groups toward competition instead of cooperative solutions.

#### Personal Reputation vs. Anonymous Mass Politics

In small groups, reputation was visible and consequences were immediate. In modern mass politics — especially online — people can signal extreme loyalty or hostility with little personal cost. This reduces accountability and rewards *performative outrage* over careful reasoning.

#### Ancient Threat Responses vs. Modern Systemic Risks

Our threat detection systems evolved to react to immediate dangers like raids or rival groups. Today’s major risks — such as climate change, pandemics, and systemic [inequality]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#inequality) — are slower, more complex, and require cross-group coordination. Tribal politics oversimplifies these challenges into _blame narratives_{: .bold} instead of shared problem-solving.

#### “Us vs Them” Instincts vs. Shared Humanity

Tribal psychology automatically divides the world into insiders and outsiders. Ethical and scientific perspectives emphasise shared human interests and **interdependence**. Political tribalism continually redraws dividing lines, *even where cooperation would clearly produce better outcomes*.

Taken together, political tribalism is not a modern invention but an ancient inheritance. When amplified by [mass media]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#mass-media) and large-scale political systems, it weakens cooperation, distorts judgment, and makes evidence-based democratic governance much harder to achieve.

## Conclusion: A Verdict on Cultivating a "Super-Identity"

If we had to get it right, as if the ability of our societies to solve existential challenges and maintain peace depended on it—because it does—what would our conclusion be?

"Political Tribalism" is not a superficial flaw but a deep-seated, evolved instinct that functions as a powerful "_Social Lock-In_{: .neon}" in modern politics. It represents a fundamental "Mismatch" between our small-group evolutionary psychology and the demands of large-scale, pluralistic governance. The objective consequences are *profound*: affective polarisation, informational echo chambers, policy gridlock, _dehumanisation_{: .warn}, and the erosion of democratic norms. Left unchecked, it threatens the very capacity of societies to function rationally and cohesively.

- _It's Instinct, Not Just Ideology:_{: .bullet} We must abandon the naive belief that political differences are purely rational disagreements. They are often rooted in *emotional, tribal identities*.

- _The Mismatch is Destructive:_{: .bullet} Ignoring the "Mismatch" between our ancient social wiring and modern societal needs leads to *predictable conflict* and dysfunction.

- _Universalism is the Antidote:_{: .bullet} Countering tribalism requires consciously cultivating a broader "_super-identity_{: .neon}" that transcends narrow group loyalties.

A [scientific approach]({{ "/guide/first-principles/scientific-method/" | relative_url }}) to mitigating political tribalism demands systemic and cultural interventions that acknowledge its power while consciously working to build bridges and foster a shared civic identity:

- _Foster a "Super-Ordinate Identity":_{: .bullet} Actively promote a _shared national identity_{: .cool} (or civic/global identity) that transcends partisan or ethnic divides, emphasizing common challenges and shared values (e.g., "we are all citizens", "we are all humans facing climate change").

- _Institutional Reforms to Reduce Polarisation:_{: .bullet} Implement _electoral system reforms_{: .cool} (e.g., ranked-choice voting, multi-member districts) that reward moderation and compromise rather than purity and extremism.

- _Promote Inter-Group Contact:_{: .bullet} Create opportunities for _equal-status interaction_{: .cool} between individuals from different political groups, which evidence shows can reduce prejudice and increase empathy.

- _Cultivate Media Literacy & Critical Thinking:_{: .bullet} Educate citizens to _critically evaluate information_{: .cool}, identify [cognitive biases]({{ "/guide/thinking-for-yourself/cognitive-biases/" | relative_url }}), and resist the allure of emotionally manipulative, tribal narratives, especially online. This builds on "The Fourth Estate" discussions.

- _Civic Education Focused on Shared Values & Norms:_{: .bullet} Reinvigorate civic [education]({{ "/case-studies/society/social-structures/education/" | relative_url }}) that emphasises the shared norms of democratic discourse, _respectful disagreement_{: .cool}, and the importance of objective evidence over partisan loyalty.

- _Leaders Who Bridge Divides:_{: .bullet} Encourage and reward political leaders who consciously work to _unite rather than divide_{: .cool}, who articulate a shared vision, and who model respectful engagement with opponents.

- _Digital Platform Responsibility:_{: .bullet} Advocate for _social media platforms_{: .terminal} to redesign _algorithms_{: .terminal} that currently amplify outrage and division, instead promoting constructive dialogue and diverse information exposure.

Ultimately, addressing political tribalism is about recognizing and deliberately counteracting one of our deepest evolutionary impulses. It requires a conscious effort to build a shared future by transcending the ancient divides, cultivating a broader sense of "us", and embracing a _universalist perspective_{: .bold} grounded in shared humanity and objective reality.

_We must outgrow our tribal past to secure a shared human future.{: .mark}_
