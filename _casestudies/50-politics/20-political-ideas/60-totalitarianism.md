### Overview of The Ultimate Myth Lock-In

Fascism and totalitarianism represent [political systems]({{ "/case-studies/politics/political-ideas/" | relative_url }}) characterized by an *extreme concentration of* [power]({{ "/case-studies/politics/governance/power/" | relative_url }}) *in a single party or leader*, the *absolute* [suppression]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#suppression) *of dissent*, and *total control over all aspects of public and private life*. Unlike traditional authoritarianism, which may seek to *maintain a* [status quo]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#status-quo), totalitarian regimes are driven by a radical, transformative ideology aimed at fundamentally reshaping [society]({{ "/case-studies/society/social-structures/" | relative_url }}) and [human nature]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#human-nature) *itself*. The prevailing [narrative]({{ "/case-studies/narratives/" | relative_url }}) in such systems is one of absolute unity, collective purpose, national (or racial) destiny, and the "infallible wisdom" of the leader or party. It promises order, strength, and a transcendent [meaning]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#meaning), often in the face of *perceived national decline* or *external threats*.

![Fascism]({{ "/assets/images/casestudies/politics/fascism.webp" | relative_url }})

This case study will argue that, from a [scientific worldview]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#scientific-worldview), Fascism and Totalitarianism are the ultimate examples of a "Myth [Lock-In]({{ "/case-studies/locked-in/" | relative_url }})", where a powerful, all-encompassing [intersubjective]({{ "/guide/first-principles/intersubjective/" | relative_url }}) narrative completely dominates and eventually _replaces objective reality_{: .bold}. These ideologies _systematically suppress_{: .bold} [evidence]({{ "/guide/first-principles/evidence/" | relative_url }}), individual *reason*, and *dissenting viewpoints*, creating *a closed loop* that becomes *self-justifying and impervious to correction*. This process exploits [evolved]({{ "/guide/human-history/evolution/" | relative_url }}) human tendencies towards [tribalism]({{ "/case-studies/politics/political-dynamics/tribalism/" | relative_url }}), [deference]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#deference) to [authority]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#authority), and the psychological need for meaning and belonging, transforming them into *instruments of mass control and* [oppression]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#oppression). We will examine how these regimes systematically achieve this narrative lock-in, the mechanisms of their power, and the _catastrophic consequences_{: .warn} when a society fully divorces itself from truth.

### Background Information

While elements of authoritarian rule are ancient, modern Fascism and Totalitarianism are phenomena of the 20th century, emerging in specific historical contexts:

- _Rise of Mass Society & Industrialisation:_{: .bullet} The breakdown of traditional social structures, the alienation of industrial life, and the rise of [mass media]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#mass-media) created *conditions ripe for new forms of political mobilisation and control*.

- _World War I & Economic Crises:_{: .bullet} The trauma of World War I, followed by economic instability (e.g., the Great Depression), fueled widespread disillusionment with liberal [democracy]({{ "/case-studies/politics/democracy/" | relative_url }}) and [capitalism]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#capitalism), *creating a desire for radical solutions and strong leadership*.

- _Italy (Fascism):_{: .bullet} Benito Mussolini's Fascist movement (founded 1919, in power 1922-1943) was a reaction against both liberalism and communism. It emphasized [nationalism]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#nationalism), militarism, the subordination of the individual to the state, and a cult of personality around Mussolini (Il Duce).

- _Germany (Nazism):_{: .bullet} Adolf Hitler's National Socialism (Nazism, in power 1933-1945) shared many fascist characteristics but added a virulent strain of racial ideology (anti-Semitism and Aryan supremacy), *leading to genocide and expansionist war*.

- _Soviet Union (Stalinism):_{: .bullet} Joseph Stalin's regime (in power 1922-1953) represents a form of totalitarian communism. While ideologically distinct from fascism, it shared *structural similarities*: a *single party*, *secret police*, [propaganda]({{ "/guide/human-history/propaganda/" | relative_url }}), *cult of personality*, *state control over the economy*, and *mass purges*, all *justified* by an all-encompassing Marxist-Leninist narrative.

- _Other Examples:_{: .bullet} Totalitarian characteristics have also been observed in other regimes, such as Maoist China and Pol Pot's Cambodia.

These historical examples demonstrate the terrifying potential for ideologies (see Final Thought below) to utterly dominate the public and private sphere, with often catastrophic objective consequences.

### Step 1: Wipe the Slate Clean

Let’s strip away the specific historical actors and their atrocities for a moment, and analyse the underlying mechanics. We start with a [clean slate]({{ "/guide/clean-slate/" | relative_url }}) to understand the core claims and functional dynamics of these systems.

#### The Problem

Society is seen as fragmented, decadent, weak, or threatened by internal enemies and external forces. Traditional values are lost, and national destiny is unfulfilled.

#### The Stated Need

Absolute unity, order, strength, and a radical transformation are required to overcome these threats and achieve a glorious national or [ideological]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#ideology) destiny.

#### The Prevailing Narrative

A single, all-encompassing ideology, embodied by an infallible leader and a vanguard party, possesses the sole truth and the unique ability to guide society. Individual identity is subsumed into the collective (nation, race, class). All dissent is treason, and all institutions (media, [education]({{ "/case-studies/society/social-structures/education/" | relative_url }}), arts, family) must serve the state's ideological goals.

_This frames the "truth" as an instrument of state power, not an objective reality._{: .subtle}

This narrative claims that meaning, purpose, and salvation can only be found through _absolute submission_{: .bold} to the collective and its leadership.

### Step 2: Applying First Principles

#### Testability

The claims and performance of totalitarian regimes are, chillingly, highly testable, though the testing often comes at immense human cost:

- _Ideological Claims:_{: .bullet} The "truth" claims of totalitarian ideologies (e.g., racial superiority, historical determinism, the inevitable collapse of capitalism) can be objectively tested against scientific, economic, and historical evidence.

- _Societal Outcomes:_{: .bullet} We can objectively measure the "performance" of these regimes in terms of economic development, public health, _scientific innovation_{: .cool}, and human welfare, comparing them to their own stated goals and to other systems.

- _Suppression of Dissent:_{: .bullet} The efficacy of propaganda, censorship, and _state terror_{: .warn} in suppressing objective information and alternative narratives is empirically observable.

- _Psychological Impact:_{: .bullet} The impact of living under total state control on individual [psychology]({{ "/guide/human-history/psychology/" | relative_url }}), creativity, and trust can be studied through historical accounts, survivour testimonies, and comparative social science.

#### Objective vs. Intersubjective

- _Objective:_{: .bullet} The objective [facts]({{ "/guide/first-principles/correct-answers/" | relative_url }}) of _mass starvation_{: .warn}, economic collapse, scientific stagnation, or the systematic murder of millions are quantifiable and undeniable. The objective fact that an economy operating under central planning is less efficient than a market economy is measurable.

- _Intersubjective:_{: .bullet} The [belief]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#beliefs) in the infallible wisdom of the leader, the purity of the race, the historical inevitability of communism, or the absolute righteousness of the national cause are immensely powerful intersubjective narratives. These narratives are painstakingly constructed and enforced to create a complete, self-referential "reality" for the population, making _objective reality_{: .bold} irrelevant or dangerous.

---

#### Only EVIDENCE Matters

We must move beyond the terrifying propaganda, the cult of personality, and the claims of a higher historical or national purpose. We must look at the data, the body counts, the economic output, and the lived experiences.

- Do these regimes *deliver on their promises* of [prosperity]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#prosperity), unity, and a better future, or do they lead to widespread suffering and systemic failure?

- Does the *suppression of dissent* and objective truth ultimately strengthen or weaken a society's ability to adapt and survive?

- What are the objective consequences of allowing a single, all-encompassing narrative to *dictate all aspects of reality*?

### Step 3: Identify the Objective Consequences – The Ultimate Myth Lock-In

_Totalitarianism is the extreme case of a manufactured intersubjective narrative._{: .mark}

Fascism and totalitarianism are the extreme case of what happens when a manufactured intersubjective narrative becomes dominant enough to suppress and rewrite objective reality. This produces *predictable and catastrophic consequences*.

#### Truth becomes a tool of Power

In totalitarian systems, truth is not treated as something to discover, but *something to deploy*. Science, history, education, and journalism are *co-opted and bent to serve the ruling ideology*. Facts that contradict the party line are denied, suppressed, or labelled as enemy propaganda. Once institutions are forced to “agree” with the official story, the system loses its ability to notice errors and change course based on evidence.

#### Propaganda and Censorship replace Pluralism

Totalitarian regimes invest heavily in [indoctrination]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#brainwashing) and censorship. The goal is constant reinforcement of the official ideology while removing alternative viewpoints. When people cannot access competing accounts of reality—or cannot safely discuss them—the population becomes trapped inside a _manufactured world_{: .warn}.

#### Fear enforces outward Conformity

Secret police, surveillance, and punishment create a climate where disagreement becomes dangerous. People may privately doubt the official story, *but the cost of expressing that doubt becomes too high*. Over time, public life fills with _forced agreement_{: .bold}, and real critique disappears—making course correction even less likely.

#### The leader becomes sacred

The leader is *presented as infallible*: the embodiment of the nation, the movement, or history itself. This exploits a human tendency toward deference to authority and group loyalty, amplifying it to extremes. Once criticism of the leader is treated as heresy or treason, [rational]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#rational) debate becomes structurally *impossible*.

#### Failure is explained through Scapegoats, not self-correction

When policy fails—*economic stagnation, military setbacks,* [social dysfunction]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#dysfunction)—the system cannot admit ideological mistakes without weakening its legitimacy. Instead, it blames “traitors”, “saboteurs”, or [demonised]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#demonisation) out-groups, *often through purges*. This preserves the [myth]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#myth) at the cost of truth, and *it turns problems that could be corrected into spirals of paranoia and violence*.

#### Ideology suffocates innovation and learning

When economic policy is *dictated by* [doctrine]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#doctrine) *rather than feedback*, it tends toward inefficiency and often collapse. When science is subordinated to ideology (for example, the suppression of genuine [biology]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#biology) in favour of politically convenient claims, or the promotion of pseudo-science to justify [hierarchy]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#hierarchies)), societies lose competence, produce worse decisions, and can slide into ethical atrocities. 

_A culture that cannot openly test ideas cannot adapt._{: .mark}

The objective consequence is a society increasingly disconnected from reality: sacrificing knowledge, efficiency, [liberty]({{ "/case-studies/law/pragmatic/liberty/" | relative_url }}), and eventually the [well-being]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#wellbeing) of its population *to protect a single, all-encompassing myth*.

### Step 4: Reflect on Human History – The Great Mismatch

Totalitarianism is a profound and dangerous "[mismatch]({{ "/case-studies/mismatches/" | relative_url }})" between evolved human psychology (especially our tribal instincts and need for meaning) and the requirements for a stable, truth-seeking, and adaptive [civilisation]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#civilisations).

- _The Mismatch of Tribal Belonging vs. Totalitarian Assimilation:_{: .bullet} Humans have an evolved, deep need for belonging, identity, and group cohesion (as discussed in [Political Tribalism]({{ "/case-studies/politics/political-dynamics/tribalism/" | relative_url }})). Totalitarianism exploits this by offering a powerful, all-encompassing identity (national, racial, class) and a profound sense of belonging to a "chosen" collective. However, this is a dangerous mismatch because it demands absolute, unquestioning assimilation, stripping away individual [autonomy]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#autonomy) and dissent, *which are also vital human needs and essential for societal adaptation*.

- _The Mismatch of Search for Meaning vs. Imposed Dogma:_{: .bullet} Humans naturally *seek meaning and purpose*. Totalitarian ideologies provide this, offering grand narratives of *historical destiny*, *a clear vision of good and evil*, and *a purpose* that transcends individual life. This is a powerful appeal, but it's a mismatch because it replaces genuine, individual search for meaning with rigidly imposed [dogma]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#dogma), crushing intellectual freedom and [critical thought]({{ "/guide/thinking-for-yourself/" | relative_url }}), which are essential for navigating complex realities.

- _The Mismatch of Authority Deference vs. Critical Scrutiny:_{: .bullet} Humans have an evolved tendency to defer to authority, especially in times of crisis. Totalitarianism capitalises on this by creating a cult of personality around an _infallible leader_{: .bold}. This becomes a mismatch when critical scrutiny—*essential for preventing error and* [corruption]({{ "/case-studies/politics/bureaucratic-machine/corruption/" | relative_url }})—is actively suppressed. The [natural]({{ "/guide/human-history/natural-vs-normal/" | relative_url }}) tendency to trust authority is *perverted into blind obedience*.

- _The Mismatch of Truth-Seeking vs. Ideological Purity:_{: .bullet} Our evolved capacity for curiosity and problem-solving drives us to seek objective truth. Totalitarianism is a direct mismatch with this fundamental drive because it demands _ideological purity_{: .bold} above all else. Objective reality, when it contradicts the narrative, is sacrificed, leading to catastrophic errors in judgment (e.g., underestimating enemies, mismanaging resources) that eventually threaten the very survival of the regime and its people.

- _The Mismatch of Human Imperfection vs. Utopian Vision:_{: .bullet} Totalitarian ideologies often *promise a utopian future* (a "Thousand-Year Reich", a classless society). This mismatch with human imperfection and the inherent unpredictability of complex systems means that when reality inevitably fails to [conform]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#conformity) to the utopian vision, the regime resorts to violence, purges, and the creation of [scapegoats]({{ "/case-studies/mismatches/scapegoats/" | relative_url }}) to explain away its failures, *rather than adapting its flawed ideology*.

Totalitarianism, therefore, is the ultimate example of a societal system profoundly mismatched with core human drives for truth, _intellectual freedom_{: .cool}, and genuine adaptation. It exploits our evolved vulnerabilities to create *a collective delusion with devastating real-world consequences*.

_Totalitarianism is a collective delusion with devastating real-world consequences._{: .subtle}

## Conclusion: A Verdict on the Dangers of Unchecked Narrative

If we had to get it right, as if the freedom, well-being, and very [survival]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#survival) of humanity depended on it—because it does—what would our conclusion be?

Fascism and Totalitarianism are chilling demonstrations of the dangers inherent when a single, all-encompassing intersubjective **Myth** achieves complete **Lock-In**, effectively consuming and replacing objective reality. These systems, though promising unity and purpose, objectively lead to oppression, inefficiency, and _mass suffering_{: .warn} by systematically suppressing truth, individual liberty, and the adaptive capacity of society.

- _Truth is the First Victim:_{: .bullet} Totalitarianism requires the systematic destruction of objective truth and independent inquiry. When a society loses its ability to engage with facts, it *loses its ability to* _self-correct_{: .cool}.

- _Exploitation of Human Vulnerabilities:_{: .bullet} These regimes masterfully [exploit]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#exploitation) *evolved human psychological needs* for *belonging*, *meaning*, and *deference to authority*, twisting them into instruments of mass control and intellectual enslavement.

- _The Cost of Inflexibility:_{: .bullet} By locking society into a rigid, unquestionable narrative, totalitarianism makes itself catastrophically inflexible. It cannot adapt to new information, acknowledge errors, or respond effectively to challenges, ultimately leading to its own downfall and immense human cost.

A scientific worldview provides the essential antidote to the totalitarian impulse:

- _Unwavering Commitment to Evidence:_{: .bullet} *A constant, rigorous demand for* _objective evidence_{: .bold}*, even when it challenges comforting narratives or established power*.

- _Defence of Intellectual Freedom:_{: .bullet} The *robust protection* of *free inquiry*, *critical thinking*, and the *open exchange of diverse ideas* as *the only reliable path to truth and adaptation*.

- _Skepticism Towards Absolute Narratives:_{: .bullet} A *healthy skepticism* towards any ideology that claims to possess ultimate truth or demands absolute conformity, _recognizing that complexity requires nuance and continuous learning._{: .cool}

- _Understanding Human Nature:_{: .bullet} A clear-eyed understanding of both the cooperative and potentially destructive aspects of human nature, allowing for the design of systems that channel the former and constrain the latter, rather than relying on *idealized assumptions* or *exploiting our vulnerabilities*.

The lesson of Fascism and Totalitarianism is stark: societies that abandon the pursuit of objective truth and allow a single, all-consuming narrative to become locked-in are condemned to a reality of oppression, delusion, and eventual catastrophe.

The very capacity for [human flourishing]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#human-flourishing) depends on our vigilance in upholding the scientific worldview against such destructive forces.

### Final Thought: A Warning About Our Stories

Human beings are not naturally “evidence-first” creatures. We are [natural]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#natural)-born storytellers. For most of our [evolutionary]({{ "/guide/human-history/" | relative_url }}) history, shared narratives helped small [groups]({{ "/case-studies/society/social-structures/the-clan/" | relative_url }}) coordinate, transmit values, and create belonging. But in the post [Agricultural Transition]({{ "/guide/human-history/agricultural-transition/" | relative_url }}) world, our storytelling powers operate inside mass societies, [mass media]({{ "/case-studies/locked-in/mass-media/" | relative_url }}), and now algorithmic amplification. **That changes the stakes completely.**

Fascism and totalitarianism are the extreme case: a single story becomes [sacred]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#sacred), total, and enforced—until it consumes reality itself. But the deeper warning is that *the same psychological machinery does not vanish in liberal democracies*. The difference is often *degree*, not *kind*. Even without secret police and one-party rule, societies can still be governed by stories that “feel true” while sidestepping evidence, trade-offs, and measurable outcomes.

This is why the *“lessons of history”* are so often not learned. The lesson *is itself* a story—and stories compete on emotion, identity, and simplicity, not on accuracy. A population can sincerely believe it has learned the lesson, while quietly repeating the same pattern with a new cast of villains, a new [moral panic]({{ "/case-studies/politics/dysfunction/moral-panics/" | relative_url }}), and *a new righteous certainty*.

Here are a few common ways destructive narrative dynamics show up in modern democracies (without requiring a totalitarian regime):

- _Moral panics that demand immediate action:_{: .bullet} A shocking event produces a simple villain story, and policy follows urgency rather than evidence. *The pressure is to "do something", not to do what works*.

- _Symbolic laws that optimise for signalling:_{: .bullet} Legislation that looks strong in headlines but has weak real-world impact (or causes predictable side effects). *The goal becomes narrative victory, not measurable improvement.*

- _Scapegoat stories for complex problems:_{: .bullet} Social and economic failures get explained by a single [out-group]({{ "/case-studies/us-vs-them/" | relative_url }})—immigrants, elites, “the media”, “the left”, “the right”—*instead of system dynamics, incentives, and institutional design*.

- _Conspiracy narratives as psychological relief:_{: .bullet} When reality is frightening or confusing, a conspiracy story *can feel empowering*: it turns chaos into intention and uncertainty into certainty.

- _Policy debates framed as myth vs. myth:_{: .bullet} Two emotionally satisfying stories collide, each protected from falsification. Evidence becomes a weapon rather than a constraint.

- _Identity-first reasoning:_{: .bullet} Beliefs become badges of belonging. When views signal loyalty, [changing your mind]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#changing-your-mind) becomes _social betrayal rather than intellectual growth._{: .cool}

- _Algorithmic amplification of outrage:_{: .bullet} Platforms reward attention, not truth. The system selects for anger, [fear]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#fear), and certainty—the exact emotional ingredients that make bad stories contagious.

In short: totalitarianism is what happens when narrative power becomes absolute and uncorrectable. But _narrative drift_{: .bold}—where compelling stories steadily outrun evidence—can happen anywhere humans form tribes, build institutions, and try to govern large, complex societies.

_The antidote is not to abandon stories, but to subordinate them._{: .mark}

A scientific worldview demands that narratives remain *accountable* to reality:

- _Treat stories as hypotheses:_{: .bullet} *emotionally persuasive, but always* [testable]({{ "/guide/first-principles/testability/" | relative_url }}) *against outcomes*.
- _Build institutions that reward correction:_{: .bullet} *transparency, independent inquiry, free criticism, and clear metrics*.
- _Design for human vulnerability:_{: .bullet} *assume we will myth-make,* [scapegoat]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#scapegoats)*, and moralise—and build guardrails accordingly*.
- _Prefer slow, evidence-based governance over fast, story-driven reaction:_{: .bullet} *especially when the stakes are high*.

The 20th century showed what happens when a civilisation hands the steering wheel to a single sacred story. The 21st century **adds a new danger**: *countless micro-stories*, amplified at machine speed, each *pulling at our instincts*, each competing for attention, and each capable of **pushing policy away from evidence**.

> If the scientific worldview has a civic purpose, it is this: *to keep our stories powerful enough to inspire, but never powerful enough to replace reality.*

_Objective reality must always constrain the stories we tell ourselves._{: .mark}
