### Overview

Is _abortion_{: .neon} *right or wrong*?

That depends on how you _frame the question_{: .bold}.

- If you're asking _objectively_{: .cool}, then only evidence matters.
- If you're asking _subjectively_{: .bold}, you're speaking from personal feelings.
- If you're asking _intersubjectively_{: .bold}, you're operating within a cultural [belief system]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#beliefs).

Most public debates on abortion conflate all three. [Religious]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#religion) arguments appeal to faith. Political arguments invoke [rights]({{ "/case-studies/politics/governance/rights/" | relative_url }}). But *neither* faith nor rights can be objectively [tested]({{ "/guide/first-principles/testability/" | relative_url }}).

![Abortion]({{ "/assets/images/casestudies/law/pro-abortion.webp" | relative_url }})

To think clearly about abortion—or any _polarising issue_{: .warn}—we must start with a [clean slate]({{ "/guide/clean-slate/" | relative_url }}). Discard all untestable claims. Ask only: what are the **measurable consequences**? What does the _reliable evidence_{: .cool} say?

### Historical Perspective / Background Information

Abortion is often treated as a modern “culture war” issue, but it has been a recurring feature of [human societies]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#anthropology) for thousands of years—*and law has rarely handled it consistently*.

Across the last ~5,000 years, a few broad patterns keep repeating:

- _Abortion existed long before modern medicine:_{: .bullet} Abortion existed long before modern medicine, but it was far riskier without safe surgical methods, antibiotics, and [regulated]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#regulation) drugs. That matters because “_banning abortion_{: .bold}” historically tends to mean _pushing it underground_{: .warn}, not making it vanish.

- _Many legal systems historically drew lines around timing:_{: .bullet} Many legal systems historically drew lines around timing, not a simple “always legal” or “always murder” rule. In parts of Europe and the English common-law tradition, a key boundary was often *“quickening”* (when fetal movement is felt). In other words, even historically, the moral and legal status was frequently treated as *gradual and disputed*, not absolute.

- _A major shift happened in the 19th century:_{: .bullet} A major shift happened in the 19th century: many countries criminalised abortion more broadly, often alongside professionalisation of medicine and _state control_{: .bold} of reproduction. This is one reason the modern “illegal vs legal” framework feels older than it really is.

- _Another major shift happened in the 20th century:_{: .bullet} Another major shift happened in the 20th century: many jurisdictions liberalised abortion laws (to reduce deaths and injuries from unsafe procedures, and to reflect changing [norms]({{ "/case-studies/society/sociology/norms/" | relative_url }}) around women’s [autonomy]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#autonomy)). Some later tightened restrictions again, showing that abortion law tends to swing with politics and institutions, not [evidence]({{ "/guide/first-principles/evidence/" | relative_url }}) as it should.

_When abortion is restricted, it doesn’t reliably end abortion—it changes the safety, the inequality, and the consequences._{: .mark}

### Step 1: Filter Out Untestable Claims

> “God says abortion is murder.”

> “Every woman has a right to choose.”

Both are [intersubjective]({{ "/guide/first-principles/intersubjective/" | relative_url }}) claims. They reflect [group consensus]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#consensus), not evidence. _Beliefs are not enough when crafting policies that affect millions._{: .mark}

In this [worldview]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#scientific-worldview), only testable claims count. So we ask: **What happens in the real world when abortion is legal—or banned?**

---

### Step 2: Identify Objective Outcomes

Forget philosophy for a moment. Focus on consequences:

- _Public health:_{: .bullet} Does access to abortion affect _public health_{: .cool}?
- _Socioeconomic impact:_{: .bullet} Does it reduce [poverty]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#poverty), crime, or suffering?
- _Social metrics:_{: .bullet} Does restricting abortion improve social [wellbeing]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#wellbeing)?

These are [empirical questions]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#empirical). They have answers—if we care enough to look.

---

### Step 3: Review the Evidence

One famous example comes from economists **John Donohue** and **Steven Levitt**. They studied _crime rates_{: .bold} in the US following the 1973 Roe v. Wade decision.

_Their controversial conclusion?_{: .subtle}

> _Legalised abortion_{: .cool} led to _unwanted children_{: .warn}, and therefore **lower crime rates** 15–20 years later.

It’s not just about crime. A broad body of evidence shows:

- _Fewer maternal deaths:_{: .bullet} _Fewer maternal deaths_{: .warn} where abortion is legal  
- _Fewer unwanted births:_{: .bullet} _Fewer unwanted births_{: .warn}  
- _Better life outcomes:_{: .bullet} Better life outcomes for both women and children  
- _Greater gender equality and economic opportunity:_{: .bullet} Greater gender equality and economic opportunity

---

### Step 4: Consider Counterarguments

Science thrives on scepticism. So we must ask:

- _Alternative drivers:_{: .bullet} Could falling crime rates have other causes? (e.g. policing, economics, drug policy)
- _Psychological tracking:_{: .bullet} Is there emotional harm to women post-abortion?
- _Neurological metrics:_{: .bullet} What about potential _fetal pain_{: .warn}?

These concerns are legitimate. But they too must be tested with evidence, not assumed on moral grounds.

---

### Step 5: Add Historical Perspective

It’s easy to think abortion is a modern invention. But **our [evolutionary history]({{ "/guide/human-history/" | relative_url }}) tells another story**.

Hunter-gatherer societies had extensive _botanical knowledge_{: .bold}. Plants like pennyroyal and tansy were used to induce miscarriage. Physical methods were also known—risky, yes, but evidence suggests they were practiced.

In societies where [survival]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#survival) was tough and mobility essential, _reproductive control_{: .neon} was a necessity. It's *likely* that abortion, in some form, has always existed.

---

### Final Appraisal: With My Life on the Line

If I had to bet my life on what leads to _better real-world outcomes_{: .cool}:

_Access to legal, safe abortion should be available._{: .mark}

Why?

- It saves lives  
- It reduces _suffering_{: .warn}  
- It enhances freedom  
- It can reduce crime  
- It aligns with our understanding of human history  
- And most importantly, **it is supported by reliable evidence**

### A Note on Moral Complexity

This isn’t about *dismissing* emotions or ethics. It’s about building a _shared, testable foundation_{: .bold} for *evaluating policies*.

Moral discomfort? That’s human. But discomfort doesn’t invalidate *[facts]({{ "/guide/first-principles/correct-answers/" | relative_url }})*.

A _scientific worldview_{: .cool} doesn’t tell you what to feel. It tells you **how to think clearly**—even when the subject is difficult.

_True public health policy relies on objective tracking of harm, not subjective moral judgment._{: .mark}
