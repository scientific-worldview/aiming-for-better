### Overview: Behaviour-Control Lock-In

For most of post [Agricultural Transition]({{ "/guide/human-history/agricultural-transition/" | relative_url }}) [history]({{ "/guide/human-history/" | relative_url }}) (and especially after the rise of [civilisations]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#civilisations)), the [power]({{ "/case-studies/politics/governance/power/" | relative_url }}) of the state was written on the body of the criminal through *public torture and execution*. Punishment was a brutal, physical spectacle. But as the philosopher Michel Foucault (**Discipline and Punish**) famously documented, a profound shift occurred around the 18th and 19th centuries. _The target of punishment moved from the body to the "soul"_{: .mark}.

The goal was no longer simply to contain or destroy the offender, but to *reform* and *discipline* them—to create a new, more obedient and productive kind of citizen. This was the birth of the modern prison and the _ambition of rehabilitation_{: .bold}.

This ambition has taken many forms, from [religious]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#religion) salvation to *psychological reprogramming*. The latest and most dominant iteration is the **[Offender Treatment Industry]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#offender-treatment-industry)**. Grounded in psychological and cognitive-behavioural hypotheses, this movement promises to fix the _"faulty thinking"_{: .bold} of criminals through a series of structured courses, "offender behaviour programmes" (**OBPs**).

![Pseudoscience]({{ "/assets/images/casestudies/ps/OBPgenerator.webp" | relative_url }})

This case study will argue that this modern, so-called "scientific" approach to _rehabilitation_{: .bold}, for all its good intentions, has morphed into a classic [bureaucratic]({{ "/case-studies/politics/bureaucratic-machine/" | relative_url }}) and pseudoscientific [Lock-In]({{ "/case-studies/locked-in/" | relative_url }}). It has created a massive, **self-perpetuating industry** of *programme designers*, *facilitators*, *psychologists*, and *probation officers* whose own existence is now tied to a specific, scientifically flawed, model of rehabilitation. When the [evidence]({{ "/guide/first-principles/evidence/" | relative_url }}) shows these programmes fail—*or even make things worse*—the system doesn't shut down. It simply _rebrands_{: .warn}, creating a new, slightly different programme and continuing the cycle, locking [society]({{ "/case-studies/society/social-structures/" | relative_url }}) out of *more effective, evidence-based solutions*.

### A Brief History of "Reforming the Soul"

The idea of "fixing" the criminal is not a new one. The methods have simply evolved alongside our dominant [intersubjective]({{ "/guide/first-principles/intersubjective/" | relative_url }}) [narratives]({{ "/case-studies/narratives/" | relative_url }}).

- _The Religious Model (18th-19th Centuries):_{: .bullet} The first great wave of prison reform, driven by religious groups like the Quakers, was based on a [narrative]({{ "/case-studies/narratives/" | relative_url }}) of [sin]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#sin) and salvation_{: .bold}. The first "penitentiaries" (from the word "penitence") were designed for solitary confinement, silence, and Bible study. The goal was to force the _sinner_{: .bold} to confront their soul and achieve moral and religious _redemption_{: .cool}.

- _The Disciplinary Model (19th Century):_{: .bullet} The rise of the factory and the army created a new ideal: the disciplined, obedient individual. The prison was redesigned as a machine for _instilling discipline_{: .bold} through *rigid timetables*, *constant surveillance* (like Jeremy Bentham's "Panopticon"), and *repetitive labour*. The goal was to remold the _chaotic individual_{: .bold} into a compliant, productive _citizen_{: .cool}.

- _The Medical/Therapeutic Model (Early-Mid 20th Century):_{: .bullet} With the rise of _psychiatry_{: .bold} and [psychology]({{ "/guide/human-history/psychology/" | relative_url }}), *the narrative shifted again*. Criminality was now seen as a sickness or pathology located within the individual's mind. This led to the [medicalisation]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#medicalisation) of [deviance]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#deviant) and the use of treatments ranging from *psychoanalysis* to, in its most extreme forms, *lobotomies*.

- _The Cognitive-Behavioural Model (Late 20th Century - Present):_{: .bullet} This is the model that birthed the modern Offender Treatment Industry. The narrative became that *crime is a result of "faulty thinking"*, "cognitive deficits"/"cognitive distortions" or "maladaptive schemas". The solution was a [standardized]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#standardisation), so-called "scientific" course, like [a _Cognitive-Behavioural Therapy_{: .bold}]({{ "/guide/human-history/therapy-culture/" | relative_url }}) (CBT) programme, to "correct" these thinking errors. This is the model that led to the proliferation of OBPs in countries like Canada, the UK, and the US.

#### The Cognitive-Behavioural Model

- _Canada (The 1980s):_{: .bullet} Canadian psychologists were at the forefront, developing early CBT-based programmes that aimed to teach offenders "pro-social" thinking skills. These models were *heavily influential* and *exported worldwide*.

- _The UK (The 1990s - "What Works"):_{: .bullet} The UK government embraced this model with enthusiasm, launching the "What Works" initiative. This led to the rollout of a suite of accredited OBPs across the prison and probation service, such as the Sex Offender Treatment Programme (SOTP) and Enhanced Thinking Skills (ETS). The idea was to apply a standardized, so-called "scientific" treatment model across the entire system. (The irony, of course, is that they didn't work...unless you mean jobs and status.)

- _The USA:_{: .bullet} While the US was focused on a more _punitive_{: .warn} "mass incarceration" model, similar therapeutic and treatment-based programmes became a standard feature within its prisons and parole systems, often *mandated as a condition of release*.

The *narrative has changed*—from sinner to undisciplined body to sick patient to faulty thinker—but *the underlying ambition has remained the same*: to reshape the inner world of the offender according to the *dominant* _ideology_{: .warn} of the day. Today, this dominant ideology says crime is a result of individual cognitive shortcomings, and the solution is a professionally administered psychological course.

### Step 1: Wipe the Slate Clean

Let's set aside the therapeutic jargon and the official reports. We start with a [clean slate]({{ "/guide/clean-slate/" | relative_url }}).

- _The Problem:_{: .bullet} People released from prison often commit more crimes (*recidivism*).

- _The Stated Need:_{: .bullet} A system is needed to *reduce the likelihood* that they will re-offend.

- _The Prevailing Narrative:_{: .bullet} The problem lies in the offender's "criminal thinking", "lack of [empathy]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#empathy), or "poor impulse control". The solution is a psychological programme to fix these internal deficits.

---

### Step 2: Applying First Principles

#### Testability

Yes, the effectiveness of these programmes is [highly testable]({{ "/guide/first-principles/testability/" | relative_url }}). We can—*and must*!—conduct rigourous, randomized controlled trials to compare the re-offending rates of offenders who complete a programme with a matched group who do not.

#### Objective vs. Intersubjective

- _Objective:_{: .bullet} The re-offending rate of a group of ex-prisoners over a five-year period is a hard, measurable, objective fact.

- _Intersubjective:_{: .bullet} The [belief]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#beliefs) that a 24-session course on "thinking skills" is the most effective way to stop a lifetime of crime is an intersubjective narrative, promoted by the professionals who design and deliver it. Just as the [eugenics movement]({{ "/case-studies/pseudoscience/eugenics/" | relative_url }}) took a _social problem_{: .bold} and gave it a convenient (and false) *biological explanation*, the Offender Treatment Industry takes a _social problem_{: .bold} and tries to dress it up as a problem of *individual psychology*.

#### Only EVIDENCE Matters

We must ignore the professional credentials and the virtuous-sounding programme names. We must look at the data.

_Do these programmes actually work?_{: .mark}

### Step 3: Identify the Consequences - The Lock-In Mechanism in Action

The Offender Treatment Industry has created a powerful, self-perpetuating bureaucratic loop that is incredibly _resistant_{: .warn} to *evidence of its own failure*.

- _The Professional Class:_{: .bullet} A vast _industry_{: .warn} of forensic psychologists, programme designers, facilitators, and probation officers is employed to design, deliver, and manage these courses. Their careers, expertise, and professional identity are entirely invested in this specific, psychological model of rehabilitation.

- _The Bureaucratic Inertia:_{: .bullet} Once a programme is *"accredited"* and rolled out across an entire national prison system, it develops enormous [_institutional inertia_{: .bold}]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#inertia). Training manuals are printed, staff are hired, and budgets are allocated. Dismantling this machinery is far more difficult than creating it.

- _The "Illusion of Action":_{: .bullet} For Courts, politicians and prison governors, these programmes are a *perfect solution*. They allow them to claim they are _"doing something"_{: .warn}, namely "rehabilitation", regardless of whether the programmes actually work. It creates a positive headline and *a tick in a box*. (For more on the "illusion of action" and "doing something", see [Modern Rituals]({{ "/case-studies/politics/dysfunction/performance/" | relative_url }}))

- _The "Moving the Goalposts" Defence:_{: .bullet} When a programme is shown to fail, the system rarely admits the underlying model is flawed. Instead, it blames the implementation ("it wasn't delivered correctly") or, more often, simply scraps the old brand name and rolls out a "new and improved" version that is often *remarkably similar to the old one*.

#### The Evidence of Failure: The SOTP and its Ghosts

The UK's Sex Offender Treatment Programme (SOTP) is the most damning piece of evidence.

- After running for _over two decades_{: .warn}, a major Ministry of Justice evaluation found that its participants had *a higher rate* of both general and specific sexual re-offending than a matched group of untreated offenders.

- _The Lock-In in Action:_{: .bullet} Was the entire model of psychological group therapy for sex offenders abandoned? No. The SOTP was scrapped, but it was replaced by a new suite of programmes, like "Kaizen" and "Horizon", based on similar (though updated) psychological principles. *The industry survived by* _rebranding_{: .warn}.

This pattern is repeated across the board. Programmes like *Enhanced Thinking Skills (ETS)* were quietly replaced by the very similar *Thinking Skills Programme (TSP)*. Programmes like *RESOLVE* and *Building Better Relationships (BBR)* were rolled out to thousands of prisoners for years without any robust evidence of their effectiveness.

### Step 4: Reflect on Human History - The Great Mismatch

The entire Offender Treatment Industry is built on a foundational [mismatch]({{ "/case-studies/mismatches/" | relative_url }}). It is a system designed by and for the realities of a complex, anonymous, civilized state, and it is almost completely _blind_{: .warn} to the _evolutionary psychology_{: .bold} of the human beings it is supposed to be *"treating"*.

#### The Mismatch of Cause (Faulty Thinker vs. Mismatched Animal)

The industry's core narrative is that crime is primarily the result of individual cognitive deficits—"faulty thinking", "criminal attitudes", "maladptive schemas", "lack of empathy", etc.

- _The Evolutionary Reality:_{: .bullet} A _scientific worldview_{: .neon} suggests that for many, crime is not a sign of a "broken [brain]({{ "/case-studies/who-we-are/brain/" | relative_url }})", but a predictable, often [rational]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#rational) (from a [survival]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#survival) perspective) response to a mismatched and deprived environment. Our brains [evolved]({{ "/guide/human-history/evolution/" | relative_url }}) for a world of [high status]({{ "/case-studies/society/sociology/status/" | relative_url }}), clear purpose, and *strong community bonds*. When a modern environment _denies_{: .warn} individuals these _fundamental needs_{: .bold}—offering only [poverty]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#poverty), *social exclusion*, and a *lack of legitimate pathways to success*—our ancient programming can drive us toward alternative, anti-social strategies to gain resources, status and procreation. The Offender Treatment Industry is designed to "fix" the individual, systematically ignoring the _environmental mismatch_{: .bold} that is the _root cause_{: .mark} of much [rule breaking]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#rules) and many "broken" brains.

#### The Mismatch of Method (Abstract Therapy vs. Tangible Needs)

The industry's solution is abstract, psychological intervention—group therapy, worksheets, and cognitive exercises.

- _The Evolutionary Reality:_{: .bullet} Our brains are pragmatic _survival machines_{: .bold}. They respond to tangible realities, not *abstract theories*. The most powerful drivers of behavioural change are not CBT worksheets, but real-world changes in one's environment and opportunities. The hunter-gatherer who stopped being a freeloader did so because the tangible consequence was *social ostracism* and the tangible reward for cooperating was *social inclusion*. A rational, evolutionarily-informed "rehabilitation" programme would focus less on "fixing thoughts" and more on providing the tangible building blocks of a pro-social life: a stable job, a safe place to live, and *a supportive community*. Instead, rule-breakers, who are often isolated to begin with, often grew up in [The Care System]({{ "/case-studies/society/social-structures/the-care-system/" | relative_url }}), are further _marginalised_{: .warn} by the *'holier-than-thou'* forces of society, in particular sensationalist _tabloid_{: .warn} media.

#### The Mismatch of Justice (Punishment vs. Restoration)

As explored previously, our _evolved sense of [justice]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#justice-and-injustice) is deeply *social and restorative*.

_Its goal is to repair the tear in the social fabric._{: .subtle}

- _The Civilized Mismatch:_{: .bullet} The Offender Treatment Industry operates as an adjunct to a punitive, carceral system. It is a layer of *pseudoscientific gloss* applied to _the fundamentally unnatural act of caging a social primate in isolation_{: .mark}. The programmes are often delivered in a [coercive]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#coercion), artificial environment* that is completely _divorced_{: .warn} from the real-world _community_{: .bold} where the harm occurred and where the offender must eventually learn to live.

### Hoodwinking Society

The Offender Treatment Industry is a classic example of a "civilized" solution that is profoundly _disconnected from our nature_{: .bold}. It _medicalises_{: .warn} a [sociological problem]({{ "/case-studies/society/sociology/" | relative_url }}), _pathologises_{: .warn} a predictable response to a *mismatched environment*, and creates *a vast, self-perpetuating bureaucracy* to deliver "cures" that the evidence shows don't work. It is a system that is perfectly designed to ignore the simple, evolutionary truth: _if you want to change a person's_{: .mark} [behaviour]({{ "/guide/human-history/psychology/" | relative_url }}), _you must first change their world_{: .mark}.

## Conclusion: A Verdict in Favour of Reality over Ritual

The story of the Offender Treatment Industry is a clear and tragic example of a bureaucratic and pseudoscientific _Lock-In_{: .neon}. It is a system that has become untethered from its stated goal of reducing crime and is now primarily focused *on its own self-perpetuation*.

- _The Narrative Defines the Solution:_{: .bullet} By [framing]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#framing) crime as a problem of _"faulty thinking"_{: .bold}, the system creates a *permanent demand* for "thinking skills" courses.

- _The Industry Becomes the Expert:_{: .bullet} The professional class that runs these programmes becomes the sole, trusted [authority]({{ "/glossary/" | relative_url }}#authority) on rehabilitation, making it difficult for outside, _evidence-based critiques_{: .bold} to gain traction.

- _Failure Leads to Rebranding, Not Rejection:_{: .bullet} The system's response to failure is not to question its fundamental assumptions, but to offer a new, cosmetically different product. It is a _ritual_{: .warn} of "[doing something]({{ "/case-studies/politics/dysfunction/performance/" | relative_url }})" that shields the system from the need to achieve *actual results*.

A truly _scientific approach_{: .neon}, grounded in the _Mismatch Hypothesis_{: .bold}, would suggest a completely different approach. It would argue that for many, crime is not a result of a cognitive deficit, but often a rational (if socially unacceptable) response to a mismatched and deprived environment, especially when growing up.

The rational path forward is to have the *courage to challenge* this _lock-in_{: .neon}:

- _Demand Rigorous, Independent Testing:_{: .bullet} No programme should be rolled out without a randomized controlled trial *proving its effectiveness*.

- _Shift Resources from "Cure" to "Prevention":_{: .bullet} We must divert the vast sums of money being wasted on ineffective psychological programmes and invest it in things *the evidence proves reduce crime*: ensuring _every child is loved_{: .bold}, [group living and community spirit]({{ "/case-studies/society/social-structures/community-and-anonymity/" | relative_url }}), real-world [education]({{ "/case-studies/society/social-structures/education/" | relative_url }}), vocational job training in prison, secure housing upon release, addiction treatment and [drug decriminalisation]({{ "/case-studies/law/pragmatic/drug-laws/" | relative_url }}).

- _Focus on Sociology, Not Just Psychology:_{: .bullet} We must recognise that the most powerful rehabilitative tool is not a CBT worksheet; _it is a stable job, a safe place to live, and a supportive community_{: .mark}.

_The current system offers a comforting but false narrative of a psychological "cure"._{: .subtle}

A *rational society* must have the courage to abandon this *expensive and ineffective* [modern ritual]({{ "/case-studies/politics/dysfunction/performance/" | relative_url }}) and invest in the practical, evidence-based, and systemic solutions that actually work.
